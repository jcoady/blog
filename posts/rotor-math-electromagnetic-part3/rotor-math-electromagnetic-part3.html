Where Part 2 left off

Part 1 built the anchor/field-strength dictionary and an exact Cayley U(1) lattice. Part 2 showed that the identity of Lecture 13 [8] (video, handwritten notes) \(F_{\mu\nu}=-\tfrac ie[D_\mu,D_\nu]\) holds exactly on that lattice in the form \([\mathcal D_\mu,\mathcal D_\nu]\psi=(U_P-1)\,\mathcal T_{\mu\nu}\psi\), and that charge \(k\) is the map \(a\mapsto\tan(k\arctan a)\). Conventions are as in Part 2: \(\mathcal D_\mu\psi(x)=U_\mu(x)\psi(x+\hat\mu)-\psi(x)\), \(U=(1-ia)/(1+ia)\), \(\psi\to g\psi\), \(U_\mu(x)\to g(x)U_\mu(x)g(x+\hat\mu)^{-1}\).

This post does two things from the lecture’s list that remained: the gauge-invariance obstruction to a bare photon mass term (Calculation 13) and a matter field (Calculation 14). It starts with three housekeeping results that affect the earlier posts and the paper.

Proofs and checks. The identities in this post are proved by the short derivations in the text. The tables labelled exact are computational verification in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q\) and over \(\mathbb F_p\) (a separate field class per prime); they check the derivations and the scripts and are not themselves proofs. Floating-point results (the continuum limit in Calculation 13(c), the Brillouin-zone singular values in Calculation 14, and the Part 1 re-runs) are marked as numerical. The scripts are at the end.

Proposition 6.3(3) of the paper

The paper states that the vector Cayley anchor \((\Lambda-I)(\Lambda+I)^{-1}\) is related to the rotor anchor by the doubling map \(\zeta\mapsto2\zeta/(1+\zeta\cdot\zeta)\). As worded, this is not correct. Let \(\zeta_s\) be a spin Cayley parameter, \(\sigma=\zeta_s\cdot\zeta_s\), \(G=U_\text{spin}(\zeta_s)\) and \(\Lambda\) its Lorentz matrix. Then, in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q(i)\):

  1. The rotor anchor of \(\Lambda\) is \(\zeta=2\zeta_s/(1+\sigma)\). This is the paper’s Theorem 7.1, and it checks.
  2. The vector Cayley anchor is \[ \zeta_C=\frac{2\,\zeta_s\,(1-\bar\sigma)}{1-\sigma\bar\sigma},\qquad \bar\sigma=\text{Galois conjugate of }\sigma . \]
  3. \(\zeta_C=\zeta\) (vector Cayley anchor equals rotor anchor) if and only if \(\sigma\in F\), i.e. \(\kappa\cdot\rho=0\) for \(\zeta_s\). In that case \(\zeta_C=2\zeta_s/(1+\sigma)\).
  4. Applying the doubling map to the rotor anchor never gives \(\zeta_C\) in any tested case, including a pure boost (\(\zeta=1/2\) has \(\zeta_C=1/2\), not \(4/5\)).

So the doubling map relates the spin parameter to the rotor anchor (item 1); it relates the spin parameter to the vector Cayley anchor only when \(\sigma\) is real (item 3).

Domain. Item 1 needs \(\sigma\neq1\) (the cap \(U_\text{spin}\) exists) and \(\sigma\neq-1\) (so that \(x\neq0\) and the anchor is defined). Item 2 needs \(1-\sigma\bar\sigma\neq0\) and \(\Lambda+I\) invertible (the vector Cayley transform exists); item 3 additionally needs \(\zeta_s\neq0\). These restrictions should be stated in the corrected proposition of the paper, which should replace item (3) of Proposition 6.3 by items 1–3 above.

Why. A real Lorentz transformation acts on anchors by a complex rotation, and the Cayley transform commutes with conjugation, so one may take \(\zeta_s=t\hat z\) with \(t=\alpha+i\beta\), \(t^2=\sigma\). With \(\tau_L X=(\zeta_s\!\cdot\!\sigma)X\) and \(\tau_R X=X(\bar\zeta_s\!\cdot\!\sigma)\) one has \(\Lambda=\exp(L+R)\) and the Cayley transform is \((\tau_L+\tau_R)/(1+\tau_L\tau_R)\). On the diagonal entries of \(X\) this is \(2\alpha/(1+|t|^2)\) and on the off-diagonal entries \(2i\beta/(1-|t|^2)\), i.e. a boost parameter and a rotation parameter with different denominators. Rewriting \(\bigl(\tfrac{2\alpha}{1+|t|^2}+i\tfrac{2\beta}{1-|t|^2}\bigr)/t=2(1-\bar t^{\,2})/(1-|t|^4)\) gives item 2. If \(\sigma\) is real this collapses to \(2/(1+\sigma)\).

Calculation 13: the obstruction to a bare mass term

The lecture’s argument: a Proca term \(\propto m_A^2A_\mu A^\mu\) changes under \(A\to A-\partial\phi\) by \(-2A\cdot\partial\phi+(\partial\phi)^2\), so \(m_A=0\). Here is the lattice version. What is proved below is narrower than “the photon is massless”: in the pure abelian lattice gauge theory, no non-constant gauge-invariant function of a single link exists, so a bare Proca-type mass term built from the links alone is impossible. Models with extra fields (Stückelberg, Higgs) are outside this argument.

(a) Gauge transformations in Cayley form

With \(g(x)=\frac{1-i\gamma}{1+i\gamma}\) and \(g(x+\hat\mu)\) of parameter \(\gamma'\), the transformed link \(U'=gUg'^{-1}\) has Cayley parameter

\[ a'=\gamma\oplus a\oplus(-\gamma'),\qquad a\oplus b=\frac{a+b}{1-ab}. \]

This matches the direct phase computation 1000/1000 over \(\mathbb Q\), 1000/1000 over \(\mathbb F_7\), 1000/1000 over \(\mathbb F_{11}\) (with \(a=\infty\) included).

(b) The single-link no-go

The two gauge parameters \(\gamma(x)\) and \(\gamma(x+\hat\mu)\) are independent. Given any \(a\) and any target \(b\), take \(\gamma'=0\) and \(\gamma=b\ominus a\): then \(a'=b\). So the gauge orbit of a single link is the whole phase group, and a function of one link that is gauge invariant must be constant. A mass term, which is a sum of identical non-constant functions of single links, cannot be invariant.

(c) The continuum limit matches the lecture (numerical)

With \(U\approx e^{i\varepsilon A}\) (\(e=1\)) and \(\phi(x)\) smooth, the variation of the link term satisfies

\[ \frac{f(\theta')-f(\theta)}{\varepsilon^2/2}\;\longrightarrow\;-2A\,\partial\phi+(\partial\phi)^2 , \]

which is the lecture’s \((A-\partial\phi)^2-A^2\) in the limit. This is a floating-point check, not an exact identity: with \(A=0.7\) and \(\partial\phi=0.4\) (continuum value \(-0.4\)), the lattice value approaches it at first order:

(d) Gauge-invariant functions are functions of holonomies

On a connected lattice with \(V\) sites and \(E\) links, the gauge group \(G^V\) acts on \(G^E\) with kernel the constant transformations, so the number of orbits is \(|G|^{E-V+1}\), which equals the number of independent holonomies (plaquettes plus non-contractible loops). I checked this by brute force on the \(2\times2\) torus over \(\mathbb F_3[i]\) (\(|G|=N=4\), \(E=8\), \(V=4\)):

  • \(4^8=65536\) link configurations;
  • every orbit has size \(64=N^{V-1}\) (30 random configurations, all 256 gauge transformations each);
  • orbits: \(65536/64=1024=N^{E-V+1}\);
  • distinct holonomy data (the four plaquettes plus the two non-contractible loops): 1024.

So the holonomies separate the orbits: two configurations with the same holonomies are gauge equivalent, and every gauge-invariant function of the links is a function of holonomies. There is no local gauge-invariant function of the links other than those built from closed loops. For the abelian theory on a connected lattice, this is the lattice form of “gauge invariance leaves room only for \(F_{\mu\nu}\), not for a bare mass term.”

What this does and does not give

  • Gives: an exact lattice realization of the gauge-invariance obstruction to a bare Proca-type mass term (the single-link no-go, and orbit counting for the abelian case), and its continuum limit.
  • Does not give: a proof that a photon must be massless in every model. A Stückelberg or Higgs field can make a vector field massive gauge-invariantly, but that adds a field and is not tested here.
  • Abelian-specific in part: the orbit counting and the identification of invariants with holonomies were checked for an abelian group on a connected lattice. For non-abelian links the single-link argument goes through by the same one-line reasoning (\(U\mapsto gUh^{-1}\) with independent \(g,h\) reaches every group element; not computed here), but the orbit count and the holonomy classification are more complicated.

Calculation 14: a Wilson–Dirac matter field

Gamma matrices over \(K\)

The paper’s field \(K=F[i]\) contains the \(i\) that the Pauli matrix \(\sigma_2\) needs. Take the Euclidean chiral gamma matrices

\[ \gamma_k=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\sigma_k\\ i\sigma_k&0\end{pmatrix}\ (k=1,2,3),\qquad \gamma_4=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \gamma_5=\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3\gamma_4 , \]

all with entries in \(K\). Exactly, over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb F_7\) and \(\mathbb F_{11}\): \(\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\}=2\delta_{\mu\nu}\) (16/16), every \(\gamma_\mu\) and \(\gamma_5\) is Hermitian under conjugate-transpose (the paper’s \(\dagger\)), \(\gamma_5^2=1\) and \(\gamma_5\) anticommutes with each \(\gamma_\mu\). The six spin generators \([\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu]/4\) are block diagonal with trace-free \(2\times2\) blocks (6/6), so they lie in \(\mathfrak{sl}(2,K)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,K)\). The two blocks are the two chiralities, which is the \(M_2(K)\) structure of the paper in its Euclidean form. This is a Euclidean hypercubic construction; the Minkowski version through the paper’s Pauli identification of Hermitian matrices is not attempted here.

The operator

\[ \bigl(D\psi\bigr)(x)=(m+4r)\,\psi(x)-\tfrac12\sum_{\mu}\Bigl[(r-\gamma_\mu)\,U_\mu(x)\,\psi(x+\hat\mu)+(r+\gamma_\mu)\,U_\mu(x-\hat\mu)^{-1}\psi(x-\hat\mu)\Bigr]. \]

\(r=0\) is the naive operator and \(r=1\) the Wilson operator. The factor \(\tfrac12\) uses \(\mathrm{char}(F)\neq2\), one of the paper’s standing hypotheses. The fields \(\psi,\bar\psi\) are \(K^4\)-valued and treated as ordinary (commuting) fields; the identities below are identities of the operator.

Gauge covariance and the forced interaction

Under \(\psi\to g\psi\), \(\bar\psi\to\bar\psi g^{-1}\), \(U\to gUg'^{-1}\), each hopping term transforms by the factor \(g(x)\) because \(U'_\mu(x)\psi'(x+\hat\mu)=g(x)U_\mu(x)\psi(x+\hat\mu)\). The covariance is proved by the computation just given. Exact-arithmetic verification (a check of that derivation and of the script, not a proof) on the 4D torus \(L=3\) (81 sites, 324 links), random links including \(a=\infty\), random \(\psi,\bar\psi\in K^4\) at every site, and the same results for \(r=0\) and \(r=1\):

The current

Write the action as \(S=\sum_x\bar\psi(x)(D\psi)(x)\) and define the link current

\[ j_\mu(x)=\bar\psi(x)\,\tfrac{r-\gamma_\mu}2\,U_\mu(x)\psi(x+\hat\mu)-\bar\psi(x+\hat\mu)\,\tfrac{r+\gamma_\mu}2\,U_\mu(x)^{-1}\psi(x). \]

With \(U_\mu(x)=e^{i\theta_\mu(x)}\) one finds directly from \(S\) that

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)}=-\,i\,j_\mu(x), \]

so \(j_\mu\) is exactly what couples to the gauge field. For constant fields and \(U=1\) it is \(j_\mu=-\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi\) (4/4 directions, all three fields), so to first order \(S_\text{int}\approx i\,e\varepsilon\sum A_\mu\,\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi\), the lecture’s \(iq\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu A_\mu\psi\) term. This is the sense in which locality forces the interaction.

The current is gauge invariant (324/324 in all three fields) and satisfies the exact identity

\[ \sum_\mu\bigl[j_\mu(x)-j_\mu(x-\hat\mu)\bigr]=(\bar\psi D)(x)\,\psi(x)-\bar\psi(x)\,(D\psi)(x)\qquad(81/81\text{ in all three fields}), \]

where \((\bar\psi D)\) is the row action of \(D\). On shell (\(D\psi=0\), \(\bar\psi D=0\)) the right side vanishes, which is the lecture’s \(\partial_\nu J^\nu=0\) on the lattice.

\(\gamma_5\)-hermiticity and chiral symmetry

\(\langle\phi,\gamma_5D\gamma_5\psi\rangle=\langle D\phi,\psi\rangle\) holds exactly (3/3 random pairs per field, \(r=0\) and \(r=1\)), where \(\langle a,b\rangle=\sum\bar a\cdot b\) uses the Galois conjugate of \(K\). The naive operator at \(m=0\) has the exact chiral symmetry \(\gamma_5D\gamma_5=-D\) (81/81 sites in all three fields); the Wilson term breaks it (0/81). This is the standard trade-off between chiral symmetry and doublers.

Fermion doubling, exactly

On an \(L=4\) torus the character \(\omega=i\) is a primitive fourth root of unity in \(K\) for every \(F\), so plane waves exist exactly and no analysis is needed. With \(t_\mu=\tfrac12(\omega^{k_\mu}-\omega^{-k_\mu})\) and \(c_\mu=\tfrac12(\omega^{k_\mu}+\omega^{-k_\mu})\), the free operator on \(\psi=v\,\omega^{k\cdot x}\) is \(D(k)=M+\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu t_\mu\) with \(M=m+r\sum_\mu(1-c_\mu)\). I checked this against the position-space operator on plane waves (6/6 random cases in each field). Since \(t_\mu\in\{0,\pm i\}\) and \(t_\mu^2=-1\) exactly when \(k_\mu\) is odd,

\[ D(k)\bigl(M-\textstyle\sum\gamma_\mu t_\mu\bigr)=M^2+n_\text{odd},\qquad \det D(k)=\bigl(M^2+n_\text{odd}\bigr)^2, \]

with \(n_\text{odd}\) the number of odd components of \(k\).

Over \(\mathbb Q(i)\). At \(m=0\), \(r=0\): \(M=0\), so \(\det D=n_\text{odd}^2\) vanishes exactly on the 16 momenta with all components in \(\{0,2\}\) (the corners \(k\in\{0,\pi\}^4\)), giving 64 zero modes. At \(r=1\): \(M=n_\text{odd}+2n_2\) with \(n_2\) the number of components equal to 2, and \(M^2+n_\text{odd}=0\) forces \(n_\text{odd}=0\) and \(M=0\), hence \(k=0\) only: 1 momentum, 4 zero modes. The Wilson term removes all 15 doublers, exactly.

With \(n_\text{odd}=0\) there are \(2^4=16\) momenta with \(D(k)=0\) (4 zero modes each). With \(n_\text{odd}=3\) there are \(\binom43\cdot2^3\cdot2=64\) momenta: three odd components with two values each, and the fourth component even (\(k_\mu\in\{0,2\}\), two values). There \(D(k)\) is nilpotent of rank 2 (2 zero modes each). The total is \(16+64=80\) momenta and \(16\cdot4+64\cdot2=192\) zero modes, as in the table. What this does and does not give

  • Gives: an exactly gauge-covariant Dirac operator over \(K=F[i]\) with the \(K\)-structure of the paper; the forced interaction (free operator invariant only globally); an exact current identity with the gauge coupling as its derivative; exact charge-\(k\) covariance; exact fermion doubling and its removal over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), with a computable obstruction over \(\mathbb F_p\).
  • Does not give: dynamics of \(\psi\) (no Grassmann integral, no propagator, no positivity: over \(\mathbb F_p\) the inner product \(\langle a,b\rangle\) takes values in \(K\), not in an ordered field); coupling of \(j\) back to the electric field of Calculation 10; a Minkowski formulation; Lorentz covariance on the hypercubic lattice.
  • Not a theorem about the continuum: the no-go theorem for chiral lattice fermions (Nielsen–Ninomiya, standard reference [5]) is not tested or reproduced here.

        def __mul__(s, o): return FpP(s.v * _v(o))
        __rmul__ = __mul__
        def __truediv__(s, o): return FpP(s.v * pow(_v(o), -1, FpP.P))
        def __rtruediv__(s, o): return FpP(_v(o) * pow(s.v, -1, FpP.P))
        def __eq__(s, o): return s.v == _v(o) % FpP.P
        def __hash__(s): return hash(s.v)
        def __repr__(s): return str(s.v)
    return FpP

def _v(o): return o.v if hasattr(o, "v") else int(o)

def kadd(u, v): return (u[0] + v[0], u[1] + v[1])
def ksub(u, v): return (u[0] - v[0], u[1] - v[1])
def kmul(u, v): return (u[0] * v[0] - u[1] * v[1], u[0] * v[1] + u[1] * v[0])
def kneg(u): return (-u[0], -u[1])

def kinv(u):
    n = u[0] * u[0] + u[1] * u[1]
    return (u[0] / n, -u[1] / n)
def kpow(u, q, one, zero):
    if q < 0: return kpow(kinv(u), -q, one, zero)
    r = (one, zero)
    for _ in range(q): r = kmul(r, u)
    return r

class Ring:
    def __init__(s, mk, p=None):
        s.mk, s.p, s.one, s.zero = mk, p, mk(1), mk(0)
        s.K1, s.K0, s.Ki = (s.one, s.zero), (s.zero, s.zero), (s.zero, s.one)
    def phase(s, a):                                   # U = (1 - i a)/(1 + i a);  a = None means infinity
        if a is None: return (-s.one, s.zero)
        d = s.one + a * a

        return None if u[0] == -s.one else -u[1] / (s.one + u[0])
    def neg_a(s, a): return None if a is None else -a
    def oplus(s, a, b): return s.a_of(kmul(s.phase(a), s.phase(b)))
    def iszero(s, u): return u[0] == s.zero and u[1] == s.zero

def mm(R, A, B):
    n = len(A); m = len(B[0]); k = len(B)
    out = []
    for i in range(n):
        row = []
        for j in range(m):
            s = R.K0
            for t in range(k): s = kadd(s, kmul(A[i][t], B[t][j]))
            row.append(s)
        out.append(row)
    return out
def mv(R, A, v):
    out = []
    for i in range(len(A)):
        s = R.K0
        for t in range(len(v)): s = kadd(s, kmul(A[i][t], v[t]))
        out.append(s)
    return out
def vm(R, v, A):
    out = []
    for j in range(len(A[0])):
        s = R.K0
        for t in range(len(v)): s = kadd(s, kmul(v[t], A[t][j]))
        out.append(s)
    return out
def vdot(R, a, b):
    s = R.K0
    for x, y in zip(a, b): s = kadd(s, kmul(x, y))
    return s
def vs(c, v): return [kmul(c, x) for x in v]
def vadd(a, b): return [kadd(x, y) for x, y in zip(a, b)]
def vsub(a, b): return [ksub(x, y) for x, y in zip(a, b)]
def msc(c, A): return [[kmul(c, x) for x in row] for row in A]
def madd(A, B): return [[kadd(x, y) for x, y in zip(r1, r2)] for r1, r2 in zip(A, B)]
def msub(A, B): return [[ksub(x, y) for x, y in zip(r1, r2)] for r1, r2 in zip(A, B)]
def mdag(A): return [[kconj(A[j][i]) for j in range(len(A))] for i in range(len(A[0]))]
def ident(R, n): return [[R.K1 if i == j else R.K0 for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
def zeros(R, n): return [[R.K0 for _ in range(n)] for _ in range(n)]
def meq(R, A, B): return all(x == y for r1, r2 in zip(A, B) for x, y in zip(r1, r2))

def rank_det(R, M):
    """Gaussian elimination over K: returns (rank, det)."""
    n = len(M); A = [row[:] for row in M]; det = R.K1; rank = 0
    for c in range(n):
        piv = next((r for r in range(rank, n) if not R.iszero(A[r][c])), None)
        if piv is None:
            det = R.K0; continue
        if piv != rank:
            A[piv], A[rank] = A[rank], A[piv]; det = kneg(det)
        det = kmul(det, A[rank][c]); inv = kinv(A[rank][c])
        for r in range(rank + 1, n):
            f = kmul(A[r][c], inv)
            A[r] = [ksub(A[r][t], kmul(f, A[rank][t])) for t in range(n)]
        rank += 1
    return rank, det

def gammas(R):
    o, z, i_ = R.K1, R.K0, R.Ki
    mi = kneg(i_)
    sig = [[[z, o], [o, z]], [[z, mi], [i_, z]], [[o, z], [z, kneg(o)]]]         # Pauli matrices over K
    def blocks(a, b, c, d):
        return [a[0] + b[0], a[1] + b[1], c[0] + d[0], c[1] + d[1]]
    Z2 = [[z, z], [z, z]]; I2 = [[o, z], [z, o]]
    g = []
    for k in range(3):
        mi_s = msc(mi, sig[k]); i_s = msc(i_, sig[k])                            # -i sigma_k and i sigma_k
        g.append(blocks(Z2, mi_s, i_s, Z2))
    g.append(blocks(Z2, I2, I2, Z2))
    return g                                                                      # gamma_1, gamma_2, gamma_3, gamma_4

class Lat:
    def __init__(s, L, d=4):
        s.L, s.d = L, d
        s.sites = list(itertools.product(range(L), repeat=d))
    def f(s, x, m): return tuple((x[i] + (i == m)) % s.L for i in range(s.d))
    def b(s, x, m): return tuple((x[i] - (i == m)) % s.L for i in range(s.d))

class Dirac:
    """Wilson-Dirac operator  D psi(x) = (m+4r) psi(x) - sum_mu [ (r-g_mu)/2 U_mu(x) psi(x+mu) + (r+g_mu)/2 U_mu(x-mu)^-1 psi(x-mu) ]"""
    def __init__(s, R, lat, m, r):
        s.R, s.lat, s.m, s.r = R, lat, m, r
        s.g = gammas(R); I4 = ident(R, 4); half = (R.one / R.mk(2), R.zero)
        rI = msc((R.mk(r), R.zero), I4)
        s.Pm = [msc(half, msub(rI, s.g[mu])) for mu in range(4)]
        s.Pp = [msc(half, madd(rI, s.g[mu])) for mu in range(4)]
        s.diag = (R.mk(m + 4 * r), R.zero)
        s.g5 = mm(R, mm(R, s.g[0], s.g[1]), mm(R, s.g[2], s.g[3]))
    def D(s, U, psi):                                 # column action; U = None means the free operator
        R, lat = s.R, s.lat; out = {}
        for x in lat.sites:
            acc = vs(s.diag, psi[x])
            for mu in range(4):
                xf, xb = lat.f(x, mu), lat.b(x, mu)
                uf = U[(x, mu)] if U else R.K1
                ub = kinv(U[(xb, mu)]) if U else R.K1
                acc = vsub(acc, mv(R, s.Pm[mu], vs(uf, psi[xf])))
                acc = vsub(acc, mv(R, s.Pp[mu], vs(ub, psi[xb])))
            out[x] = acc
        return out
    def Dbar(s, U, pb):                               # row action  (psi-bar D)(y)
        R, lat = s.R, s.lat; out = {}
        for y in lat.sites:
            acc = vs(s.diag, pb[y])
            for mu in range(4):
                yb, yf = lat.b(y, mu), lat.f(y, mu)
                u1 = U[(yb, mu)] if U else R.K1
                u2 = kinv(U[(y, mu)]) if U else R.K1
                acc = vsub(acc, vs(u1, vm(R, pb[yb], s.Pm[mu])))
                acc = vsub(acc, vs(u2, vm(R, pb[yf], s.Pp[mu])))
            out[y] = acc
        return out
    def current(s, U, pb, psi, x, mu):
        R, lat = s.R, s.lat; xf = lat.f(x, mu)
        t1 = vdot(R, pb[x], mv(R, s.Pm[mu], vs(U[(x, mu)], psi[xf])))
        t2 = vdot(R, pb[xf], mv(R, s.Pp[mu], vs(kinv(U[(x, mu)]), psi[x])))
        return ksub(t1, t2)

def rand_vec(R, rng, num): return [(num(rng), num(rng)) for _ in range(4)]

                    g5ps = {x: mv(R, dm.g5, ps[x]) for x in lat.sites}

                res[(r, 1)]["g5herm"] = ghe
    # constant field: j_mu = - psi-bar gamma_mu psi when U = 1
    dm = Dirac(R, lat, 2, 1); pb = rand_vec(R, rng, num); ps = rand_vec(R, rng, num)

    x0 = lat.sites[0]
    const_ok = sum(dm.current(U1, cpb, cst, x0, mu) == kneg(vdot(R, pb, mv(R, dm.g[mu], ps))) for mu in range(4))

# ---- doubling, exact on the L = 4 torus: omega = i is a primitive 4th root of unity in K ----
def doubling_exact(R, r, m=0):
    g = gammas(R); half = (R.one / R.mk(2), R.zero)
    omega = [R.K1, R.Ki, (-R.one, R.zero), kneg(R.Ki)]                        # i^0 ... i^3
    cnt = 0; nullity = 0; accidental = []
    for k in itertools.product(range(4), repeat=4):
        M = (R.mk(m), R.zero); Dk = zeros(R, 4)
        for mu in range(4):
            wp, wm = omega[k[mu]], omega[(-k[mu]) % 4]
            t = kmul(half, ksub(wp, wm)); c = kmul(half, kadd(wp, wm))
            M = kadd(M, kmul((R.mk(r), R.zero), ksub(R.K1, c)))
            Dk = madd(Dk, msc(t, g[mu]))
        Dk = madd(Dk, msc(M, ident(R, 4)))
        rk, _ = rank_det(R, Dk)
        if rk < 4:
            cnt += 1; nullity += 4 - rk
            accidental.append(k)
    return cnt, nullity, accidental

def doubling_predicted(p):
    """Wilson r = 1, m = 0: det D(k) = (M^2 + n_odd)^2 with M = n_odd + 2 n_2.
    n_odd = j components of k odd (2 values each), n_2 = l components equal to 2.  Count momenta with M^2 = -j (mod p)."""
    from math import comb
    cnt = 0
    for j in range(0, 5):
        for l in range(0, 5 - j):
            if ((j + 2 * l) ** 2 + j) % p == 0:
                cnt += comb(4, j) * 2 ** j * comb(4 - j, l)
    return cnt
