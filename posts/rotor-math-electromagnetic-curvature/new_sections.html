Calculation 8: flux through a plaquette, and the duality U(1)

So far the anchor has appeared as the generator that acts on a charge’s velocity. The field strength has a second job: it is the curvature of the U(1) gauge connection, \(F = dA\), and as a curvature it is evaluated on surfaces. This calculation shows where the rotor machinery meets that second job.

Setup. Two vertices \(u = (u^0,\mathbf{u})\) and \(v = (v^0,\mathbf{v})\) span a parallelogram. The edge bivector of the companion finitist papers (the Plücker coordinates of the plane through \(u\) and \(v\)) is

\[ z_{uv} = \bigl(v^0\,\mathbf{u} - u^0\,\mathbf{v}\bigr) \;-\; i\,(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}) \;\in K^3 , \]

bilinear in \(u\) and \(v\). The flux of the field through the parallelogram is the field strength contracted with its area bivector,

\[ \Phi_F(u,v) = F_{\mu\nu}\,u^\mu v^\nu = B\bigl(u,\,A(\zeta_F)\,v\bigr), \qquad B(x,y) = x^{T}\eta\,y, \]

where \(A(\zeta_F)\) is the mixed-index \(F^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\) and the index is lowered with \(\eta\).

The identity. With the complex bilinear pairing \(\zeta_F\cdot z = \sum_k \zeta_{F,k}\,z_k\) (no conjugation) and the dual field \({*F}:(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{B})\to(-\mathbf{B},\mathbf{E})\),

\[ \boxed{\;\zeta_F\cdot z_{uv} \;=\; -\,\Phi_F(u,v) \;+\; i\,\Phi_{*F}(u,v)\;} \]

The real part is minus the flux of \(F\) through the plane of \(u\) and \(v\). The imaginary part is the flux of the dual field, which is what a magnetic charge would couple to. Both sides are polynomial in the components, so the identity involves no division and holds over any field.

Worked example. Take \(\mathbf{E} = (1,2,0)\), \(\mathbf{B} = (0,1,1)\), \(u = (2,1,0,0)\), \(v = (2,0,1,1)\). Then

\[ z_{uv} = (2,-2,-2) + i\,(0,1,-1), \qquad \zeta_F = (1,2,0) + i\,(0,1,1), \qquad \zeta_F\cdot z_{uv} = -2 - 2i . \]

On the matrix side, \(A(\zeta_F)\,v = (2,2,4,0)\), so \(\Phi_F = B(u, A v) = 2\cdot 2 - 1\cdot 2 = 2\), which is \(-\mathrm{Re}(\zeta_F\cdot z_{uv})\). For the dual field \((-\mathbf{B},\mathbf{E})\) one finds \(A'v = (-2,-2,-1,-3)\) and \(\Phi_{*F} = 2\cdot(-2) - 1\cdot(-2) = -2\), which is \(\mathrm{Im}(\zeta_F\cdot z_{uv})\).

Why this is the U(1) connection. A charge \(q\) carried once around the parallelogram in a uniform field picks up the phase \(e^{\,i q \Phi_F}\) (the Aharonov–Bohm or Wilson-loop phase; the sign depends on the coupling convention), because \(\oint A = \int F\) by Stokes. So the U(1) holonomy angle of a plaquette is \(\Phi_F = -\mathrm{Re}(\zeta_F\cdot z_{uv})\). The same bivector \(z_{uv}\) that generates a Lorentz rotation in the plane of \(u\) and \(v\) also measures the flux of \(F\) through that plane. That is the link between the rotor machinery and the gauge curvature.

I checked the identity on 400 random samples in each of exact integers, \(\mathbb{F}_7\) and \(\mathbb{F}_{11}\): the real part matched \(-\Phi_F\) and the imaginary part matched \(\Phi_{*F}\) in all 400 cases each time.

The duality U(1)

Multiplying the anchor by a norm-one \(\lambda = c + is\) (\(c^2 + s^2 = 1\)) rotates the field,

\[ \zeta_F \to \lambda\,\zeta_F \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\mathbf{E},\mathbf{B}) \to (c\mathbf{E} - s\mathbf{B},\; s\mathbf{E} + c\mathbf{B}), \]

which is electromagnetic duality, \(F \to cF + s\,{*F}\). Because \(\zeta_F\cdot z_{uv}\) is linear in \(\zeta_F\), the flux pair rotates as a doublet:

\[ \Phi_F' = c\,\Phi_F + s\,\Phi_{*F}, \qquad \Phi_{*F}' = -s\,\Phi_F + c\,\Phi_{*F}. \]

Checked over \(\mathbb{F}_7\) and \(\mathbb{F}_{11}\) on 400 random fields each:

Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) I also checked, with exact rotors, that a duality rotation by \(\lambda = \tfrac35 + \tfrac45 i\) commutes with a Lorentz transformation: once for a pure boost and once for the non-simple rotor \((a,p,\kappa,\rho) = (2,3,(1,\tfrac12,0),(6,0,0))\). This follows from the fact that Lorentz transformations act on \(\zeta\) by complex rotations, which commute with scalar multiplication.

Two different U(1)s

The duality group is the same group, the norm-one elements of \(K\), in which the Cayley phases \((1-a\delta)/(1+a\delta)\) live. But it plays a different role. Duality rotates the field \((\mathbf{E},\mathbf{B})\) and is a symmetry of the source-free equations. A gauge phase rotates the wavefunction of a charged particle and leaves \(F\) unchanged. The duality U(1) is intrinsic to the rotor structure. The gauge U(1) is not: it is a connection on a line bundle and must be supplied separately, which is what Calculation 9 does.

Calculation 9: the gauge U(1) as a Cayley phase lattice

Take the link variable to be the Cayley phase

\[ U = \frac{1 - i a}{1 + i a} = e^{-2i\arctan a}, \qquad a \in \mathbb{P}^1 \ (a = \infty \mapsto U = -1), \]

the rational parametrization of the circle. Phases multiply by tangent addition, \(\mathrm{phase}(a)\,\mathrm{phase}(b) = \mathrm{phase}(a\oplus b)\) with \(a \oplus b = (a+b)/(1-ab)\). The plaquette variable is the ordered product of four link phases, with \(a_P\) obtained by tangent addition of the four link values. To connect with Calculation 8, define the lattice flux through a plaquette by \(U_P = e^{\,i q\Phi}\), i.e. \(a_P = -\tan(q\Phi/2)\), so that \(a_P \approx -q\Phi/2\) for small flux.

A fuller 4D run (an \(L = 3\) torus, all rectangles up to \(2\times2\), in the companion script cayley_u1_lattice.py) gives plaquette gauge invariance 486/486, rectangle Stokes 1944/1944, Bianchi 324/324 in all three fields. Over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) the map from \(\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)\) to the norm-one group of \(\mathbb{F}_p[i]\) is a bijection onto \(p+1\) elements (8 for \(p=7\), 12 for \(p=11\)) and a homomorphism on all \((p+1)^2\) pairs.

In field language these are the homogeneous Maxwell equations: Stokes says \(\oint A = \int F\), and Bianchi says \(dF = 0\) (Faraday’s law and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0\)). They hold exactly, in rational and finite-field arithmetic. This is not a surprise: the group is abelian and the Cayley map is an isomorphism onto the circle group, and \(F\) is defined as the plaquette product, so \(dF = 0\) is an identity of the construction and not a dynamical statement.

The action, and which potential to use

The Wilson term is rational in the Cayley variable:

\[ 1 - \mathrm{Re}\,U = \frac{2a^2}{1+a^2} = \tfrac12 N(1 - U), \]

so over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) it has a canonical \(\mathbb{F}_p\)-valued analogue. Compare it with the quadratic “Cayley \(F^2\)” action \(\sum a_P^2\) on a smooth periodic field in the real regime (\(A_0 = 0.5\cos 2\pi x_1\), \(A_2 = 0.5\cos 2\pi x_3\) on an \(L^4\) torus with \(\epsilon = 1/L\); continuum \(\sum_{\mu<\nu}\!\int F_{\mu\nu}^2 = 9.8696\), so the target is \(W \to 4.9348\)):

Both actions converge to the continuum \(F^2\) energy with \(O(\epsilon^2)\) error (the last column times \(L^2\) settles near \(-3.3\)). Their ratio differs from 1 only at \(O(\epsilon^4)\), because per plaquette \(2a^2/(1-\cos\theta) = 1 + \theta^2/4 + \cdots\) and \(\theta = O(\epsilon^2)\). At strong coupling they are different theories. For a single plaquette, with Wilson weight \(e^{-\beta(1-\cos\theta)}\) and Cayley weight \(e^{-2\beta a^2}\):

The half-trace potential is not the Maxwell action

If \(U(1)\) is embedded in \(SL(2)\) as \(\mathrm{diag}(U,\bar U)\), the half-trace of the holonomy is \(w = \mathrm{Re}\,U\), and the potential \(N(1-w) = (1-w)^2\) used in the finitist papers becomes \((1-\mathrm{Re}\,U)^2 = 4a^4/(1+a^2)^2 \approx \theta^4/4\). That is quartic in the plaquette angle and vanishes in the continuum limit, instead of reproducing \(F^2\). I checked the \(SU(2)\) version on a smooth non-commuting field: summed over the lattice it scales like \(\epsilon^4\) and approaches \(\tfrac14\int\sum(F^aF^a)^2\), not the Yang–Mills energy. For a statement about electromagnetism, use \(1-\mathrm{Re}\,U = \tfrac12 N(1-U)\) (Wilson) or \(\sum a_P^2\).

What this does and does not give

  • Gives: an exact, rational U(1) lattice gauge theory in which the plaquette flux is the curvature of Calculation 8, with exact Stokes and Bianchi identities over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and \(\mathbb{F}_p\), and the correct \(F^2\) continuum limit.
  • Does not give: the inhomogeneous equations \(d{*F} = J\) (Gauss’s law and Ampère’s law), which would need a dual lattice and a Hodge star; any dynamics for the potential; or a continuum comparison over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), where there is no ordering and no notion of a weak field. Over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) only the exact identities survive.
  • Not provided by the rotor machinery: the gauge structure itself. The rotor calculus supplies the exact arithmetic and the flux pairing with the plaquette bivector. The connection on the line bundle is extra input.

Reproducing Calculations 8 and 9

The script below reproduces every table in Calculations 8 and 9 except the continuum and strong-coupling comparisons (those use cayley_u1_lattice.py). It needs only the standard library and SymPy, and runs in about a second.

"""
Reproduction script for Calculations 8 and 9 (flux through a plaquette, duality U(1), Cayley U(1) lattice).

  1. Flux identity   F_{mu nu} u^mu v^nu = B(u, A(zeta_F) v) = -Re(zeta_F . z_uv),   *F-flux = +Im(zeta_F . z_uv)
        over Z (exact integers) and over F_7, F_11
  2. Duality U(1)    norm-one lambda = c + i s acts on zeta_F by (E,B) -> (cE - sB, sE + cB)
        over F_p:  E^2+B^2 invariant, zeta.zeta -> lambda^2 zeta.zeta, flux doublet rotates by lambda
        over Q:    commutes with a Lorentz transformation (exact rational rotors)
  3. Cayley U(1) lattice (3D torus, L = 3): plaquette = ordered product of Cayley phases,
        Stokes on a whole plane, Bianchi on every cube, Wilson term N(1-U) = 4 a^2/(1+a^2),
        exactly, over Q (Fractions) and over F_7, F_11

Needs: sympy (part 2 only).  Colab / terminal: just run it.
"""
import itertools
import random
from fractions import Fraction as Fr

import sympy as sp

ETA = [1, -1, -1, -1]


def Amat(E, B):                                   # anchor matrix A(zeta), zeta = E + iB  (entries: ints, Fractions or sympy)
    return [[0, E[0], E[1], E[2]],
            [E[0], 0, B[2], -B[1]],
            [E[1], -B[2], 0, B[0]],
            [E[2], B[1], -B[0], 0]]


def apply(M, v): return [sum(M[i][j] * v[j] for j in range(4)) for i in range(4)]
def Bf(x, y): return sum(ETA[i] * x[i] * y[i] for i in range(4))


# ------------------------------------------------------------ 1. flux --------
def flux_check(p, n=400, seed=1):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    red = (lambda x: x % p) if p else (lambda x: x)
    okre = okim = 0
    for _ in range(n):
        E = [rng.randint(-9, 9) for _ in range(3)]; B = [rng.randint(-9, 9) for _ in range(3)]
        u = [rng.randint(-9, 9) for _ in range(4)]; v = [rng.randint(-9, 9) for _ in range(4)]
        flux = Bf(u, apply(Amat(E, B), v))                              # F_{mu nu} u^mu v^nu
        dflux = Bf(u, apply(Amat([-b for b in B], E), v))               # flux of the dual field (E,B) -> (-B,E)
        kap = [v[0] * u[k] - u[0] * v[k] for k in (1, 2, 3)]            # z_uv = kap + i rho  (rotor-paper edge bivector)
        cr = [u[2] * v[3] - u[3] * v[2], u[3] * v[1] - u[1] * v[3], u[1] * v[2] - u[2] * v[1]]
        rho = [-c for c in cr]
        re = sum(E[k] * kap[k] - B[k] * rho[k] for k in range(3))       # Re(zeta_F . z_uv)
        im = sum(E[k] * rho[k] + B[k] * kap[k] for k in range(3))       # Im(zeta_F . z_uv)
        okre += red(re) == red(-flux)
        okim += red(im) == red(dflux)
    return okre, okim, n


# --------------------------------------------------------- 2. duality --------
def duality_check_Fp(p, n=400, seed=2):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    circle = [(c, s) for c in range(p) for s in range(p) if (c * c + s * s) % p == 1]
    ok = dict(norm=0, zz=0, rot=0, flux=0)
    for _ in range(n):
        E = [rng.randrange(p) for _ in range(3)]; B = [rng.randrange(p) for _ in range(3)]
        c, s = rng.choice(circle)
        E2 = [(c * e - s * b) % p for e, b in zip(E, B)]; B2 = [(s * e + c * b) % p for e, b in zip(E, B)]
        ok["norm"] += sum(e * e + b * b for e, b in zip(E, B)) % p == sum(e * e + b * b for e, b in zip(E2, B2)) % p
        zz = lambda E, B: (sum(e * e - b * b for e, b in zip(E, B)) % p, 2 * sum(e * b for e, b in zip(E, B)) % p)
        re, im = zz(E, B)                                               # zeta.zeta = re + i im
        expected = (((c * c - s * s) * re - 2 * c * s * im) % p, (2 * c * s * re + (c * c - s * s) * im) % p)   # lambda^2 * zeta.zeta
        ok["zz"] += zz(E2, B2) == expected
        # flux doublet (Phi, Phi_dual) for a random plaquette (u, v)
        u = [rng.randrange(p) for _ in range(4)]; v = [rng.randrange(p) for _ in range(4)]
        fl = lambda E, B: (Bf(u, apply(Amat(E, B), v)) % p, Bf(u, apply(Amat([-b for b in B], E), v)) % p)
        P, Pd = fl(E, B); P2, Pd2 = fl(E2, B2)
        ok["flux"] += (P2 == (c * P + s * Pd) % p) and (Pd2 == (-s * P + c * Pd) % p)
        ok["rot"] += len(circle) == p + 1
    return ok, n, len(circle)


def duality_commutes_with_lorentz():
    I = sp.I
    sig = [sp.Matrix([[0, 1], [1, 0]]), sp.Matrix([[0, -I], [I, 0]]), sp.Matrix([[1, 0], [0, -1]])]
    X = lambda x: x[0] * sp.eye(2) + x[1] * sig[0] + x[2] * sig[1] + x[3] * sig[2]
    comps = lambda H: [(H[0, 0] + H[1, 1]) / 2, (H[0, 1] + H[1, 0]) / 2, (H[1, 0] - H[0, 1]) / (2 * I), (H[0, 0] - H[1, 1]) / 2]

    def Lam(a, p, kap, rho):
        G = (a + I * p) * sp.eye(2) + sum(((kap[i] + I * rho[i]) * sig[i] for i in range(3)), sp.zeros(2))
        N = a ** 2 - p ** 2 - sum(c ** 2 for c in kap) + sum(c ** 2 for c in rho)
        cols = [[sp.simplify(c) for c in comps(G * X([int(i == j) for i in range(4)]) * G.H / N)] for j in range(4)]
        return sp.Matrix(cols).T

    c, s = sp.Rational(3, 5), sp.Rational(4, 5)                          # lambda = 3/5 + 4i/5, norm one
    E = [sp.Rational(1), sp.Rational(2), sp.Rational(0)]; B = [sp.Rational(0), sp.Rational(1), sp.Rational(3)]
    rot = lambda E, B: ([c * e - s * b for e, b in zip(E, B)], [s * e + c * b for e, b in zip(E, B)])
    A4 = lambda E, B: sp.Matrix(Amat(E, B))
    extract = lambda M: ([M[0, 1], M[0, 2], M[0, 3]], [M[2, 3], M[3, 1], M[1, 2]])
    out = []
    for name, rotor in (("boost (2,0,(1,0,0),0)", (2, 0, [1, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0])),
                        ("non-simple (2,3,(1,1/2,0),(6,0,0))", (2, 3, [1, sp.Rational(1, 2), 0], [6, 0, 0]))):
        L = Lam(*rotor)
        F1 = L * A4(*rot(E, B)) * L.inv()                                  # rotate by lambda, then Lorentz transform
        Ek, Bk = extract(L * A4(E, B) * L.inv())                           # Lorentz transform, then rotate by lambda
        F2 = A4(*rot(Ek, Bk))
        out.append((name, sp.simplify(F1 - F2) == sp.zeros(4) and sp.simplify(L * A4(E, B) * L.inv() - A4(Ek, Bk)) == sp.zeros(4)))
    return out


# ----------------------------------------------------- 3. Cayley lattice -----
class Fp:
    p = 7
    def __init__(s, v): s.v = int(v) % Fp.p
    def __add__(s, o): return Fp(s.v + _v(o))
    __radd__ = __add__
    def __sub__(s, o): return Fp(s.v - _v(o))
    def __neg__(s): return Fp(-s.v)
    def __mul__(s, o): return Fp(s.v * _v(o))
    __rmul__ = __mul__
    def __truediv__(s, o): return Fp(s.v * pow(_v(o), -1, Fp.p))
    def __eq__(s, o): return s.v == _v(o) % Fp.p
    def __hash__(s): return hash(s.v)


def _v(o): return o.v if isinstance(o, Fp) else int(o)


def lattice_checks(mk, rand, L=3, seed=3):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    one, zero = mk(1), mk(0)

    def phase(a):                                                       # U = (1 - i a)/(1 + i a);  a = None means infinity
        if a is None: return (-one, zero)
        d = one + a * a
        return ((one - a * a) / d, mk(-2) * a / d)

    gm = lambda u, v: (u[0] * v[0] - u[1] * v[1], u[0] * v[1] + u[1] * v[0])
    gi = lambda u: (u[0], -u[1])                                        # norm one: inverse = conjugate
    a_of = lambda u: None if u[0] == -one else -u[1] / (one + u[0])
    sites = list(itertools.product(range(L), repeat=3))
    sh = lambda x, m: tuple((x[i] + (i == m)) % L for i in range(3))
    U = {(x, m): phase(rand(rng)) for x in sites for m in range(3)}
    P = lambda x, m, n: gm(gm(U[(x, m)], U[(sh(x, m), n)]), gm(gi(U[(sh(x, n), m)]), gi(U[(x, n)])))
    ident = (one, zero)
    # Stokes on a torus: the product of all plaquettes of a 2-plane (fixed transverse coordinate) is 1
    st = tot = 0
    for m, n in ((0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 0)):
        r = ({0, 1, 2} - {m, n}).pop()
        for t in range(L):
            B = ident
            for i in range(L):
                for j in range(L):
                    x = [0, 0, 0]; x[m], x[n], x[r] = i, j, t
                    B = gm(B, P(tuple(x), m, n))
            tot += 1; st += (B == ident)
    # Bianchi (dF = 0): boundary of every unit cube
    bi = 0
    for x in sites:
        B = ident
        for (m, n, o) in ((1, 2, 0), (2, 0, 1), (0, 1, 2)):
            B = gm(B, gm(P(x, m, n), gi(P(sh(x, o), m, n))))
        bi += (B == ident)
    # Wilson term N(1-U_P) = 4 a_P^2 / (1 + a_P^2)
    wl = n_pl = 0
    for x in sites:
        for m, n in ((0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 0)):
            Up = P(x, m, n); a = a_of(Up)
            lhs = (one - Up[0]) * (one - Up[0]) + Up[1] * Up[1]
            rhs = mk(4) if a is None else mk(4) * a * a / (one + a * a)
            n_pl += 1; wl += (lhs == rhs)
    return (st, tot), (bi, len(sites)), (wl, n_pl)


def main():
    print("1. flux identity:  -Re(zeta_F . z_uv) = F_{mu nu} u^mu v^nu ,   Im(zeta_F . z_uv) = flux of the dual field")
    for label, p in (("Z (exact)", None), ("F_7", 7), ("F_11", 11)):
        r, i, n = flux_check(p)
        print(f"   {label:<10} Re: {r}/{n}   Im: {i}/{n}")
    print("\n2. duality U(1): zeta_F -> lambda zeta_F,  lambda = c + i s,  c^2 + s^2 = 1")
    for p in (7, 11):
        ok, n, size = duality_check_Fp(p)
        print(f"   F_{p}: |U(1)| = {size} (= p+1);  E^2+B^2 invariant {ok['norm']}/{n};  zeta.zeta -> lambda^2 zeta.zeta {ok['zz']}/{n};"
              f"  flux doublet rotates by lambda {ok['flux']}/{n}")
    for name, good in duality_commutes_with_lorentz():
        print(f"   Q: duality rotation commutes with the Lorentz transformation of {name}: {good}")
    print("\n3. Cayley U(1) lattice, 3D torus L = 3  (Stokes on whole planes, Bianchi on cubes, Wilson term)")
    rq = lambda rng: None if rng.random() < 0.06 else Fr(rng.randint(-6, 6), rng.randint(1, 4))
    (s, t), (b, nb), (w, nw) = lattice_checks(Fr, rq)
    print(f"   Q (Fractions): Stokes {s}/{t}, Bianchi {b}/{nb}, Wilson identity {w}/{nw}")
    for p in (7, 11):
        Fp.p = p
        rf = lambda rng: None if rng.randrange(p + 1) == p else Fp(rng.randrange(p))
        (s, t), (b, nb), (w, nw) = lattice_checks(Fp, rf)
        print(f"   F_{p}: Stokes {s}/{t}, Bianchi {b}/{nb}, Wilson identity {w}/{nw}")


main()
