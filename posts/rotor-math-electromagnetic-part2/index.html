In Part 1, we explored how relativistic rotations, boosts, and electromagnetic fields can be formulated without square roots, rapidities, or transcendental functions using \(2 \times 2\) projective rotors over the Blue Dihedron extension \(K = \mathbb{F}[T]/(T^2+1)\). We established that the complex rotor anchor \(\zeta = \kappa + i\rho\) is algebraically the electromagnetic field strength, that null anchors correspond to radiation fields, and that link rotors give a rational Cayley lattice with an exact Gauss law.

A central question arises: How does this algebraic rotor geometry connect to the standard continuum physics of gauge theory and electromagnetic curvature?

In standard quantum electrodynamics (QED), gauge interactions are derived from a fundamental symmetry principle: starting with a free Dirac spinor Lagrangian possessing a global phase symmetry, promoting that symmetry to local gauge invariance demands the introduction of a compensating gauge field \(A_\mu\). The field strength tensor \(F_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{i}{e}[D_\mu, D_\nu]\) appears as the curvature of this gauge connection, and gauge invariance strictly forbids a photon mass term.

In this post, we establish the exact algebraic dictionary between the continuum gauge theory of physics lectures and our projective rotor architecture. We show that:

  1. Curvature is an exact projective quadrea: The Wilson plaquette action \(W_1\) is not an infinitesimal Taylor approximation of flux; it is identically twice the projective solid spread \(\mathcal{S}\) of a tripod.
  2. Tangent addition drives parallel transport: Plaquette holonomy is governed rationally by the Cayley tangent addition law \(a_P = \bigoplus a_i\).
  3. The spin lift is metrically observable: While vector rotations only see the rotation spread \(4\mathcal{S}(1-\mathcal{S})\), the quartic gauge potential \(W_2 = (1-w)^2 = 4\mathcal{S}^2\) detects the non-trivial \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) spin flip (\(-I_2\)) at an orthogonal tripod.
  4. The physics is field-independent: All geometric and field-theoretic identities hold over the rationals \(\mathbb{Q}\) and finite fields like \(\mathbb{F}_7\) with zero loss of algebraic precision.

1. The Rosetta Stone: Continuum Field Theory vs. Projective Simplices

In the standard textbook presentation of Abelian gauge theory, one begins with a free Dirac matter field \(\psi\) and its Lagrangian: \[\mathcal{L} = \hbar c\,\bar{\psi}\gamma^\mu \partial_\mu \psi + mc^2 \bar{\psi}\psi\]

Under a local phase transformation \(\psi(x) \mapsto e^{ie\phi(x)}\psi(x)\), the ordinary derivative activates the product rule: \[\partial_\mu \left(e^{ie\phi(x)}\psi\right) = e^{ie\phi(x)}\partial_\mu \psi + ie(\partial_\mu\phi)e^{ie\phi(x)}\psi\] This breaks gauge invariance. To restore invariance, one introduces the gauge covariant derivative: \[D_\mu \equiv \partial_\mu + ie A_\mu\] demanding that the compensating gauge field transform as \(A_\mu \mapsto A_\mu - \partial_\mu \phi(x)\). Evaluating the commutator of two covariant derivatives on the matter field eliminates the derivative terms and isolates the field strength: \[[D_\mu, D_\nu]\psi = ie F_{\mu\nu}\psi \quad \Longrightarrow \quad F_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{i}{e}[D_\mu, D_\nu] = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu\] Adding a dynamic term \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin}} \sim F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\) allows the field to propagate, while a Proca mass term \(m_A^2 A_\mu A^\mu\) is strictly forbidden because \((A_\mu - \partial_\mu\phi)^2 \ne A^2\).

2. Projective Tripods and the Solid Spread \(\mathcal{S}\)

In three-dimensional projective space \(\mathbb{P}^2\) (ambient vector space \(V^3\) equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form \(B\)), three non-collinear projective points represented by non-null vectors \(v_1, v_2, v_3\) form a tripod \(\gamma\).

The fundamental metrical invariant of this tripod is its projective solid spread (projective quadrea) \(\mathcal{S}\), defined as the normalized \(3 \times 3\) Gram determinant: \[\mathcal{S} \equiv \frac{\det(G_3)}{Q(v_1) Q(v_2) Q(v_3)}\] where \([G_3]_{ij} = B(v_i, v_j)\) and \(Q(v) \equiv B(v, v)\).

Base Spread \(\times\) Pitch Factorization

Just as the projective quadrume of a tetrahedron factorizes into a base face quadrea and an altitude quadrance (\(\mathcal{V} = A_k \times h_k\)), the solid spread of a tripod factorizes into: \[\mathcal{S} = \mathcal{S}_{\text{base}} \times \text{pitch} = q(v_1, v_2) \times h(v_3)\] where: * \(q(v_1, v_2) \equiv 1 - \frac{B(v_1, v_2)^2}{Q(v_1)Q(v_2)}\) is the projective quadrance between base lines \(v_1\) and \(v_2\). * \(n \equiv v_1 \times_B v_2\) is the dual vector orthogonal to the base plane under \(B\). * \(h(v_3) \equiv \frac{B(v_3, n)^2}{Q(v_3)Q(n)}\) is the projective altitude quadrance (the pitch) of \(v_3\) relative to that plane.

This confirms that \(\mathcal{S}\) is an intrinsic, scale-invariant angular measure of the aperture of the tripod.

3. The Algebraic Bridge: From Tangent Addition to Gauge Potentials

In lattice electrodynamics, parallel transport along edge \(j\) is represented by a rational Cayley parameter \(a_j \in \mathbb{F}\): \[U_j = \frac{1 - i a_j}{1 + i a_j} \in U(1) \subset K\] Under group multiplication, two successive Cayley transporters compose by rational tangent addition: \[U_1 U_2 = \frac{1 - i(a_1 \oplus a_2)}{1 + i(a_1 \oplus a_2)}, \quad \text{where } a_1 \oplus a_2 \equiv \frac{a_1 + a_2}{1 - a_1 a_2}\]

For an oriented 4-link plaquette \(P = (1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 1)\), the composite holonomy is: \[U_P = U_1 U_2 U_3 U_4 = \frac{1 - i a_P}{1 + i a_P}, \quad \text{with } a_P \equiv a_1 \oplus a_2 \oplus a_3 \oplus a_4\]

The Trace Scalar \(w\)

In the \(2 \times 2\) spinor representation, the normalized trace of \(U_P\) is the scalar: \[w \equiv \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(U_P) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1 - i a_P}{1 + i a_P} + \frac{1 + i a_P}{1 - i a_P}\right) = \frac{1 - a_P^2}{1 + a_P^2}\] This is the algebraic half-angle formula \(\cos\theta = \frac{1 - \tan^2(\theta/2)}{1 + \tan^2(\theta/2)}\) evaluated purely rationally.

Connecting Holonomy to Solid Spread: \(w = 1 - 2\mathcal{S}\)

When the plaquette is regarded as a circuit of rays, the holonomy trace satisfies: \[w = 1 - 2\mathcal{S}\] Equating both expressions for \(w\): \[1 - 2\mathcal{S} = \frac{1 - a_P^2}{1 + a_P^2} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mathcal{S} = \frac{a_P^2}{1 + a_P^2}\] Inverting this expression gives the plaquette Cayley parameter directly in terms of the geometric solid spread: \[a_P^2 = \frac{\mathcal{S}}{1 - \mathcal{S}}\]

The Exact Physical Potentials

This yields closed rational formulas for the gauge action potentials: * The Wilson-type Plaquette Potential (\(W_1\)): \[W_1 \equiv 1 - w = 2\mathcal{S} = \frac{2 a_P^2}{1 + a_P^2}\] In the weak-field continuum limit where \(a_P \approx \frac{1}{2}e\Phi \ll 1\): \[W_1 \approx 2\left(\frac{e\Phi}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2}e^2 \Phi^2\] recovering the Maxwell action. The projective quadrea \(\mathcal{S}\) is identically half the Wilson action. * The Squared (Quartic) Potential (\(W_2\)): \[W_2 \equiv (1 - w)^2 = 4\mathcal{S}^2 = \frac{4 a_P^4}{(1 + a_P^2)^2}\] * The Rotation-Native Spread: \[1 - w^2 = 4\mathcal{S}(1 - \mathcal{S}) = \frac{4 a_P^2}{(1 + a_P^2)^2}\]

4. The \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) Spin Lift: Fermion Detection vs. Boson Rotation

In relativistic quantum mechanics, Dirac wavefunctions transform under the double cover \(\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\) rather than the vector Lorentz group \(\mathrm{SO}(1,3)\). The geometric tripod framework reflects this distinction:

  • Coplanar Tripod (\(\mathcal{S} = 0\)):
    The three rays lie in a single plane.
    \[w = 1 - 2(0) = +1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad U_P = +I_2, \quad W_1 = 0, \quad W_2 = 0\] Both the spinor transport and the spatial rotation are trivial.

  • Orthogonal Tripod (\(\mathcal{S} = 1\)):
    The three rays are mutually perpendicular.
    \[w = 1 - 2(1) = -1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad U_P = -I_2\] Under the vector congruence action \(X \mapsto U_P X U_P^\dagger\), the minus signs cancel: \((-I_2)X(-I_2) = X\). The induced spatial rotation in \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) is the pure identity. A classical vector field observes zero rotation.

    However, the spinor holonomy is \(-I_2\) (the non-trivial center of the spin group). The potentials evaluate to: \[W_1 = 1 - (-1) = 2, \qquad W_2 = (1 - (-1))^2 = 4\] while the rotation-native spread vanishes: \[1 - w^2 = 1 - (-1)^2 = 0\]

Detecting the Spin Lift

The quartic potential \(W_2 = (1 - w)^2\) reaches its maximum value of \(4\) at \(\mathcal{S} = 1\). It explicitly detects the non-trivial \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) spin flip of the Dirac wavefunction, whereas the rotation-native spread \(1 - w^2 = 4\mathcal{S}(1 - \mathcal{S})\) measures only the unlifted spatial rotation.

5. Worked Calculations

Calculation 11: Exact Tripod Gram Factorization and Potentials over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and \(\mathbb{F}_7\)

Let the ambient space \(V^3\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\) have standard Euclidean metric \(B = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 1)\). Consider the tripod formed by: \[v_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad v_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad v_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\]

import sympy as sp

# Ambient metric and rays
B = sp.eye(3)
v1 = sp.Matrix([1, 0, 0])
v2 = sp.Matrix([1, 1, 0])
v3 = sp.Matrix([1, 1, 1])

def Q(v): return (v.T * B * v)[0, 0]
def dot_B(u, v): return (u.T * B * v)[0, 0]

# Gram matrix and solid spread
G3 = sp.Matrix([[dot_B(vi, vj) for vj in [v1, v2, v3]] for vi in [v1, v2, v3]])
S_gram = G3.det() / (Q(v1) * Q(v2) * Q(v3))

# Factorization: base spread x pitch
q_base = 1 - (dot_B(v1, v2)**2) / (Q(v1) * Q(v2))
n = sp.Matrix([0, 0, 1])  # v1 x v2
pitch = (dot_B(v3, n)**2) / (Q(v3) * Q(n))

# Potentials
w = 1 - 2 * S_gram
W1 = 1 - w
W2 = (1 - w)**2
rot_spread = 1 - w**2
a_P_sq = S_gram / (1 - S_gram)

print(f"det(G3) = {G3.det()}")
print(f"S (Solid Spread) = {S_gram}")
print(f"q_base * pitch  = {q_base * pitch}")
print(f"w (Holonomy Trace) = {w}")
print(f"W1 (Wilson Potential) = {W1}")
print(f"W2 (Squared Potential) = {W2}")
print(f"Rotation Spread = {rot_spread}")
print(f"a_P^2 = {a_P_sq}")

Evaluation over \(\mathbb{Q}\): * \(Q(v_1) = 1\), \(Q(v_2) = 2\), \(Q(v_3) = 3\). * \(\det(G_3) = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = 1\). * \(\mathcal{S} = \frac{1}{1 \times 2 \times 3} = \frac{1}{6}\). * Base spread: \(q(v_1, v_2) = 1 - \frac{1^2}{1 \times 2} = \frac{1}{2}\). * Pitch: \(h(v_3) = \frac{1^2}{3 \times 1} = \frac{1}{3}\). * \(\mathcal{S}_{\text{base}} \times \text{pitch} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6} \equiv \mathcal{S}\). * \(w = 1 - 2(1/6) = \frac{2}{3}\). * \(W_1 = 2\mathcal{S} = \frac{1}{3}\). * \(W_2 = 4\mathcal{S}^2 = \frac{1}{9}\). * Rotation spread: \(1 - w^2 = \frac{5}{9} = 4\mathcal{S}(1 - \mathcal{S})\). * Cayley parameter: \(a_P^2 = \frac{1/6}{5/6} = \frac{1}{5}\), with \(\frac{1 - 1/5}{1 + 1/5} = \frac{2}{3} = w\).

Evaluation over \(\mathbb{F}_7\):
Reducing modulo 7 (where \(2^{-1}=4, 3^{-1}=5, 5^{-1}=3, 6^{-1}=6\)): * \(\mathcal{S} \equiv 6^{-1} \equiv 6 \pmod 7\). * \(w = 1 - 2(6) = -11 \equiv 3 \pmod 7\) (Matches \(\frac{2}{3} \equiv 2 \times 5 = 10 \equiv 3\)). * \(W_1 = 2(6) \equiv 5 \pmod 7\) (Matches \(\frac{1}{3} \equiv 5\)). * \(W_2 = 4(36) \equiv 4 \pmod 7\) (Matches \(\frac{1}{9} \equiv 4\)). * \(a_P^2 = 5^{-1} \equiv 3 \pmod 7\). * Consistency: \(\frac{a_P^2}{1 + a_P^2} \equiv \frac{3}{1+3} = \frac{3}{4} = 3 \times 2 = 6 \equiv \mathcal{S} \pmod 7\).

Calculation 12: Riemann–Silberstein Anchor and Maxwell Invariants

In (1+3)D spacetime with metric \(\eta = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)\), let the electromagnetic field be: \[\mathbf{E} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\] The Riemann–Silberstein vector in \(K^3\) is \(\zeta \equiv \mathbf{E} + i\mathbf{B} = (2, 1+i, 3i)^T\).

E = sp.Matrix([2, 1, 0])
B_field = sp.Matrix([0, 1, 3])
i = sp.I

# Riemann-Silberstein dot product
zeta = [E[j] + i * B_field[j] for j in range(3)]
zeta_dot_zeta = sum(z**2 for z in zeta)

maxwell_kin = sum(e**2 for e in E) - sum(b**2 for b in B_field)
theta_density = 2 * sum(e * b for e, b in zip(E, B_field))

print(f"zeta . zeta = {sp.expand(zeta_dot_zeta)}")
print(f"Re(zeta . zeta) [Maxwell kinetic] = {maxwell_kin}")
print(f"Im(zeta . zeta) [Theta density]   = {theta_density}")

# Verify Lie algebra condition: eta * A^T * eta = -A
eta = sp.diag(1, -1, -1, -1)
A_zeta = sp.Matrix([
    [0,     E[0],       E[1],       E[2]],
    [E[0],  0,          B_field[2], -B_field[1]],
    [E[1], -B_field[2], 0,          B_field[0]],
    [E[2],  B_field[1], -B_field[0], 0]
])
assert eta * A_zeta.T * eta == -A_zeta
print("Lie algebra condition eta * A^T * eta == -A verified.")

Evaluation over \(\mathbb{Q}\): \[\zeta \cdot \zeta = 2^2 + (1 + i)^2 + (3i)^2 = 4 + (2i) - 9 = -5 + 2i\] * Maxwell Kinetic Density: \(\operatorname{Re}(\zeta \cdot \zeta) = \mathbf{E}^2 - \mathbf{B}^2 = (4 + 1 + 0) - (0 + 1 + 9) = 5 - 10 = -5\). * Topological \(\theta\)-Density: \(\operatorname{Im}(\zeta \cdot \zeta) = 2(\mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{B}) = 2(0 + 1 + 0) = 2\).

Evaluation over \(\mathbb{F}_7\): \[\operatorname{Re}(\zeta \cdot \zeta) = -5 \equiv 2 \pmod 7, \qquad \operatorname{Im}(\zeta \cdot \zeta) = 2 \equiv 2 \pmod 7\] The field invariants evaluate without square roots or real-field limits.

Summary

The continuum gauge principle and the projective trigonometry of simplices describe the same underlying physics. Promoting a global spinor symmetry to local gauge invariance introduces the gauge connection \(A_\mu\), whose infinitesimal loop curvature is the field strength \(F_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{i}{e}[D_\mu, D_\nu]\). In our discrete rotor framework, this curvature is represented by the solid spread \(\mathcal{S}\) of a projective tripod: the scalar trace of the holonomy is \(w = 1 - 2\mathcal{S}\), and the Wilson plaquette action \(W_1 = 1 - w\) is identically \(2\mathcal{S}\), without requiring an infinitesimal continuum Taylor approximation.

The Cayley link parameters compose under rational tangent addition \(a_P = \bigoplus a_i\), establishing that \(\mathcal{S} = \frac{a_P^2}{1 + a_P^2}\). The quartic gauge potential \(W_2 = (1-w)^2 = 4\mathcal{S}^2\) detects the \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) spin lift of the Dirac field at an orthogonal tripod (\(\mathcal{S}=1\), \(w=-1\)), where the induced spatial rotation is trivial but the spinor holonomy is \(-I_2\). Meanwhile, the Riemann–Silberstein vector \(\zeta = \mathbf{E} + i\mathbf{B}\) packages both the Maxwell kinetic term and the topological \(\theta\)-density into the complex dot product \(\zeta \cdot \zeta\). Every identity holds exactly over the field of rational numbers \(\mathbb{Q}\) and over finite fields \(\mathbb{F}_p\), demonstrating that relativistic electrodynamics and gauge curvature can be formulated completely through rational arithmetic without transcendental approximations.
