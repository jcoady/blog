Where Part 7 left off

Part 7 defined a minimal coupled model (compact U(1) links, electric variables, a charged complex scalar), proved that its kick–Cayley-drift–kick update preserves the Gauss function exactly, satisfies discrete charge continuity, commutes with gauge transformations and is reversible, and checked those identities in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb F_7\) and \(\mathbb F_{11}\). It stated plainly what was not established: that the scheme approximates Maxwell theory with matter. This post starts on that question. It reports floating-point consistency tests of the same scheme, and says what they do not show.

Scope

Tested here (floating point, \(32\times32\) lattice, \(m^2=1\)): unforced evolution with nonzero charge density and magnetic curvature starting from Gauss-law initial data; tracking of the Gauss function, total charge and energy; a forward–backward round trip; temporal convergence against a fine-step reference.

Not tested here: spatial convergence of the coupled system; a manufactured-solution test; wave dispersion of the coupled system; a weak-field comparison with Part 4’s linear scheme or Part 1’s compact dynamics; any fermionic matter; any comparison with an independent Maxwell–matter solver. Nothing below establishes that the scheme approximates Maxwell theory with matter in the continuum limit, and nothing is derived from the rotor anchor.

Three levels of evidence should be kept apart. Algebraic correctness (identities such as gauge covariance, continuity and constraint preservation) is established in Part 7. Numerical consistency (agreement with a specified solution as the discretisation is refined) is what this post begins to measure. Physical validation (that the model’s unforced dynamics are the intended Maxwell–matter dynamics) is not established by either.

Setup

The scheme is exactly that of Part 7, vectorised (validate_coupled_scheme.py). As a first check, the vectorised step agrees with the reference implementation of Part 7’s script (the one used for the exact tests, run with floating-point numbers) to \(2.2\times10^{-16}\) on a random \(3\times3\) state.

Initial data. On the \(32\times32\) torus (\(k=2\pi/L\)): \[ \phi=0.5\cos(ki)\cos(kj)(1+0.3i)+0.05i\sin(kj),\qquad \pi=0.3i\sin(ki)\cos(kj)+0.02\cos(kj), \] link angles \(\theta_x=0.1\sin(kj)\), \(\theta_y=0.15\cos(ki)\) (so the plaquette angles are non-zero), and an electric field built to satisfy Gauss’s law: the longitudinal part solves the lattice Poisson equation \(\Delta\chi=\rho\) with \(E_\mu=-(\chi(x+\hat\mu)-\chi(x))\) by FFT, plus divergence-free transverse parts \(0.05\sin(kj)\) and \(0.04\cos(ki)\).

A Poisson equation on a torus is solvable only if the total charge vanishes. The chosen data have zero total charge (up to floating-point round-off): each of the three terms in \(\rho=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(\pi\phi)\) sums to zero over the lattice by the symmetry of the sine and cosine factors. The code projects the right-hand side onto the zero-mean subspace for compatibility, and asserts that the mean it removes is negligible. This projection does not modify the scalar fields or their charge density, so it is a safeguard against future changes to the initial data rather than an adjustment of the present ones.

The initial \(\max|G|\) is \(7.5\times10^{-16}\), the maximum \(|\rho|\) is \(0.154\), the maximum plaquette angle is \(0.049\), and \(H_0=115.05\). The evolution is unforced: no source or forcing is added after initialisation.

Results

Constraints, conservation and reversibility (evolution to \(T=20\); the round trip runs forward for \(T\) and then back with \(-h\)):

\(h\) steps \(\max\lvert G\rvert\) \(\max\lvert Q-Q_0\rvert\) \(\max\lvert H/H_0-1\rvert\) round-trip error
0.1 200 \(1.1\times10^{-15}\) \(6.2\times10^{-15}\) \(1.51\times10^{-3}\) \(4.6\times10^{-15}\)
0.05 400 \(1.4\times10^{-15}\) \(5.8\times10^{-15}\) \(3.78\times10^{-4}\) \(4.3\times10^{-15}\)
0.025 800 \(1.8\times10^{-15}\) \(6.7\times10^{-15}\) \(9.47\times10^{-5}\) \(5.8\times10^{-15}\)

Here \(Q=\sum_x\rho(x)\) is the total charge and \(G\) is the Gauss function of Part 7.

Temporal convergence (lattice fixed, \(T=2\), reference step \(h_{\rm ref}=0.003125\), maximum-norm error over all fields):

\(h\) error ratio to previous
0.2 \(4.49\times10^{-3}\)
0.1 \(1.12\times10^{-3}\) 4.02
0.05 \(2.79\times10^{-4}\) 4.02
0.025 \(6.88\times10^{-5}\) 4.05

The ratios sit slightly above 4, and the excess grows as \(h\) approaches \(h_{\rm ref}\). This is expected: the reference solution is itself in error. If the error of a run is \(C h^2\) and the reference carries \(C h_{\rm ref}^2\), the measured quantity is \(C(h^2-h_{\rm ref}^2)\), which would give ratios of about \(4.00\), \(4.01\) and \(4.05\) for the three pairs. That accounts for the last ratio and most of the drift in the others. The measured values \(4.02,\,4.02,\,4.05\) are consistent with this, but the test does not separate the two effects cleanly, and it assumes a pure \(h^2\) error term. A reference at a much smaller step, or self-convergence between three step sizes, would remove the issue (see below).

What these results show

  • The algebra holds in floating point. The Gauss function stays at round-off level (\(\sim10^{-15}\)) for hundreds of steps with nonzero charge and curvature, independent of \(h\), and the total charge is constant to round-off. These are the numerical counterparts of Proposition 22 of Part 7. They confirm the implementation; they are not independent evidence beyond the proof.
  • The scheme is reversible to round-off on nontrivial coupled data (round-trip error \(\sim5\times10^{-15}\) after \(T=20\)).
  • The observed energy deviation scales as \(h^2\) over \(T=20\). The maximum relative deviations are \(1.51\times10^{-3}\), \(3.78\times10^{-4}\) and \(9.47\times10^{-5}\) for \(h=0.1,\,0.05,\,0.025\), with a ratio of \(4.0\) each time. This is consistent with the behaviour expected from a symmetric second-order splitting. Although the Cayley drift admits a modified-kinetic-energy interpretation (Part 7), these tests do not establish that the complete update conserves a modified Hamiltonian. Longer-time behaviour, and a candidate modified energy, remain to be investigated.
  • The temporal error is second order. The ratios \(4.02,\,4.02,\,4.05\) are consistent with \(O(h^2)\) for the combined update at fixed lattice, allowing for the finite-reference effect described above.

What they do not show

  • Spatial accuracy. The lattice spacing was not varied. Whether the scheme converges to a continuum limit as the spacing shrinks, and at what order, is not tested. A fair test needs the Hamiltonian written with an explicit spacing \(\varepsilon\), which I expect to be \(H=\tfrac12\sum E^2+\varepsilon^{-2}\sum(1-\cos\theta_P)+\sum\pi\pi^*+\varepsilon^{-2}\sum|U\phi'-\phi|^2+m^2\sum|\phi|^2\) for fields scaled as \(\theta=\varepsilon A\), \(E=\varepsilon E_{\rm c}\), \(\phi=\varepsilon\Phi\) (derived by matching to the continuum Hamiltonian, not yet implemented or tested).
  • That the dynamics are Maxwell–matter dynamics. Second-order consistency in time of one discretisation, and conservation properties, do not establish agreement with the intended continuum equations. No independent solver or exact coupled solution was used.
  • Anything about the rotor. The tests concern the lattice model. They say nothing about whether the model follows from the rotor anchor.

What a fuller validation needs

  1. Spatial and combined convergence with the explicit-spacing Hamiltonian, using self-convergence on nested grids or a manufactured solution, with the time step small enough that the temporal error does not mask the spatial one. For the temporal study, comparing three step sizes (\(h\), \(h/2\), \(h/4\)) avoids dependence on a reference solution’s own error.
  2. A manufactured-solution test with a spatially varying magnetic field, with forcing chosen to make a smooth coupled solution exact. This tests consistency of all terms. It does not by itself show that the unforced equations are right, which is why the unforced tests above matter.
  3. Wave dispersion of the coupled system, and a weak-field comparison with Part 4’s linear scheme and Part 1’s compact dynamics.
  4. Longer-time energy behaviour, against a conserved modified energy if one can be identified.
  5. Exact-arithmetic regression. The exact suite of Part 7 should be kept as a separate regression test for any code change, since it checks the identities that floating-point residuals can only bound.

If a manufactured-solution test and its convergence table already exist from another source, they should be added with their code so that they can be reproduced; I have not reproduced them here, so none of their numbers appear in this post.

Reproducing

The two files used for this post are available alongside it:

  • code/validate_coupled_scheme.py: the validation script listed below.
  • code/minimal_action_model.py: the reference implementation from Part 7, needed for the cross-check.

Place both files in the same directory to run the script.

"""
Numerical validation of the coupled scalar-gauge kick-drift-kick scheme of Part 7 (floating point, vectorised).

  (0) cross-check: the vectorised step equals the reference implementation of minimal_action_model.py (L = 3, floats)
  (1) unforced evolution with nonzero charge and magnetic curvature, Gauss-law initial data:
        max |G| over time, total charge, energy deviation (and its scaling with h)
  (2) forward-backward round trip on nontrivial coupled data
  (3) temporal convergence against a fine-step reference (spatial lattice fixed)
NOT tested here: spatial convergence of the coupled system, manufactured solutions, dispersion of the coupled system.
Needs numpy.  Tested with Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.4.4.
"""
import math
import random
import numpy as np


def plaq_U(U): return U[0] * np.roll(U[1], -1, axis=0) * np.conj(np.roll(U[0], -1, axis=1)) * np.conj(U[1])

def kick(th, E, phi, pi, h, m2):
    U = np.exp(1j * th); xi = plaq_U(U).imag; dE = np.empty_like(E)
    dE[0] = xi - np.roll(xi, 1, axis=1); dE[1] = -(xi - np.roll(xi, 1, axis=0))
    dpi = m2 * np.conj(phi)
    for mu in (0, 1):
        z = U[mu] * np.roll(phi, -1, axis=mu) * np.conj(phi); dE[mu] += 2 * z.imag
        dpi = dpi + 2 * np.conj(phi) - np.roll(U[mu], 1, axis=mu) * np.conj(np.roll(phi, 1, axis=mu)) - np.conj(U[mu]) * np.conj(np.roll(phi, -1, axis=mu))
    return th, E - h * dE, phi, pi - h * dpi

def drift(th, E, phi, pi, h): return th + 2 * np.arctan(h * E / 2), E, phi + h * np.conj(pi), pi

def step(s, h, m2):
    s = kick(*s, h / 2, m2); s = drift(*s, h); return kick(*s, h / 2, m2)

def gauss(E, phi, pi):
    return sum(E[mu] - np.roll(E[mu], 1, axis=mu) for mu in (0, 1)) - 2 * (pi * phi).imag

def energy(s, m2):
    th, E, phi, pi = s; U = np.exp(1j * th); H = 0.5 * np.sum(E ** 2) + np.sum(1 - plaq_U(U).real) + np.sum(abs(pi) ** 2) + m2 * np.sum(abs(phi) ** 2)
    for mu in (0, 1): H += np.sum(abs(U[mu] * np.roll(phi, -1, axis=mu) - phi) ** 2)
    return H

def initial_state(L, A=0.5, B=0.3):
    i, j = np.meshgrid(np.arange(L), np.arange(L), indexing='ij'); k = 2 * np.pi / L
    phi = A * np.cos(k * i) * np.cos(k * j) * (1 + 0.3j) + 0.05j * np.sin(k * j)
    pi = 1j * B * np.sin(k * i) * np.cos(k * j) + 0.02 * np.cos(k * j)
    th = np.array([0.1 * np.sin(k * j), 0.15 * np.cos(k * i)])
    rho = -2 * (pi * phi).imag
    rho_mean = rho.mean()
    # The Poisson problem on a torus needs zero total charge. These data have it (by symmetry);
    # the assert guards against future changes to the initial data silently breaking that.
    assert abs(rho_mean) < 1e-12, "Initial data are not charge-neutral."
    rho_poisson = rho - rho_mean            # projection for solvability only; phi and pi are not modified
    # solve  lap(chi) = rho  with  E_mu = -(chi(x+mu) - chi(x))  so that  div E = -rho
    kk = np.fft.fftfreq(L) * 2 * np.pi; K0, K1 = np.meshgrid(kk, kk, indexing='ij')
    lam = 2 * (np.cos(K0) - 1) + 2 * (np.cos(K1) - 1); lam[0, 0] = 1.0
    chi = np.real(np.fft.ifft2(np.fft.fft2(rho_poisson) / lam))
    E = np.array([-(np.roll(chi, -1, axis=0) - chi), -(np.roll(chi, -1, axis=1) - chi)])
    E[0] += 0.05 * np.sin(k * j); E[1] += 0.04 * np.cos(k * i)         # divergence-free (transverse) parts
    return th, E, phi, pi

def cross_check():
    import minimal_action_model as mam
    rng = random.Random(1); Lc = mam.L; m2 = 1.0; h = 0.13
    th = np.array([[[rng.uniform(-1, 1) for _ in range(Lc)] for _ in range(Lc)] for _ in range(2)]); E = np.array([[[rng.uniform(-1, 1) for _ in range(Lc)] for _ in range(Lc)] for _ in range(2)])
    phi = np.array([[complex(rng.uniform(-1, 1), rng.uniform(-1, 1)) for _ in range(Lc)] for _ in range(Lc)]); pi = np.array([[complex(rng.uniform(-1, 1), rng.uniform(-1, 1)) for _ in range(Lc)] for _ in range(Lc)])
    Rf = mam.Ring(float)
    a = {((i, j), mu): -math.tan(th[mu, i, j] / 2) for i in range(Lc) for j in range(Lc) for mu in (0, 1)}
    st = mam.State(a, {((i, j), mu): E[mu, i, j] for i in range(Lc) for j in range(Lc) for mu in (0, 1)},
                   {(i, j): (phi[i, j].real, phi[i, j].imag) for i in range(Lc) for j in range(Lc)}, {(i, j): (pi[i, j].real, pi[i, j].imag) for i in range(Lc) for j in range(Lc)})
    ref = mam.step(Rf, st, h, m2); vec = step((th, E, phi, pi), h, m2); err = 0.0
    for i in range(Lc):
        for j in range(Lc):
            for mu in (0, 1):
                err = max(err, abs(-2 * math.atan(ref.a[((i, j), mu)]) - vec[0][mu, i, j]), abs(ref.E[((i, j), mu)] - vec[1][mu, i, j]))
            err = max(err, abs(complex(*ref.phi[(i, j)]) - vec[2][i, j]), abs(complex(*ref.pi[(i, j)]) - vec[3][i, j]))
    return err

def main():
    m2 = 1.0; L = 32
    print(f"(0) vectorised step versus the reference implementation (L = 3, random state): max difference {cross_check():.2e}")
    s0 = initial_state(L); G0 = gauss(s0[1], s0[2], s0[3]); rho0 = -2 * (s0[3] * s0[2]).imag
    print(f"(1) L = {L}, m^2 = 1: initial max|G| = {abs(G0).max():.2e}, max|rho| = {abs(rho0).max():.3f}, max magnetic plaquette angle = {abs(np.angle(plaq_U(np.exp(1j * s0[0])))).max():.3f}, H0 = {energy(s0, m2):.5f}")
    T = 20.0
    print("    unforced evolution to T = 20 (nonzero charge density and magnetic curvature):")
    print("      h        steps   max|G|      max|total charge - initial|   max|H/H0 - 1|    round trip error (forward T then back)")
    for h in (0.1, 0.05, 0.025):
        n = int(round(T / h)); s = s0; Gm = 0.0; Qm = 0.0; Hm = 0.0; H0 = energy(s0, m2); Q0 = np.sum(-2 * (s0[3] * s0[2]).imag)
        for _ in range(n):
            s = step(s, h, m2)
            Gm = max(Gm, abs(gauss(s[1], s[2], s[3])).max()); Qm = max(Qm, abs(np.sum(-2 * (s[3] * s[2]).imag) - Q0)); Hm = max(Hm, abs(energy(s, m2) / H0 - 1))
        back = s
        for _ in range(n): back = step(back, -h, m2)
        rt = max(abs(back[0] - s0[0]).max(), abs(back[1] - s0[1]).max(), abs(back[2] - s0[2]).max(), abs(back[3] - s0[3]).max())
        print(f"      {h:<7} {n:5d}   {Gm:.2e}   {Qm:.2e}                     {Hm:.3e}        {rt:.2e}")
    print("(3) temporal convergence at T = 2 (lattice fixed), reference step h = 0.003125; max-norm error over all fields:")
    def run(h, T=2.0):
        s = s0
        for _ in range(int(round(T / h))): s = step(s, h, m2)
        return s
    ref = run(0.003125); prev = None
    for h in (0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025):
        s = run(h); err = max(abs(s[0] - ref[0]).max(), abs(s[1] - ref[1]).max(), abs(s[2] - ref[2]).max(), abs(s[3] - ref[3]).max())
        print(f"      h = {h:<6}: error {err:.3e}" + ("" if prev is None else f"   (ratio to previous {prev / err:.2f})")); prev = err

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Run with python3 validate_coupled_scheme.py. Tested with Python 3.12.3 and NumPy 2.4.4.
