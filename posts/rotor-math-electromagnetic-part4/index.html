Where Part 3 left off

Part 1 identified the anchor \(\zeta=\kappa+i\rho\) with the Riemann–Silberstein vector \(\mathbf E+i\mathbf B\). Part 2 and Part 3 built exact Cayley lattice gauge identities, a photon-mass obstruction and a Wilson–Dirac operator. They also stated what is missing: the link variables \(U_\mu(x)\) are inputs, and the field dynamics are not derived from the rotor.

This post takes four steps toward closing that gap. Two succeed, one is a negative result recorded so it is not repeated, and one is standard numerics seen through the paper’s Cayley cap.

Scope
  • Calculation 15 (links from curvature): an exact algebraic theorem, with proof and test. It is the discrete Poincaré lemma for Cayley-valued face data on a box.
  • Calculation 16 (a group-valued Yee update): a negative result. It does not give Maxwell dynamics.
  • Calculation 17 (the free equation from boost covariance): a derivation, under stated assumptions, that the rotor action forces \(\partial_t\zeta=\pm i\,\nabla\times\zeta\). It is in the continuum and uses polynomial solutions; no lattice is Lorentz invariant.
  • Calculation 18 (a Cayley time step): the Crank–Nicolson method for the linear lattice Maxwell system, recognised as the paper’s cap applied to an antisymmetric generator.

Not claimed: a derivation of dynamics from projective geometry alone (Calculation 17 assumes first-order \(K\)-linear structure and the action of the rotor group on \(\zeta\)); Lorentz covariance on a lattice; sources or matter coupling; the behaviour of Calculation 18 over \(\mathbb F_p\); any link between \(\zeta\) and the face parameters beyond the regularisation choice stated in Calculation 15.

Proofs and checks. The statements are proved by the derivations in the text. The tables are computational verification in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb F_p\) (a separate field class per prime), with the exceptions marked as floating point. The scripts are at the end.

Calculation 15: links from curvature

Let \(F\) satisfy the standing hypotheses (\(\operatorname{char}F\neq2\), \(-1\notin(F^\times)^2\)), so \((\mathbb P^1(F),\oplus)\) with \(a\oplus b=(a+b)/(1-ab)\) is the norm-one phase group of Part 2. On the box \([0,N]^3\) with links \(a_x,a_y,a_z\), the plaquette parameters are \[ P_{xy}=a_x(i,j,k)\oplus a_y(i{+}1,j,k)\ominus a_x(i,j{+}1,k)\ominus a_y(i,j,k), \] and cyclically for \(P_{yz}\), \(P_{zx}\). Face data live on the ranges \(a^{xy}(i,j,k)\) with \(i,j<N\), \(k\le N\); \(a^{yz}\) with \(i\le N\), \(j,k<N\); \(a^{zx}\) with \(j\le N\), \(i,k<N\).

The cube identity. For every cube \((i,j,k)\in[0,N)^3\), \[ \bigl[a^{xy}(i,j,k{+}1)\ominus a^{xy}(i,j,k)\bigr]\oplus\bigl[a^{yz}(i{+}1,j,k)\ominus a^{yz}(i,j,k)\bigr]\oplus\bigl[a^{zx}(i,j{+}1,k)\ominus a^{zx}(i,j,k)\bigr]=0.\tag{$\star$} \] This involves all six faces of the cube. The three-face statement \(a^{xy}\oplus a^{yz}\oplus a^{zx}=0\) is not the identity and is false for faces coming from links.

Proposition 15. (i) Faces computed from links satisfy (\(\star\)). (ii) Conversely, if face data satisfy (\(\star\)) on every cube, the links defined by the axial-gauge recursion \[ a_x\equiv0,\quad a_y(i{+}1)=a_y(i)\oplus P_{xy}(i),\quad a_z(i{+}1)=a_z(i)\ominus P_{zx}(i), \] with \(a_z(0,j,k)=0\) and \(a_y(0,j,k{+}1)=a_y(0,j,k)\ominus P_{yz}(0,j,k)\) on the slice \(i=0\), have plaquettes equal to the data.

Proof. (i) Each edge of a cube lies in two of its faces with opposite orientations, so in the abelian group the six oriented faces sum to zero; this is (\(\star\)). (ii) With \(a_x=0\) one has \(P_{xy}=a_y(i{+}1)\ominus a_y(i)\) and \(P_{zx}=a_z(i)\ominus a_z(i{+}1)\), so the recursion reproduces the \(xy\) and \(zx\) faces at every \(i\) by construction. On \(i=0\), \(a_z=0\) and the definition of \(a_y(0,\cdot,\cdot)\) reproduce \(P_{yz}(0,j,k)\). For the step \(i\to i{+}1\), the reconstructed \(yz\) face satisfies \[ \tilde P_{yz}(i{+}1)=\tilde P_{yz}(i)\ominus\bigl[P_{xy}(i,j,k{+}1)\ominus P_{xy}(i,j,k)\bigr]\ominus\bigl[P_{zx}(i,j{+}1,k)\ominus P_{zx}(i,j,k)\bigr], \] and (\(\star\)) says the same relation holds for the data. Induction on \(i\) finishes the proof. \(\square\)

On the simply connected box the links are determined by the faces up to gauge (standard; not tested here). On a torus there are further conditions: the \(\oplus\)-sum of all plaquettes of each plane must vanish (Stokes), and the non-contractible holonomies are free data.

Relation to earlier posts. Calculation 12(f) of Part 2 is the special case of constant faces. The recursion above replaces the “spanning tree, solve the fourth edge” description, which does not match what a face-by-face reconstruction does in practice.

What this does and does not give

  • Gives: an exact, rational (or modular) map from Cayley-valued curvature to links on a box, with the necessary and sufficient condition (\(\star\)), and a recovery of the earlier uniform-field case.
  • Does not give: the face data from the rotor. Part 1’s flux identity gives the flux \(\Phi_F\) of a given field through a plane. To turn it into a Cayley face parameter one must choose a regularisation, for example \(a_f:=-\tfrac12e\Phi_F(f)\). The exact relation \(a_f=-\tan(e\Phi_F/2)\) is not rational in \(\Phi_F\), so this is a choice and not a consequence. For non-constant fields the cube identity is a condition on the \(a_f\) under \(\oplus\), not on the linearised flux (Part 2, Calculation 12(f)).

Calculation 16: a group-valued Yee update (negative result)

A natural attempt at lattice dynamics keeps every variable group-valued and updates by group translations, with \(E\) on edges and \(B\) on plaquettes (\(2{+}1\) dimensions, integer step \(n\)): \[ B\leftarrow B\ominus n\,\mathrm{curl}\,E,\qquad E\leftarrow E\oplus n\,\mathrm{curl}^{T}B,\qquad B\leftarrow B\ominus n\,\mathrm{curl}\,E . \] (The two \(B\) updates are the “half-steps” written with the full \(n\).) I record what was found because it is easy to be misled by the checks that pass.

Finding Result
Reversibility (\(n=-1\) undoes \(n=1\)) and the Gauss law over 3 steps True over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb F_7\)
The same checks with plain addition mod 7 (no Cayley structure) True
Total flux of the random initial \(B\) on the torus (must be \(0\) for links to exist) \(-44/23\) over \(\mathbb Q\); \(2\) over \(\mathbb F_7\)
Wave speed of the real-valued linear scheme with the same update (lowest frequency over \(k\), floating point) \(1.4102\)
Same, with the standard Strang step (\(B\) advanced by \(\tfrac n2\) twice) \(0.9972\)
Max \(\lvert\text{eigenvalue}\rvert\) per step, update as above, \(dt=0.5,\,0.7,\,0.9,\,1.0\) \(1.000,\ 5.663,\ 10.868,\ 13.928\)
Same, standard Strang step \(1.000,\ 1.000,\ 4.244,\ 5.828\)

What follows:

  1. The checks are tautological. Reversibility and the Gauss law hold for any abelian group, as the plain-addition run shows. They say nothing about the Cayley structure, the rotor, or Maxwell’s equations.
  2. It is not a Maxwell solver. The written update advances \(B\) twice as fast as \(E\), so the wave speed is \(\sqrt2\), not 1. The step \(n\) must be an integer (it is an \(n\)-fold \(\oplus\)), so there is no small step, and the corresponding real scheme is unstable at \(n=1\) even with the standard half-steps (it is stable only for \(dt\lesssim0.7\)). In the angle \(\theta=2\arctan a\) the update is the linear Yee scheme on circle-valued fields, with none of the \(\sin\theta_P\) force of the Wilson dynamics of Calculation 10 of Part 1, and with a compact \(E\) where Calculation 10 has a real-valued momentum.
  3. The initial data is not realizable. Random face data on the torus has non-zero total flux, so no links exist (Calculation 15). “The evolved faces stay in the image of the reconstruction map” is therefore untested, and in \(2{+}1\) dimensions the cube identity is vacuous anyway.

The lesson is that an exact, reversible, Gauss-preserving update is not evidence of Maxwell dynamics. Any proposed rule must be tested against a Maxwell law (wave speed, dispersion, stability).

Calculation 17: the free equation from boost covariance

Part 1 (Calculation 6) and the paper show that Lorentz transformations act on \(\zeta=\mathbf E+i\mathbf B\) by complex rotations; a real boost acts as a rotation by an imaginary angle. Take that action as input. What first-order equation for \(\zeta\) is compatible with it?

Assumptions. A first-order, linear, \(K\)-linear, constant-coefficient equation for \(\zeta:\mathbb R^{1+3}\to K^3\) (with \(K=F[i]\)), covariant under rotations, with \(\zeta\) acted on by boosts through the rotor action and no additional fields.

Step 1 (rotations). The derivative \(\partial_k\zeta_j\) decomposes under rotations into a scalar (the divergence), a vector (the curl) and a spin-2 part. Only the curl can equal \(\partial_t\zeta\), a vector. So the equation is \[ \partial_t\zeta=c\,\nabla\times\zeta,\qquad c\in K, \] and, since \(\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\zeta)=c\,\nabla\cdot\nabla\times\zeta=0\), the constraint \(\nabla\cdot\zeta=0\) is preserved. (This step is a representation-theory argument that I did not test computationally.)

Step 2 (boosts). Let \(L=\partial_t-c\nabla\times\) and let the boost along \(x\) act by \[ K\zeta=(x\partial_t+t\partial_x)\zeta+\mu\,e_x\times\zeta, \] where \(\mu\) is the spin coupling (an imaginary-angle rotation is \(\mu=\pm i\), up to sign conventions). Using \([\partial_t,K]=\partial_x\) and \(\nabla\times(e_x\times\zeta)=e_x(\nabla\cdot\zeta)-\partial_x\zeta\), one finds on solutions (\(\partial_t\zeta=c\nabla\times\zeta\), \(\nabla\cdot\zeta=0\)): \[ [L,K]\zeta=(1+c\mu)\,\partial_x\zeta-c(c-\mu)\;e_x\times(\nabla\times\zeta),\qquad \nabla\cdot(K\zeta)=(c-\mu)(\nabla\times\zeta)_x . \] For \(K\) to map solutions to solutions both must vanish. The second condition gives \(\mu=c\); the first then gives \(1+c^2=0\). Hence \[ \boxed{\;c^2=-1,\qquad \mu=c,\qquad\text{i.e. }\ \partial_t\zeta=\pm i\,\nabla\times\zeta\ .} \] The two signs correspond to the two orientation conventions (\(\zeta\) versus its conjugate, i.e. the automorphism \(i\mapsto-i\) of \(K\)).

Why \(K\) is needed. \(c^2=-1\) has no solution in \(F\), because \(-1\) is not a square there. So no first-order covariant equation exists for \(F\)-valued \(\zeta\); the quadratic extension of the paper is exactly what makes it solvable.

What this does and does not give

  • Gives: the free vacuum equation \(\partial_t\zeta=\pm i\nabla\times\zeta\), with \(\nabla\cdot\zeta=0\), as a consequence of the rotor action and first-order \(K\)-linear structure. This is the Riemann–Silberstein form of the free Maxwell equations; in two-spinor language it is the spin-1 case of the massless free-field equations [4].
  • Does not give: the transformation law of \(\zeta\) itself, which is input (Part 1, Calculation 6, and the paper); a derivation without the first-order and linearity assumptions; any source term. With a current, \(\partial_t\zeta=\mp i\nabla\times\zeta-J\) and \(\nabla\cdot\zeta=\rho\) would need the law by which \((\rho,J)\) transforms, which I have not tested.
  • Not a lattice statement. No regular lattice is Lorentz invariant. The covariance is in the continuum.

Calculation 18: a Cayley time step for the lattice curl

Take the linear lattice Maxwell system on a periodic torus with \(E\) on edges, \(B\) on plaquettes and \(C\) the lattice curl (edges to faces): \[ \frac d{dt}\begin{pmatrix}E\\B\end{pmatrix}=M\begin{pmatrix}E\\B\end{pmatrix},\qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}0&C^T\\-C&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad M^T=-M . \] Define the Cayley step \[ Q(dt)=\bigl(I-\tfrac{dt}2M\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(I+\tfrac{dt}2M\bigr), \] the paper’s cap \(\mathrm{ca}(A)=(I+A)(I-A)^{-1}\) at the antisymmetric \(A=\tfrac{dt}2M\). This is the Crank–Nicolson method [5]; the new point is only that it is the rational orthogonal cap.

Proposition 18. (i) \(Q^TQ=I\). (ii) With \(D\) the divergence of \(E\) (extended by zero on \(B\)), \(DQ=D\). (iii) \(Q(-dt)=Q(dt)^{-1}\), and \(Q\) is defined whenever \(I-\tfrac{dt}2M\) is invertible.

Proof. (i) \((I+A)\) and \((I-A)\) commute, and \(A^T=-A\), so \(Q^TQ=(I-A)(I+A)^{-1}(I-A)^{-1}(I+A)=I\). (ii) \(DM=(0\;\;DC^T)=0\) because the divergence of a curl vanishes, so \(DA=0\); then \(D(I-A)=D\) gives \(D=D(I-A)^{-1}\), and \(DQ=D(I+A)=D\). (iii) \(Q(-dt)=(I+A)^{-1}(I-A)=Q(dt)^{-1}\). \(\square\)

What this does and does not give

  • Gives: a rational time step of arbitrary rational \(dt\), with exact energy conservation, exact Gauss preservation and exact reversibility, second-order accurate and without a CFL limit. It repairs the three failures of Calculation 16 for the linear free field.
  • Remark (not tested): in the additive potential variables \((a,E)\) with \(B=Ca\) and \(\dot a=-E\), the same step is the implicit midpoint rule for \(H=\tfrac12E^2+\tfrac12\lvert Ca\rvert^2\), which is symplectic for quadratic \(H\) (a standard fact). The flux is then realizable by construction, with no reconstruction map in the loop.
  • Does not give: the compact Wilson dynamics (Calculation 10 of Part 1 remains the integrator there); behaviour over \(\mathbb F_p\), where \(Q^TQ=I\) is an identity but invertibility of \(I-\tfrac{dt}2M\) must be checked and there is no energy; sources; Lorentz covariance.

Next directions

  1. Lattice-to-continuum diagram. Compare the Calculation 18 step with the equation of Calculation 17 on smooth data, and measure the convergence rate.
  2. Weak-field agreement. Check that Calculation 10’s compact dynamics approaches Calculation 18 at weak field.
  3. Sources. Determine the transformation law of \((\rho,J)\) and couple the Wilson–Dirac current of Part 3, so the Gauss law becomes the Noether constraint of a combined action.
  4. Finite fields. Determine the domain of Calculation 18 over \(\mathbb F_p\).
  5. Torus. State the holonomy conditions of Calculation 15 on a torus.

Reproducing Calculations 15 and 16

"""
Calculations 15 and 16 (Part 4).

  15. Reconstruction of Cayley link parameters from Cayley face parameters on an open 3D box.
      (a) the cube identity has SIX faces (three differences); the three-face statement a_xy (+) a_yz (+) a_zx = 0 is false
      (b) axial-gauge reconstruction recovers every face exactly when the cube identity holds (Q, F_7, F_11)
      (c) face data violating the cube identity is not recovered
      (d) on a torus the total flux of each plane must vanish (Stokes); random face data violates it
  16. A group-valued Yee update  B <- B (-) n curl E,  E <- E (+) n curl B,  B <- B (-) n curl E  (integer n):  a negative result
      (a) reversibility and the Gauss law hold equally for plain addition mod 7: the checks do not test the Cayley structure
      (b) the real-valued linear scheme with the same update: wave speed and stability versus dt

Needs numpy.  Runs in about a minute.
"""
import itertools
import random
from fractions import Fraction as Fr
import numpy as np


# ------------------------------------------------------------------ fields ---
def make_Fp(p):
    class FpP:
        P = p
        def __init__(s, v): s.v = int(v) % FpP.P
        def __add__(s, o): return FpP(s.v + _v(o))
        __radd__ = __add__
        def __sub__(s, o): return FpP(s.v - _v(o))
        def __rsub__(s, o): return FpP(_v(o) - s.v)
        def __neg__(s): return FpP(-s.v)
        def __mul__(s, o): return FpP(s.v * _v(o))
        __rmul__ = __mul__
        def __truediv__(s, o): return FpP(s.v * pow(_v(o), -1, FpP.P))
        def __rtruediv__(s, o): return FpP(_v(o) * pow(s.v, -1, FpP.P))
        def __eq__(s, o): return o is not None and s.v == _v(o) % FpP.P
        def __hash__(s): return hash(s.v)
        def __repr__(s): return str(s.v)
    return FpP

def _v(o): return o.v if hasattr(o, "v") else int(o)
def kmul(u, v): return (u[0] * v[0] - u[1] * v[1], u[0] * v[1] + u[1] * v[0])
def kpow(u, q, one, zero):
    r = (one, zero)
    for _ in range(q): r = kmul(r, u)
    return r

class Ring:
    """P^1(F) with the Cayley group law  a (+) b = (a + b)/(1 - ab); a = None means infinity."""
    def __init__(s, mk, p=None):
        s.mk, s.p, s.one, s.zero = mk, p, mk(1), mk(0)
    def phase(s, a):
        if a is None: return (-s.one, s.zero)
        d = s.one + a * a
        return ((s.one - a * a) / d, s.mk(-2) * a / d)
    def a_of(s, u): return None if u[0] == -s.one else -u[1] / (s.one + u[0])
    def oplus(s, a, b): return s.a_of(kmul(s.phase(a), s.phase(b)))
    def neg(s, a): return None if a is None else -a
    def ominus(s, a, b): return s.oplus(a, s.neg(b))
    def scale(s, n, a):                                  # n-fold (+), n an integer
        if n < 0: return s.neg(s.scale(-n, a))
        r = s.zero
        for _ in range(n): r = s.oplus(r, a)
        return r
    def eq(s, a, b): return (a is None and b is None) or (a is not None and b is not None and a == b)

class AddRing:
    """Z_p with plain addition: no Cayley structure at all (used to show that some checks are tautological)."""
    def __init__(s, p): s.p, s.zero = p, 0
    def oplus(s, a, b): return (a + b) % s.p
    def ominus(s, a, b): return (a - b) % s.p
    def neg(s, a): return (-a) % s.p
    def scale(s, n, a): return (n * a) % s.p
    def eq(s, a, b): return a == b

RQ = Ring(Fr)
def RF(p): return Ring(make_Fp(p), p)

def sampler(R, rng):
    if R is RQ: return lambda: None if rng.random() < 0.04 else Fr(rng.randint(-6, 6), rng.randint(1, 4))
    return lambda: None if rng.randrange(R.p + 1) == R.p else R.mk(rng.randrange(R.p))
def sampler_finite(R, rng):                              # no infinity
    if R is RQ: return lambda: Fr(rng.randint(-6, 6), rng.randint(1, 4))
    return lambda: R.mk(rng.randrange(R.p))

#  15.  RECONSTRUCTION OF LINKS FROM FACES (open 3D box)

def make_links(N, rnd): return {(i, j, k, m): rnd() for i in range(N + 1) for j in range(N + 1) for k in range(N + 1) for m in range(3)}
def Pxy(R, a, i, j, k): return R.oplus(R.oplus(R.oplus(a[(i, j, k, 0)], a[(i + 1, j, k, 1)]), R.neg(a[(i, j + 1, k, 0)])), R.neg(a[(i, j, k, 1)]))
def Pyz(R, a, i, j, k): return R.oplus(R.oplus(R.oplus(a[(i, j, k, 1)], a[(i, j + 1, k, 2)]), R.neg(a[(i, j, k + 1, 1)])), R.neg(a[(i, j, k, 2)]))
def Pzx(R, a, i, j, k): return R.oplus(R.oplus(R.oplus(a[(i, j, k, 2)], a[(i, j, k + 1, 0)]), R.neg(a[(i + 1, j, k, 2)])), R.neg(a[(i, j, k, 0)]))

def face_ranges(N):
    return {'xy': [(i, j, k) for i in range(N) for j in range(N) for k in range(N + 1)],
            'yz': [(i, j, k) for i in range(N + 1) for j in range(N) for k in range(N)],
            'zx': [(i, j, k) for i in range(N) for j in range(N + 1) for k in range(N)]}

def faces_from_links(R, a, N):
    fr = face_ranges(N); f = {'xy': Pxy, 'yz': Pyz, 'zx': Pzx}
    return {n: {p: f[n](R, a, *p) for p in fr[n]} for n in fr}

def cube_residual(R, F, i, j, k):
    """[a_xy(k+1) (-) a_xy(k)] (+) [a_yz(i+1) (-) a_yz(i)] (+) [a_zx(j+1) (-) a_zx(j)]  (all six faces of the cube)."""
    d = lambda f, p, q: R.ominus(F[f][p], F[f][q])
    return R.oplus(R.oplus(d('xy', (i, j, k + 1), (i, j, k)), d('yz', (i + 1, j, k), (i, j, k))), d('zx', (i, j + 1, k), (i, j, k)))

def reconstruct(R, F, N):
    """Axial gauge a_x = 0.   a_y(i+1) = a_y(i) (+) P_xy(i);   a_z(i+1) = a_z(i) (-) P_zx(i);
    on the slice i = 0:  a_z = 0 and a_y(0,j,k+1) = a_y(0,j,k) (-) P_yz(0,j,k)."""
    a = {(i, j, k, m): R.zero for i in range(N + 1) for j in range(N + 1) for k in range(N + 1) for m in range(3)}
    for j in range(N):
        for k in range(N): a[(0, j, k + 1, 1)] = R.ominus(a[(0, j, k, 1)], F['yz'][(0, j, k)])
    for i in range(N):
        for j in range(N):
            for k in range(N + 1): a[(i + 1, j, k, 1)] = R.oplus(a[(i, j, k, 1)], F['xy'][(i, j, k)])
        for j in range(N + 1):
            for k in range(N): a[(i + 1, j, k, 2)] = R.ominus(a[(i, j, k, 2)], F['zx'][(i, j, k)])
    return a

def match(R, F, a, N):
    G2 = faces_from_links(R, a, N)
    return {n: (sum(R.eq(G2[n][p], F[n][p]) for p in F[n]), len(F[n])) for n in F}

def torus_flux_checks(R, L=4, seed=7):
    """On an L x L torus the (+)-sum of all plaquettes is zero for faces coming from links, but not for random faces."""
    rng = random.Random(seed); rnd = sampler(R, rng); fin = sampler_finite(R, rng)
    a = {(i, j, m): rnd() for i in range(L) for j in range(L) for m in range(2)}
    def plaq(i, j):
        return R.oplus(R.oplus(R.oplus(a[(i, j, 0)], a[((i + 1) % L, j, 1)]), R.neg(a[(i, (j + 1) % L, 0)])), R.neg(a[(i, j, 1)]))
    tot_links = R.zero
    for i in range(L):
        for j in range(L): tot_links = R.oplus(tot_links, plaq(i, j))
    tot_rand = R.zero
    for i in range(L):
        for j in range(L): tot_rand = R.oplus(tot_rand, fin())
    return tot_links, tot_rand

def curl_E(R, Ex, Ey, i, j, L):
    return R.oplus(R.oplus(R.oplus(Ex[(i, j)], Ey[((i + 1) % L, j)]), R.neg(Ex[(i, (j + 1) % L)])), R.neg(Ey[(i, j)]))
def curl_B_x(R, B, i, j, L): return R.ominus(B[(i, j)], B[(i, (j - 1) % L)])
def curl_B_y(R, B, i, j, L): return R.ominus(B[((i - 1) % L, j)], B[(i, j)])
def div_E(R, Ex, Ey, i, j, L):
    return R.oplus(R.oplus(R.ominus(Ex[(i, j)], Ex[((i - 1) % L, j)]), Ey[(i, j)]), R.neg(Ey[(i, (j - 1) % L)]))

def yee_step(R, Ex, Ey, B, n, L):
    """B advanced by n twice and E by n once (the 'half-steps' use the full n)."""
    P = list(itertools.product(range(L), repeat=2))
    B1 = {(i, j): R.ominus(B[(i, j)], R.scale(n, curl_E(R, Ex, Ey, i, j, L))) for i, j in P}
    Ex1 = {(i, j): R.oplus(Ex[(i, j)], R.scale(n, curl_B_x(R, B1, i, j, L))) for i, j in P}
    Ey1 = {(i, j): R.oplus(Ey[(i, j)], R.scale(n, curl_B_y(R, B1, i, j, L))) for i, j in P}
    B2 = {(i, j): R.ominus(B1[(i, j)], R.scale(n, curl_E(R, Ex1, Ey1, i, j, L))) for i, j in P}
    return Ex1, Ey1, B2

def yee_harness(R, rnd, L=4):
    P = list(itertools.product(range(L), repeat=2))
    Ex = {k: rnd() for k in P}; Ey = {k: rnd() for k in P}; B = {k: rnd() for k in P}
    s1 = yee_step(R, Ex, Ey, B, 1, L); s0 = yee_step(R, *s1, -1, L)
    rev = all(R.eq(s0[0][k], Ex[k]) and R.eq(s0[1][k], Ey[k]) and R.eq(s0[2][k], B[k]) for k in P)
    g0 = {k: div_E(R, Ex, Ey, *k, L) for k in P}
    n = (Ex, Ey, B)
    for _ in range(3): n = yee_step(R, *n, 1, L)
    gauss = all(R.eq(g0[k], div_E(R, n[0], n[1], *k, L)) for k in P)
    tot = R.zero
    for k in P: tot = R.oplus(tot, B[k])
    return rev, gauss, tot

def real_step_matrix(L, dt, half):
    """Real-valued linear Yee step.  half = False: B advanced by dt twice (as in the group-valued update);
    half = True: standard Strang step, B advanced by dt/2 twice."""
    n = 3 * L * L; M = np.zeros((n, n))
    def step(v):
        Ex = v[:L * L].reshape(L, L).copy(); Ey = v[L * L:2 * L * L].reshape(L, L).copy(); B = v[2 * L * L:].reshape(L, L).copy()
        curlE = lambda Ex, Ey: Ex + np.roll(Ey, -1, axis=0) - np.roll(Ex, -1, axis=1) - Ey
        h = dt / 2 if half else dt
        B = B - h * curlE(Ex, Ey)
        Ex = Ex + dt * (B - np.roll(B, 1, axis=1)); Ey = Ey + dt * (np.roll(B, 1, axis=0) - B)
        B = B - h * curlE(Ex, Ey)
        return np.concatenate([Ex.ravel(), Ey.ravel(), B.ravel()])
    for c in range(n):
        e = np.zeros(n); e[c] = 1; M[:, c] = step(e)
    return M

"""
Does boost covariance force the Riemann-Silberstein equation?   (exact arithmetic over Q(i), polynomial solutions)

Equation:   d_t zeta = c curl zeta,  div zeta = 0,     zeta in K^3 (K = Q(i)),  c in K.
Rotations act by  J_x zeta = (y d_z - z d_y) zeta + s e_x x zeta
Boosts    act by  K_x zeta = (x d_t + t d_x) zeta + mu e_x x zeta          (mu = spin coupling; imaginary-angle rotation <-> mu = +-i)
We find the polynomial solution space (total degree <= 3) for each c and test closure under K_x and J_x.
"""
import itertools, sympy as sp
t,x,y,z=sp.symbols('t x y z'); X=(x,y,z); I=sp.I
mons=[t**a*x**b*y**c_*z**d for a in range(4) for b in range(4) for c_ in range(4) for d in range(4) if a+b+c_+d<=3]
def curl(v): return [sp.diff(v[2],y)-sp.diff(v[1],z), sp.diff(v[0],z)-sp.diff(v[2],x), sp.diff(v[1],x)-sp.diff(v[0],y)]
def div(v): return sum(sp.diff(v[i],X[i]) for i in range(3))
def cross_ex(v): return [0, -v[2], v[1]]            # e_x x v
def solution_space(c):
    syms=[sp.Symbol(f'q{a}_{k}') for a in range(3) for k in range(len(mons))]
    zeta=[sum(syms[a*len(mons)+k]*mons[k] for k in range(len(mons))) for a in range(3)]
    eqs=[sp.diff(zeta[a],t)-c*curl(zeta)[a] for a in range(3)]+[div(zeta)]
    rows=[]
    for e in eqs:
        e=sp.expand(e)
        if e==0: continue
        for co in sp.Poly(e,t,x,y,z).coeffs(): rows.append(co)
    M,_=sp.linear_eq_to_matrix(rows,syms)
    ns=M.nullspace()
    return [[sp.expand(sum(v[a*len(mons)+k]*mons[k] for k in range(len(mons)))) for a in range(3)] for v in ns]
def residual_ok(zeta,c):
    r=[sp.expand(sp.diff(zeta[a],t)-c*curl(zeta)[a]) for a in range(3)]+[sp.expand(div(zeta))]
    return all(e==0 for e in r)
def boost(zeta,mu): 
    cr=cross_ex(zeta); return [sp.expand(x*sp.diff(zeta[a],t)+t*sp.diff(zeta[a],x)+mu*cr[a]) for a in range(3)]
def rot(zeta,s):
    cr=cross_ex(zeta); return [sp.expand(y*sp.diff(zeta[a],z)-z*sp.diff(zeta[a],y)+s*cr[a]) for a in range(3)]
vals={'-i':-I,'+i':I,'+1':sp.Integer(1),'-1':sp.Integer(-1),'+2':sp.Integer(2)}
for cn,c in vals.items():
    sols=solution_space(c)
    boosts=[mn for mn,mu in vals.items() if all(residual_ok(boost(s,mu),c) for s in sols)]
    rots=[s_ for s_ in (1,-1) if all(residual_ok(rot(s,s_),c) for s in sols)]
    print(f"c = {cn:>3}: polynomial solutions (deg<=3): {len(sols):3d};  boost-covariant spin couplings mu: {boosts if boosts else 'none'};  rotation-covariant s: {rots}")

"""
Cayley (Crank-Nicolson) time step for the lattice Maxwell system  d/dt (E, B) = M (E, B),  M = [[0, C^T], [-C, 0]]  (antisymmetric).
Q(dt) = (I - dt M/2)^-1 (I + dt M/2) is a RATIONAL ORTHOGONAL matrix (Wildberger's cap for SO(n)).
Exact checks over Q (sympy), accuracy against the matrix exponential (scipy).
2+1 D periodic torus, E on edges, B on plaquettes.
"""
import itertools, numpy as np, sympy as sp
from scipy.linalg import expm
def build(L):
    n=L*L; ix=lambda a,i,j: a*n+(i%L)*L+(j%L)
    C=sp.zeros(n,2*n)                      # (C E)(i,j) = Ex(i,j)+Ey(i+1,j)-Ex(i,j+1)-Ey(i,j)
    for i in range(L):
        for j in range(L):
            C[i*L+j,ix(0,i,j)]+=1; C[i*L+j,ix(1,i+1,j)]+=1; C[i*L+j,ix(0,i,j+1)]-=1; C[i*L+j,ix(1,i,j)]-=1
    D=sp.zeros(n,2*n)                      # div E (i,j) = Ex(i,j)-Ex(i-1,j)+Ey(i,j)-Ey(i,j-1)
    for i in range(L):
        for j in range(L):
            D[i*L+j,ix(0,i,j)]+=1; D[i*L+j,ix(0,i-1,j)]-=1; D[i*L+j,ix(1,i,j)]+=1; D[i*L+j,ix(1,i,j-1)]-=1
    M=sp.zeros(3*n,3*n); M[:2*n,2*n:]=C.T; M[2*n:,:2*n]=-C
    return M,D,C
L=4; M,D,C=build(L); N=M.shape[0]; Id=sp.eye(N)
print("M antisymmetric:", M.T==-M, ";  div(curl^T) = 0 (so Gauss is a conserved linear functional):", D*C.T==sp.zeros(D.shape[0],C.shape[0]))
for dt in (sp.Rational(1),sp.Rational(3,2),sp.Rational(7,3)):
    Q=(Id-dt*M/2).inv()*(Id+dt*M/2)
    orth=(Q.T*Q==Id)
    gauss=(D*Q[:, :]).extract(list(range(D.shape[0])),list(range(N)))  if False else None
    Dfull=sp.zeros(D.shape[0],N); Dfull[:,:2*L*L]=D
    gauss_ok=(Dfull*Q==Dfull)
    Qinv=(Id+dt*M/2).inv()*(Id-dt*M/2)            # Q(-dt)
    rev=(Q*Qinv==Id)
    print(f"dt = {dt}: Q^T Q = I exactly: {orth};  Gauss functional preserved exactly (D Q = D): {gauss_ok};  Q(-dt) = Q(dt)^-1: {rev};  max entry denominator bits: {max(sp.Rational(e).q.bit_length() for e in Q)}")
# accuracy against exp(M t) (floating point), unconditional stability
Mf=np.array(M.tolist(),dtype=float); T=1.0
for steps in (4,8,16,32):
    dt=T/steps; Qf=np.linalg.solve(np.eye(N)-dt*Mf/2,np.eye(N)+dt*Mf/2)
    err=np.max(abs(np.linalg.matrix_power(Qf,steps)-expm(Mf*T))); print(f"accuracy at T = 1: {steps:3d} steps, max error {err:.3e}")
rng=np.random.default_rng(1); v=rng.normal(size=N)
for dt in (0.5,5.0,50.0):
    Qf=np.linalg.solve(np.eye(N)-dt*Mf/2,np.eye(N)+dt*Mf/2); w=v.copy()
    for _ in range(200): w=Qf@w
    print(f"dt = {dt:5.1f}: norm after 200 steps / initial norm = {np.linalg.norm(w)/np.linalg.norm(v):.12f}")
