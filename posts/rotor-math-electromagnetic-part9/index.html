Where Part 8 left off

Part 8 tested the coupled scalar–gauge scheme at a fixed \(32\times32\) lattice: constraint preservation, reversibility, an energy deviation scaling as \(h^2\), and second-order temporal self-convergence. It left open whether the linearised dynamics have the intended dispersion relation, and it checked the order in time only against a fine-step reference. This post addresses both, and it does so mostly in exact arithmetic. The statements being tested (a one-step matrix, a trace, a Taylor coefficient) are algebraic, so they can be verified as identities over \(\mathbb Q(i)\) without any angle, exponential or arctangent. Floating point is used only for what exact arithmetic cannot give: long-time behaviour, amplitude effects and refinement over many steps.

Scope

Tested here: (1) in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), the one-step map of the actual scheme, linearised at the vacuum and acting on a plane wave, against a prediction written down beforehand; (2) in exact arithmetic with a formal step size \(h\), the Taylor coefficients of the full nonlinear step against the exact flow, at a random rational state; (3) in floating point, measured one-step traces, refinement in \(h\) and in the lattice size, long-time energy, constraint and charge behaviour, and the amplitude dependence of the frequency.

Not tested here: the nonlinear coupled system at finite amplitude under spatial refinement (this needs the explicit-spacing Hamiltonian of Part 8, still not implemented); a manufactured-solution test; any comparison with an independent Maxwell–matter solver, or with Part 4’s linear scheme or Part 1’s compact dynamics; fermionic matter. The target dispersion relation below is derived from this model’s own linearisation, so agreement with it shows that the code implements the model and that the model’s free limit is the expected lattice wave equation. It does not show that the model is derived from the rotor anchor, and it does not by itself show that the nonlinear coupled dynamics are Maxwell–matter dynamics.

What is predicted, stated before running

Linearise at the vacuum: unit links (\(\theta=0\)), \(E=0\), \(\phi=\pi=0\). For a lattice momentum \(k=(k_0,k_1)\) write \[ z_\mu=e^{ik_\mu},\qquad d_\mu=z_\mu-1,\qquad \Omega^2=|d_0|^2+|d_1|^2=\sum_\mu 4\sin^2\tfrac{k_\mu}{2}. \] If \(t_\mu=\tan(k_\mu/2)\) is rational, then \(z_\mu=(1+it_\mu)/(1-it_\mu)\) is a Gaussian rational and \(|d_\mu|^2=4t_\mu^2/(1+t_\mu^2)\) is rational: the lattice momentum enters only through rational spreads, never through an angle.

Gauge sector. A perturbation \(\theta_\mu=a_\mu e^{ik\cdot x}\) has plaquette angle \(B=-d_1a_0+d_0a_1\), and the equations of motion \(\dot\theta=E\), \(\dot E=-K\theta\) have \[ K=\begin{pmatrix}|d_1|^2&-\bar d_1d_0\\-\bar d_0d_1&|d_0|^2\end{pmatrix}=ww^\dagger,\qquad w=(\bar d_1,\,-\bar d_0). \] \(K\) has eigenvalue \(0\) on the pure-gauge direction \((d_0,d_1)\) and eigenvalue \(\Omega^2\) on \(w\), which is the only direction Gauss’s law allows for \(E\). There is no mass term.

Scalar sector. With \(\chi=\bar\pi\), the equations are \(\dot\phi=\chi\), \(\dot\chi=-\Lambda\phi\) with \(\Lambda=m^2+\Omega^2\).

One step. The kick–drift–kick update acts on \((\theta,E)\) in the gauge sector, and on \((\phi,\chi)\) in the scalar sector, by \[ \begin{pmatrix}1-\tfrac{h^2}{2}\lambda&h\\-h\lambda+\tfrac{h^3}{4}\lambda^2&1-\tfrac{h^2}{2}\lambda\end{pmatrix},\qquad \lambda=K\ (\text{matrix})\ \text{or}\ \Lambda . \] The Cayley drift does not change this: its Jacobian at \(E=0\) is exactly \(h\). The determinant is \(1\) and the trace is \(2-h^2\lambda\). On the physical gauge mode and on the scalar mode the eigenvalues are therefore \(e^{\pm i\omega h}\) with \[ \sin\frac{\omega h}{2}=\frac{h\Omega}{2}\quad(\text{gauge}),\qquad \sin\frac{\omega h}{2}=\frac{h\sqrt\Lambda}{2}\quad(\text{scalar}). \] In Wildberger’s language [4]: the half-step phase has spread \(h^2\Omega^2/4\) (respectively \(h^2\Lambda/4\)), a rational number, and the step is a rotation exactly when that spread lies in \([0,1]\).

Stability. The step is linearly stable on every lattice mode iff \(h^2\lambda_{\max}\le 4\). In two dimensions \(\Omega^2_{\max}=8\), so the gauge sector needs \(h\le 1/\sqrt2\) and the scalar sector needs \(h\le 2/\sqrt{m^2+8}\) (\(=2/3\) for \(m^2=1\)).

Consistency in \(h\). The step should agree with the exact flow through order \(h^2\) and differ at order \(h^3\). The Gauss function should be constant in \(h\) to all orders. The exact flow should conserve the displayed Hamiltonian, and the step’s energy change should first appear at order \(h^3\).

Continuum limit (this part needs angles). From \(4\sin^2(k/2)=k^2-k^4/12+\dots\) and \(\omega_h=\Omega(1+h^2\Omega^2/24+\dots)\), with \(h\) the time step in lattice units (a Courant number) and \(k=\kappa\varepsilon\) at fixed physical wavenumber \(\kappa\), the gauge wave should have relative frequency error \(-(1-h^2)k^2/24+O(\varepsilon^4)\), i.e. second order in \(\varepsilon\). For the scalar wave with \(m^2_{\rm lat}=(m\varepsilon)^2\) it should be \(\varepsilon^2\big[-\kappa^4/(24(m^2+\kappa^2))+h^2(m^2+\kappa^2)/24\big]\).

Check 1: exact plane-wave check over \(\mathbb Q(i)\)

Method (exact_dispersion_check.py). The scheme is rewritten as a rational map in complexified variables: the link \(U\) and its inverse \(\bar U=1/U\), the scalar \(\phi\) and \(\bar\phi\), the momentum \(\pi\) and \(\bar\pi\) are carried as independent quantities, and \(\mathrm{Im}\,z\) is written \((z-\bar z)/2i\). The drift is the exact rational map \(U\mapsto U\,(1+ihE/2)/(1-ihE/2)\), which equals \(e^{2i\arctan(hE/2)}\), so no transcendental function appears. Numbers are Gaussian rationals, and each field carries a truncated power series \(c_0+c_1\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon^2=0\) (dual numbers), so the \(\varepsilon\)-coefficient of the output is the exact derivative of the step. A perturbation \(\delta U_\mu=ia_\mu\,\mathrm{pw}(x)\) with \(\mathrm{pw}(x)=z_0^{\,i}z_1^{\,j}\) is applied on a \(9\times9\) patch; one step reaches two sites, so the output is read at interior sites at distance at least 3 from the edge, where the wrap-around of the patch cannot matter. At every interior site the output must be the same plane wave times a constant amplitude (this is asserted), and the six one-hot perturbations of the amplitude vector \((\theta_0,\theta_1,E_0,E_1,\phi,\chi)\) give the exact \(6\times6\) one-step matrix. It is compared entrywise with the matrix predicted above.

First, as in Part 8, the implementation is cross-checked: this exact step agrees with the floating-point step of Part 8 on a random \(3\times3\) state to \(2.5\times10^{-16}\).

Every entry is an exact rational. “Exact checks” means all of the following hold exactly in \(\mathbb Q(i)\):

  • the measured \(6\times6\) matrix equals the predicted one entrywise, which includes the statement that the gauge and scalar sectors do not mix at linear order;
  • \(\Omega^2\) computed from the matrix equals \(\sum_\mu4t_\mu^2/(1+t_\mu^2)\);
  • on the physical gauge subspace spanned by \((w,0)\) and \((0,w)\), the determinant is \(1\) and the trace is \(2-h^2\Omega^2\); on the full \(4\times4\) gauge block, \((S-1)^2\big(S^2-(2-h^2\Omega^2)S+1\big)=0\) (the factor \((S-1)^2\) is the Jordan block of the pure-gauge direction); on the scalar block, \(S^2-(2-h^2\Lambda)S+1=0\) with determinant \(1\).

As a negative control, comparing the first row’s measured matrix with predictions that use \(m^2=2\) or \(h=1/4\) fails, as it should, so the comparison can detect errors in the force terms and the step size.

The last row has \(h^2\Omega^2/4=4>1\): the same identities hold, but the trace is \(-14\) and the eigenvalues are real with modulus not 1, so that step is unstable. Check 3(F) measures that growth.

Check 2: consistency in \(h\), with a formal step size

Method. The same exact arithmetic is used with truncated power series in a formal step size \(h\) (order 3), at a random rational state on a \(3\times3\) torus with all fields nonzero and Gauss’s law not imposed (initial \(\max|G|=1.19\)). The step is evaluated with \(h\) formal. The exact flow of the continuous equations (\(\dot U=iUE\), \(\dot E=-F_E\), \(\dot\phi=\bar\pi\), \(\dot\pi=-F_\pi\), and their conjugates, with \(F_E\) and \(F_\pi\) the forces used in the kick) is obtained by Picard iteration in the same series arithmetic. Everything is exact.

max \(\lvert\)coefficient of (step − exact flow)\(\rvert\), all fields 0 0 0 2.689
max \(\lvert\)coefficient of \(G(\text{step})-G(x_0)\rvert\) 0 0 0 0
coefficient of \(H(\text{exact flow})-H(x_0)\) 0 0 0 0
coefficient of \(H(\text{step})-H(x_0)\) 0 0 0 19.574
  • The step is second-order consistent. It agrees with the exact flow through \(h^2\) exactly, and the first discrepancy is at \(h^3\). That is a local error \(O(h^3)\), which together with stability is the standard route to global second order. This is an exact, finite check, replacing the fine-step reference of Part 8, whose own error had to be argued about.
  • The Gauss function is preserved as a series identity, at a state where it is far from zero. This is Part 7’s proposition again, now checked to order \(h^3\) without round-off.
  • The continuous equations in the code are the Hamiltonian equations of the displayed \(H\), to the order checked: \(H\) is constant along the exact flow through \(h^3\). This checks every factor of 2 in the force terms against the energy.
  • The step’s energy change is first nonzero at \(h^3\), consistent with the \(h^2\) scaling of the bounded deviation seen in Part 8 (a local error \(O(h^3)\) per step, with bounded accumulation, as observed).

The limits: one random state, one small torus, orders through \(h^3\) only. The series check says nothing about spatial resolution.

Check 3: floating-point long-time runs

All of this section is floating point (long_time_runs.py). For a mode with eigenvalues \(e^{\pm i\omega h}\), any linear functional \(s_n\) of the state at step \(n\) satisfies \(s_{n+1}+s_{n-1}=2\cos(\omega h)\,s_n\). So \(\cos(\omega h)\), which is half the trace of the one-step map on the mode, is estimated by least squares from a recorded series. This is a direct measurement of the quantity predicted in Check 1.

The measured trace matches \(1-h^2\Omega^2/2\) for both sectors, and the frequency error relative to the continuum \(\Omega\) falls by a factor of 4 per halving of \(h\), as \(\omega_h=\Omega(1+h^2\Omega^2/24+\dots)\) predicts. The scalar standing wave is real, so it carries no charge and no current and the gauge field stays exactly at rest; its agreement to round-off therefore tests the code’s free scalar sector, not the coupling. The gauge-wave differences (\(10^{-11}\) to \(10^{-14}\)) are not round-off: they are the amplitude-squared nonlinear shift measured in (D). A fixed frequency shift changes \(\cos\omega h\) by an amount proportional to \(h^2\), which is why the differences fall by about 4 per halving of \(h\).

(E) A single complex plane wave is not stationary. A first attempt used a complex scalar plane wave \(\phi=a e^{ikx}\) for the scalar dispersion test and failed over long runs. The reason is physical, not numerical. Such a wave has zero charge density but a net current \(\propto a^2\sin k\), which drives the uniform electric field; for \(\dot E_x=-2a^2\sin k\cos^2\omega t\) the mean field is \(-2a^2\sin k\,[\,t/2+\sin(2\Omega t)/(4\Omega)\,]\) (here \(a=0.01\), \(L=32\), \(n=2\), \(h=0.05\)).

What these results show

  • The linearised scheme has the predicted dispersion, exactly. At the vacuum the actual code’s one-step map on a plane wave is the predicted matrix, over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), with determinant \(1\), trace \(2-h^2\lambda\) and the stated minimal polynomial, in six parameter sets including an unstable one. Time discretisation enters only through \(\sin(\omega h/2)=h\Omega/2\).
  • The nonlinear step is second-order consistent, exactly, at a generic rational state: its Taylor coefficients agree with the exact flow through \(h^2\), the Gauss function is preserved as a series identity, and the displayed \(H\) is conserved by the continuous equations used in the code.
  • The floating-point behaviour matches the exact prediction to \(10^{-14}\) or better in the trace, over thousands of steps, with a measured \(\varepsilon^2\) error in the frequency under refinement at fixed Courant number.
  • The energy deviation observed in Part 8 stays bounded to \(T=1000\) for these data, and keeps its \(h^2\) scaling.
  • The linear stability limit \(h^2\lambda_{\max}\le4\) is exactly as predicted, and is reached in practice.

What they do not show

  • Nonlinear spatial convergence. (B) varies the lattice at the linear level, where the field scaling does not matter. It does not test the nonlinear coupled system under refinement, which needs the explicit-spacing Hamiltonian of Part 8 (derived there, not implemented or tested).
  • That the model’s dynamics are Maxwell–matter dynamics. The linear dispersion tested here is a property of the lattice scalar and lattice wave equation. It is necessary for a Maxwell–matter model, not sufficient. In particular nothing here tests the nonlinear couplings or the compact nonlinearity beyond the amplitude-squared shift in (D). I would also flag, as something to check against a lattice gauge reference rather than a result of mine, that compact \(U(1)\) in 2+1 dimensions is expected to behave non-perturbatively at strong coupling, so the weak-field regime tested here should not be extrapolated.
  • Independent agreement. The target relation comes from linearising the same model, and no independent solver, benchmark or exact coupled solution was used.
  • Anything about the rotor. These are tests of the lattice model.

Next steps

  1. The explicit-spacing nonlinear refinement (Part 8’s list, item 1), now that the linear order in both \(h\) and \(\varepsilon\) is established: with the Hamiltonian written with \(\varepsilon\), check self-convergence of a smooth nonlinear solution on nested grids.
  2. A manufactured-solution test of all terms at finite amplitude, since the linear checks cover only the first-order terms.
  3. The weak-field comparison with Part 4’s linear scheme, using the dispersion relation above as the target.
  4. Exact-arithmetic regression: keep exact_dispersion_check.py with Part 7’s exact suite, since it checks identities that floating-point residuals can only bound.

Reproducing

The files used for this post are available alongside it:

  • code/exact_dispersion_check.py: Checks 1 and 2 (exact arithmetic).
  • code/long_time_runs.py: Check 3 (floating point).
  • code/validate_coupled_scheme.py: the Part 8 script, imported by both for the floating-point step, energy, Gauss function and initial data.

Place the three files in the same directory and run python3 exact_dispersion_check.py and python3 long_time_runs.py. Tested with Python 3.12.3 and NumPy 2.4.4. On my machine the exact script takes about 7 seconds and the long-time script about 80 seconds.

"""
Exact (rational-arithmetic) checks for the coupled scalar-gauge kick-Cayley-drift-kick scheme of Parts 7-8.

  (0) cross-check: this exact implementation equals the floating-point vectorised step of Part 8 (L = 3, random state)
  (1) plane-wave check over Q(i): the one-step map linearised at the vacuum, acting on a plane wave, equals the predicted
      matrix; determinant 1; trace 2 - h^2 Omega^2; minimal polynomial; decoupling of the gauge and scalar sectors
  (2) consistency in h: Taylor coefficients in a formal step size h of the full nonlinear step, against the exact flow,
      at a random rational state; Gauss function and energy as series in h

All numbers are exact rationals (Gaussian rationals Q(i) with truncated power series).  No angle, exp or arctan is used
inside (1) and (2).  Needs numpy (only for roll on object arrays) and, for (0), validate_coupled_scheme.py from Part 8.
Tested with Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.4.4.
"""
import random
import numpy as np
from fractions import Fraction as Fr


# ---------- Gaussian rationals Q(i) ----------
class G:
    __slots__ = ("re", "im")
    def __init__(self, re=0, im=0): self.re = Fr(re); self.im = Fr(im)
    @staticmethod
    def co(o):
        if isinstance(o, G): return o
        if isinstance(o, (int, Fr)): return G(o)
        return None
    def __add__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else G(s.re + o.re, s.im + o.im)
    __radd__ = __add__
    def __sub__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else G(s.re - o.re, s.im - o.im)
    def __rsub__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else o - s
    def __neg__(s): return G(-s.re, -s.im)
    def __mul__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else G(s.re * o.re - s.im * o.im, s.re * o.im + s.im * o.re)
    __rmul__ = __mul__
    def inv(s): n = s.re ** 2 + s.im ** 2; return G(s.re / n, -s.im / n)
    def __truediv__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else s * o.inv()
    def __rtruediv__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else o * s.inv()
    def conj(s): return G(s.re, -s.im)
    def __eq__(s, o):
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else (s.re == o.re and s.im == o.im)
    def __hash__(s): return hash((s.re, s.im))
    def iszero(s): return s.re == 0 and s.im == 0
    def mag(s): return max(abs(float(s.re)), abs(float(s.im)))
    def __repr__(s): return f"({s.re}+{s.im}i)"

I = G(0, 1)


# ---------- truncated power series over Q(i):  c[0] + c[1] x + ... + c[ORDER] x^ORDER ----------
ORDER = 1
class S:
    __slots__ = ("c",)
    def __init__(self, c=0):
        self.c = c if isinstance(c, list) else [G.co(c)] + [G() for _ in range(ORDER)]
    @staticmethod
    def co(o):
        if isinstance(o, S): return o
        if isinstance(o, (G, int, Fr)): return S(o)
        return None
    def __add__(s, o):
        o = S.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else S([a + b for a, b in zip(s.c, o.c)])
    __radd__ = __add__
    def __sub__(s, o):
        o = S.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else S([a - b for a, b in zip(s.c, o.c)])
    def __rsub__(s, o):
        o = S.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else o - s
    def __neg__(s): return S([-a for a in s.c])
    def __mul__(s, o):
        if not isinstance(o, S):
            o = G.co(o)
            return NotImplemented if o is None else S([a * o for a in s.c])
        c = [G() for _ in range(ORDER + 1)]
        for i, a in enumerate(s.c):
            if a.iszero(): continue
            for j, b in enumerate(o.c):
                if i + j > ORDER: break
                c[i + j] = c[i + j] + a * b
        return S(c)
    __rmul__ = __mul__
    def inv(s):
        b = [s.c[0].inv()]
        for n in range(1, ORDER + 1):
            acc = G()
            for k in range(1, n + 1): acc = acc + s.c[k] * b[n - k]
            b.append(-(acc * b[0]))
        return S(b)
    def __truediv__(s, o):
        if isinstance(o, S): return s * o.inv()
        o = G.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else s * o.inv()
    def __rtruediv__(s, o):
        o = S.co(o)
        return NotImplemented if o is None else o * s.inv()
    def integ(s): return S([G()] + [s.c[n] * Fr(1, n + 1) for n in range(ORDER)])


def amap(f, a):
    out = np.empty(a.shape, dtype=object)
    for idx in np.ndindex(a.shape): out[idx] = f(a[idx])
    return out

def roll(a, k, ax): return np.roll(a, k, axis=ax)


# ---------- the scheme (complexified: U, Ub = 1/U, phi, phib = conj(phi), pi, pib = conj(pi) independent) ----------
# state = [U0, U1, Ub0, Ub1, E0, E1, phi, phib, pi, pib]; each an array of S

def forces(st, m2):
    U, Ub, E = st[0:2], st[2:4], st[4:6]; phi, phib, pi, pib = st[6:10]
    plaq = U[0] * roll(U[1], -1, 0) * roll(Ub[0], -1, 1) * Ub[1]
    plaqb = Ub[0] * roll(Ub[1], -1, 0) * roll(U[0], -1, 1) * U[1]
    xi = (plaq - plaqb) * G(0, Fr(-1, 2))                                  # Im(plaq) = (plaq - conj plaq)/(2i)
    dE = [xi - roll(xi, 1, 1), -(xi - roll(xi, 1, 0))]
    dpi = m2 * phib; dpib = m2 * phi
    for mu in (0, 1):
        z = U[mu] * roll(phi, -1, mu) * phib; zb = Ub[mu] * roll(phib, -1, mu) * phi
        dE[mu] = dE[mu] + (z - zb) * G(0, -1)                              # 2 Im z = (z - zb)/i
        dpi = dpi + 2 * phib - roll(U[mu], 1, mu) * roll(phib, 1, mu) - Ub[mu] * roll(phib, -1, mu)
        dpib = dpib + 2 * phi - roll(Ub[mu], 1, mu) * roll(phi, 1, mu) - U[mu] * roll(phi, -1, mu)
    return dE, dpi, dpib

def kick(st, h, m2):
    dE, dpi, dpib = forces(st, m2)
    return st[0:4] + [st[4] - h * dE[0], st[5] - h * dE[1], st[6], st[7], st[8] - h * dpi, st[9] - h * dpib]

def drift(st, h):
    E = st[4:6]; out = list(st)
    for mu in (0, 1):
        c = h * E[mu] * Fr(1, 2)
        r = (1 + I * c) / (1 - I * c)                                      # exp(2i arctan(hE/2)), exactly
        out[mu] = st[mu] * r; out[2 + mu] = st[2 + mu] / r
    out[6] = st[6] + h * st[9]; out[7] = st[7] + h * st[8]                 # phi += h conj(pi), and its conjugate
    return out

def step(st, h, m2):
    st = kick(st, h * Fr(1, 2), m2); st = drift(st, h); return kick(st, h * Fr(1, 2), m2)

def vector_field(st, m2):
    """Continuous-time equations (the h -> 0 limit of the step)."""
    dE, dpi, dpib = forces(st, m2); E = st[4:6]
    return [I * st[0] * E[0], I * st[1] * E[1], -I * st[2] * E[0], -I * st[3] * E[1], -dE[0], -dE[1], st[9], st[8], -dpi, -dpib]

def gauss(st):
    E, phi, phib, pi, pib = st[4:6], st[6], st[7], st[8], st[9]
    return sum(E[mu] - roll(E[mu], 1, mu) for mu in (0, 1)) + (pi * phi - pib * phib) * I       # div E - 2 Im(pi phi)

def energy(st, m2):
    U, Ub, E = st[0:2], st[2:4], st[4:6]; phi, phib, pi, pib = st[6:10]
    plaq = U[0] * roll(U[1], -1, 0) * roll(Ub[0], -1, 1) * Ub[1]
    plaqb = Ub[0] * roll(Ub[1], -1, 0) * roll(U[0], -1, 1) * U[1]
    H = sum(np.sum(E[mu] * E[mu]) * Fr(1, 2) for mu in (0, 1)) + np.sum(1 - (plaq + plaqb) * Fr(1, 2))
    H = H + np.sum(pi * pib) + m2 * np.sum(phi * phib)
    for mu in (0, 1): H = H + np.sum((U[mu] * roll(phi, -1, mu) - phi) * (Ub[mu] * roll(phib, -1, mu) - phib))
    return H

def mm(A, B): return np.dot(A, B)
def eye(n): return np.array([[G(1) if i == j else G() for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)], dtype=object)
def mat(rows): return np.array(rows, dtype=object)
def zero_matrix(M): return all(x.iszero() for x in M.flatten())
def scal(c, M): return amap(lambda x: x * c, M)

def spec(t): return Fr(4) * t * t / (1 + t * t)                            # 4 sin^2(k/2) = 4 t^2/(1+t^2), t = tan(k/2)

def plane_wave_matrix(t0, t1, h, m2, P=9, margin=3):
    """One-step linear map at the vacuum on the amplitude vector (theta0, theta1, E0, E1, phi, chi = conj(pi))."""
    global ORDER
    ORDER = 1
    z = [(1 + I * t) / (1 - I * t) for t in (t0, t1)]                      # exp(i k) as a Gaussian rational
    pw = np.empty((P, P), dtype=object)
    for i in range(P):
        for j in range(P):
            v = G(1)
            for _ in range(i): v = v * z[0]
            for _ in range(j): v = v * z[1]
            pw[i, j] = v
    sites = [(i, j) for i in range(margin, P - margin) for j in range(margin, P - margin)]
    def field(c0, amp):                                                     # c0 + eps * amp * plane wave
        o = np.empty((P, P), dtype=object)
        for i in range(P):
            for j in range(P): o[i, j] = S([G.co(c0), amp * pw[i, j]])
        return o
    cols = []
    for k in range(6):
        v = [G(1) if q == k else G() for q in range(6)]                    # one-hot perturbation
        st = [field(1, I * v[0]), field(1, I * v[1]), field(1, -I * v[0]), field(1, -I * v[1]),
              field(0, v[2]), field(0, v[3]), field(0, v[4]), field(0, G()), field(0, G()), field(0, v[5])]
        out = step(st, h, m2)
        col = None
        for (i, j) in sites:
            w = pw[i, j]
            vec = [out[0][i, j].c[1] / (I * w), out[1][i, j].c[1] / (I * w), out[4][i, j].c[1] / w, out[5][i, j].c[1] / w,
                   out[6][i, j].c[1] / w, out[9][i, j].c[1] / w]
            assert out[7][i, j].c[1].iszero() and out[8][i, j].c[1].iszero()           # conjugate sector stays at rest
            assert (out[2][i, j].c[1] + out[0][i, j].c[1]).iszero() and (out[3][i, j].c[1] + out[1][i, j].c[1]).iszero()  # Ub = 1/U
            assert all(out[q][i, j].c[0] == (1 if q < 4 else 0) for q in (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9))    # vacuum is fixed
            if col is None: col = vec
            else: assert all(a == b for a, b in zip(col, vec)), "output is not a plane wave"
        cols.append(col)
    return mat(cols).T, z

def predicted_matrix(z, h, m2):
    d = [zz - 1 for zz in z]
    K = mat([[d[1] * d[1].conj(), -(d[1].conj() * d[0])], [-(d[0].conj() * d[1]), d[0] * d[0].conj()]])
    h2 = h * h; Om2 = (d[0] * d[0].conj() + d[1] * d[1].conj())
    A = eye(2) - scal(h2 * Fr(1, 2), K)
    B = scal(-h, K) + scal(h2 * h * Fr(1, 4), mm(K, K))
    gauge = np.block([[A, scal(h, eye(2))], [B, A]])
    Lam = m2 + Om2
    scalar = mat([[1 - h2 * Lam * Fr(1, 2), G(h)], [-h * Lam + h2 * h * Lam * Lam * Fr(1, 4), 1 - h2 * Lam * Fr(1, 2)]])
    S6 = np.empty((6, 6), dtype=object)
    for a in range(6):
        for b in range(6): S6[a, b] = G()
    S6[:4, :4] = gauge; S6[4:, 4:] = scalar
    return S6, d, Om2, Lam

def check_case(t0, t1, h, m2):
    M, z = plane_wave_matrix(t0, t1, h, m2)
    Mp, d, Om2, Lam = predicted_matrix(z, h, m2)
    ok_pred = all(a == b for a, b in zip(M.flatten(), Mp.flatten()))
    Om2q = spec(t0) + spec(t1)
    ok_om = (Om2 == Om2q)
    Sg = M[:4, :4]; Ss = M[4:, 4:]
    tau_g = 2 - h * h * Om2q; tau_s = 2 - h * h * (m2 + Om2q)
    Id4 = eye(4); Id2 = eye(2)
    # physical gauge mode: theta, E both along w = (conj d1, -conj d0); two-dimensional invariant subspace
    w = [d[1].conj(), -d[0].conj()]
    uth = np.array([w[0], w[1], G(), G()], dtype=object)
    uE = np.array([G(), G(), w[0], w[1]], dtype=object)
    a1 = mm(Sg, uth); a2 = mm(Sg, uE)
    Pm = [[1 - h * h * Om2q * Fr(1, 2), G(h)], [-h * Om2q + h ** 3 * Om2q * Om2q * Fr(1, 4), 1 - h * h * Om2q * Fr(1, 2)]]
    inv_sub = all(a1[q] == Pm[0][0] * uth[q] + Pm[1][0] * uE[q] and a2[q] == Pm[0][1] * uth[q] + Pm[1][1] * uE[q] for q in range(4))
    det_g = (Pm[0][0] * Pm[1][1] - Pm[0][1] * Pm[1][0]) == 1
    tr_g = (Pm[0][0] + Pm[1][1]) == tau_g
    N = mm(mm(Sg - Id4, Sg - Id4), mm(Sg, Sg) - scal(tau_g, Sg) + Id4)
    minpoly_g = zero_matrix(N)
    det_s = (Ss[0, 0] * Ss[1, 1] - Ss[0, 1] * Ss[1, 0]) == 1
    tr_s = (Ss[0, 0] + Ss[1, 1]) == tau_s
    ch_s = zero_matrix(mm(Ss, Ss) - scal(tau_s, Ss) + Id2)
    ok = ok_pred and ok_om and inv_sub and det_g and tr_g and minpoly_g and det_s and tr_s and ch_s
    return ok, Om2q, m2 + Om2q, tau_g, tau_s, dict(pred=ok_pred, omega=ok_om, inv=inv_sub, det_g=det_g, tr_g=tr_g, minpoly=minpoly_g, det_s=det_s, tr_s=tr_s, ch=ch_s)

def random_state(L, rng):
    def rnd(): return Fr(rng.randint(-9, 9), 11)
    def arr(f):
        o = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object)
        for i in range(L):
            for j in range(L): o[i, j] = S(f())
        return o
    a = [[[rnd() for _ in range(L)] for _ in range(L)] for _ in range(2)]
    U, Ub, E = [], [], []
    for mu in (0, 1):
        Um = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object); Ubm = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object)
        for i in range(L):
            for j in range(L):
                ai = G(a[mu][i][j]); Um[i, j] = S((1 - I * ai) / (1 + I * ai)); Ubm[i, j] = S((1 + I * ai) / (1 - I * ai))
        U.append(Um); Ub.append(Ubm); E.append(arr(lambda: G(rnd())))
    phi = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object); pi = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object)
    phib = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object); pib = np.empty((L, L), dtype=object)
    for i in range(L):
        for j in range(L):
            p = G(rnd(), rnd()); q = G(rnd(), rnd())
            phi[i, j] = S(p); phib[i, j] = S(p.conj()); pi[i, j] = S(q); pib[i, j] = S(q.conj())
    return U + Ub + E + [phi, phib, pi, pib]

def series_check(L=3, m2=1, seed=2026):
    global ORDER
    ORDER = 3
    st0 = random_state(L, random.Random(seed))
    hh = S([G(), G(1)] + [G() for _ in range(ORDER - 1)])                  # the formal step size h
    stp = step(st0, hh, m2)
    flow = st0
    for _ in range(ORDER):
        F = vector_field(flow, m2)
        flow = [a + amap(lambda v: v.integ(), f) for a, f in zip(st0, F)]
    res = {}
    sa = [x for arr in stp for x in arr.flatten()]; sb = [x for arr in flow for x in arr.flatten()]
    res["state"] = [max((a - b).c[n].mag() for a, b in zip(sa, sb)) for n in range(ORDER + 1)]
    G0 = gauss(st0); Gs = gauss(stp)
    res["gauss0"] = max(x.c[0].mag() for x in G0.flatten())
    res["gauss"] = [max(((a - b).c[n]).mag() for a, b in zip(Gs.flatten(), G0.flatten())) for n in range(ORDER + 1)]
    H0 = energy(st0, m2); Hs = energy(stp, m2); Hf = energy(flow, m2)
    res["H_step"] = [(Hs - H0).c[n] for n in range(ORDER + 1)]
    res["H_flow"] = [(Hf - H0).c[n] for n in range(ORDER + 1)]
    return res

  (C) long-time behaviour of the constraint, charge and energy on the nonlinear data of Part 8 (T up to 1000)
  (D) amplitude dependence of the measured trace (nonlinear shift of the gauge wave)
  (E) a single complex plane wave is not stationary: its net current drives the uniform electric field
  (F) the stability limit h^2 (m^2 + 8) <= 4 predicted by the exact trace, tested above and below the limit

The measured quantity in (A), (B), (D) is the trace of the one-step map on the mode: for any linear functional s_n of the
state at step n, a mode with eigenvalues exp(+-i omega h) satisfies s_{n+1} + s_{n-1} = 2 cos(omega h) s_n, so
cos(omega h) is estimated by least squares from the recorded series.  Needs numpy and validate_coupled_scheme.py (Part 8).
Tested with Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.4.4.

import math

def trace_half(s):
    """Least-squares estimate of cos(omega h) from s_{n+1} + s_{n-1} = 2 cos(omega h) s_n."""
    s = np.asarray(s); return float(np.sum(s[1:-1] * (s[2:] + s[:-2])) / (2 * np.sum(s[1:-1] ** 2)))

def gauge_wave(L, n, amp):
    i, j = np.meshgrid(np.arange(L), np.arange(L), indexing='ij'); k = 2 * np.pi * n / L
    th = np.array([0 * i, amp * np.cos(k * i)], dtype=float)               # theta_y varying in x: transverse, E = 0, phi = pi = 0
    z = np.zeros((L, L), dtype=complex)
    probe = lambda s: 2 * np.sum(s[0][1] * np.cos(k * i)) / L ** 2
    return (th, np.zeros((2, L, L)), z.copy(), z.copy()), probe, k

def scalar_wave(L, n, amp):
    """Real standing wave phi = amp cos(k x), theta = 0, E = 0, pi = 0.  It carries no current (Im(phi(x+1) conj(phi(x))) = 0)
    and no charge, so the gauge field stays at rest and the scalar evolves exactly as the free lattice Klein-Gordon field."""
    i, j = np.meshgrid(np.arange(L), np.arange(L), indexing='ij'); k = 2 * np.pi * n / L
    phi = (amp * np.cos(k * i)).astype(complex)
    probe = lambda s: 2 * float(np.sum(s[2].real * np.cos(k * i))) / L ** 2
    return (np.zeros((2, L, L)), np.zeros((2, L, L)), phi, np.zeros((L, L), dtype=complex)), probe, k

def measure(make, L, n, amp, h, m2, nsteps):
    s, probe, k = make(L, n, amp); rec = [probe(s)]
    for _ in range(nsteps):
        s = step(s, h, m2); rec.append(probe(s))
    return trace_half(rec), k
