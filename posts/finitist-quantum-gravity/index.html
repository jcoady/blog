1 What if spacetime doesn’t have infinitely many points?

Every serious approach to quantum gravity eventually runs into the same wall: continuity. Loop Quantum Gravity, Causal Dynamical Triangulations, spin foams — all of them, sooner or later, ask you to integrate over a continuum of metrics, sum over an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, or take a limit that only barely makes analytic sense. Each of these moves imports a fresh set of headaches: measure-theoretic ambiguities, renormalization group flows that may or may not have a fixed point, and — most persistently — the specter of Ostrogradsky ghosts, the negative-norm states that plague any theory whose gravitational action includes curvature-squared terms.

This post walks through a different kind of proposal: what if you build spacetime kinematics natively over a finite field \(\mathbb{F}_p\), with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), and only afterward ask what happens as \(p \to \infty\)? No continuum to start with. No infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. No transcendental functions — not even \(\sin\) or \(\cos\). Just rational arithmetic, projective geometry, and character sums.

This is the finitist quantum gravity program, built on N. J. Wildberger’s Universal Hyperbolic Geometry (UHG) and developed across a six-step research arc culminating in the monograph this post summarizes (Zenodo, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22820802).

I want to be upfront about something before we dive in: this is a research program, not a finished theory. Large parts of it are proven, exact algebraic theorems, verified independently by computer algebra. Other parts — crucially, the final step that recovers Einstein’s equations — are honest, clearly-labeled conjectures about what should happen in a continuum limit that hasn’t been rigorously constructed yet. I’ll flag which is which as we go, because in a field this speculative, that distinction is the whole ballgame.

2 The core idea: geometry without transcendence

Ordinary trigonometry runs on \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\), distances that are square roots of sums of squares — none of which exist as rational operations. Wildberger’s Universal Hyperbolic Geometry replaces “distance” and “angle” with two purely algebraic cousins:

  • Quadrance \(q(a,b)\) — the squared separation between two points, defined via a bilinear form, with no square root taken.
  • Spread \(s(L_1, L_2)\) — the squared sine of the angle between two lines, again algebraic.

Because both are built from ratios of bilinear-form values, they make sense over any field, including a finite field \(\mathbb{F}_p\). No \(\pi\), no irrational numbers, no need for \(\mathbb{R}\) at all.

The finitist program works in projective three-space \(\mathbb{P}^3(\mathbb{F}_p)\): points are one-dimensional subspaces of a 4D vector space \(V^4 \cong \mathbb{F}_p^4\), equipped with a Lorentzian bilinear form

\[ B(u,v) = u^T \eta v, \qquad \eta = \operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1). \]

The condition \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is not decorative — it’s load-bearing. It guarantees \(-1\) is a quadratic non-residue mod \(p\), which does two things simultaneously: it lets us build a genuine Galois field extension \(\mathbb{F}_p[\delta] \cong \mathbb{F}_{p^2}\) (with \(\delta^2 = -1\) playing the role of \(i\)) whose Frobenius automorphism acts exactly like complex conjugation, and it fixes the sign of a crucial discriminant that later drives the entire ghost-cancellation mechanism.

2.1 The invariant hierarchy: quadrance → quadrea → quadrume

UHG’s real elegance shows up when you realize that “area” and “volume” are built the same way as distance — as normalized Gram determinants:

\[ \mathcal{Q}_k(v_1,\dots,v_k) \equiv \frac{\det(G_k)}{\prod_{i=1}^k Q(v_i)}, \qquad [G_k]_{ij} = B(v_i,v_j). \]

  • \(k=2\) gives the quadrance \(q_{ij}\) (squared edge length).
  • \(k=3\) gives the quadrea \(A_k\) (squared face area).
  • \(k=4\) gives the quadrume \(\mathcal{V}(\gamma)\) — the squared 4-volume of a tetrahedron, and the central object of the entire theory.

Every one of these is projectively invariant: independently rescaling each vertex vector \(v_i \mapsto \lambda_i v_i\) leaves \(\mathcal{Q}_k\) completely unchanged, because the \(\lambda_i^2\) factors cancel exactly between numerator and denominator. This is a clean, three-line proof — but it’s worth being precise about what it does and doesn’t mean. It says the representation of a projective point is redundant, the way homogeneous coordinates always are. It is not, by itself, a statement about continuous conformal transformations of physical distances — that would require a genuinely different construction, one this program does not (and does not claim to) provide.

A key structural fact, proven early in the program, is the Quadrume Factorization Theorem:

\[ \mathcal{V}(\gamma) = A_k \times h_k \qquad \text{for every vertex } k, \]

where \(h_k\) is the “altitude quadrance” from vertex \(k\) to the opposite face. Volume factors exactly as base-area times height, with no approximation — this is the discrete-algebraic mirror of the Euclidean identity you learned in school, just without ever taking a square root.

3 Step 1–2: A Clifford engine for a Lorentz group that “shouldn’t” exist

Here’s a genuinely surprising obstruction. Over the reals, you can represent \(SL(2,\mathbb{C})\) using \(2\times 2\) complex matrices, and the determinant map does double duty as a Lorentz-invariant quadratic form. Over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) with \(p\equiv 3\pmod 4\), this trick fails. The paper proves a precise Witt-index obstruction: the determinant form on \(2\times 2\) matrices has Witt index 2 (split/hyperbolic), while the Lorentzian form \(Q(v) = x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2-x_3^2\) has Witt index 1 (non-split/elliptic) — they are genuinely different quadratic-form types, and no linear isometry can identify them.

The fix is a minimal \(4\times4\) Clifford algebra built from explicit generators \(\gamma_0,\dots,\gamma_3 \in M_4(\mathbb{F}_p)\) satisfying \(\{\gamma_\mu,\gamma_\nu\} = 2\eta_{\mu\nu}I_4\). From these, bivector “edge anchors” \(X_{uv} = [\Gamma(u),\Gamma(v)]/B(u,v)\) generate parallel transport via Wildberger’s rational Cayley map:

\[ M_{uv} \equiv (I_4+X_{uv})(I_4-X_{uv})^{-1}. \]

The headline theorem here is that this transport is exactly unimodular: \(\det(M_{uv}) = 1\), identically, for every admissible edge. The proof hinges on a single elegant object — the alternating matrix \(S = \gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_2\), which defines an invariant symplectic structure (\(S^T=-S\), \(S^2=-I_4\)) under which every bivector generator anti-commutes (\(X_{uv}^TS + SX_{uv}=0\)). That symmetry forces \(M_{uv}^TSM_{uv}=S\), and unit determinant follows in three lines. No volume drift, no scale creep — the discrete parallel transport preserves 4-volume density exactly, at every step, for every prime.

Code 
import sympy as sp

# Minimal 4x4 Clifford generators over a symbolic field
sx = sp.Matrix([[0,1],[1,0]])
sz = sp.Matrix([[1,0],[0,-1]])
E  = sp.eye(2)
J  = sp.Matrix([[0,1],[-1,0]])

g0 = sp.Matrix(sp.kronecker_product(sx, E))
g1 = sp.Matrix(sp.kronecker_product(sz, sx))
g2 = sp.Matrix(sp.kronecker_product(sz, sz))
g3 = sp.Matrix(sp.kronecker_product(sz, J))
gammas = [g0, g1, g2, g3]
eta = sp.diag(1, 1, 1, -1)

# Verify Clifford relations {gamma_mu, gamma_nu} = 2 eta_{mu nu} I
I4 = sp.eye(4)
for mu in range(4):
    for nu in range(4):
        anticomm = gammas[mu]*gammas[nu] + gammas[nu]*gammas[mu]
        assert sp.simplify(anticomm - 2*eta[mu,nu]*I4) == sp.zeros(4,4)

# The invariant symplectic form S = gamma_0 gamma_1 gamma_2
S = g0 * g1 * g2
assert sp.simplify(S.T + S) == sp.zeros(4,4)   # S^T = -S
assert sp.simplify(S*S + I4) == sp.zeros(4,4)  # S^2 = -I
print("Clifford relations and symplectic invariance verified symbolically.")

4 Step 3: The Master Dihedral Relation

The next question is how the quadrume — the “volume” of a tetrahedral cell — relates to the dihedral angles along its edges, since those are what carry curvature information in a simplicial complex (this is the finite-field cousin of Regge calculus’s deficit angles).

The key result, the Dual Face-Normal Dihedral Spread Theorem, proves — without circularity, via Wildberger’s Thales theorem and a spread-invariance-under-perpendicular-duality argument — that the altitude-based dihedral spread \(E_{kl}\) equals the UHG spread between the two faces’ dual normal vectors:

\[ E_{kl} = s(n_i, n_j). \]

Combined with the factorization theorem above, this yields the Master Dihedral Relation:

\[ \mathcal{V} = \frac{A_i A_j}{q_{kl}}\, s(n_i, n_j). \]

This single equation is the hinge (pun intended) on which the whole continuum story turns: it’s what lets you later differentiate the quadrume action with respect to edge lengths and recover something that looks like a Regge-calculus variation. The differentiation itself uses a beautifully clean fact — the Gram Adjugate Parity Identity,

\[ \operatorname{adj}(G_4)_{kl} = (-1)^{k+l+1} B(n_k, n_l), \]

which lets you compute \(\partial \mathcal{V}/\partial q_{kl}\) in exact closed form via Jacobi’s determinant formula, entirely algebraically.

5 Step 4: Ghosts, parity, and a \(\pi\) phase shift

Now for the part that connects most directly to contemporary physics. In June 2026, Bateman and Turok (arXiv:2607.00096) proposed a striking resolution to the decades-old Ostrogradsky problem: four-derivative theories can be consistently quantized, on an indefinite Krein space, provided a hidden \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) “ghost parity” symmetry protects transition probabilities from going negative.

The finitist program builds a structural — and it’s important to say structural, not identical — analogue of this idea. The state space is spanned by tetrahedral configuration classes over \(\mathbb{F}_p[\delta]\), graded by a ghost-parity operator

\[ \mathcal{G}|\gamma_k\rangle = \varepsilon_k |\gamma_k\rangle, \qquad \varepsilon_k \equiv \prod_{i=1}^4 \chi_p(Q(v_i)) \in \{+1,-1\}, \]

the product of Legendre symbols of the vertex quadrances. A short argument shows the effective Hamiltonian \(H = -A + gW_\mathcal{V}\) commutes with \(\mathcal{G}\), because the allowed “mutations” between configurations are defined to preserve each vertex’s quadratic-residue character. The upshot, called kinematic sector superselection, is that physical states never dynamically evolve into ghost states: \(\langle \gamma_-|U^t|\gamma_+\rangle \equiv 0\).

It’s worth being precise about scope here: this is a superselection rule, not a reconstruction of Bateman–Turok’s more intricate “weak ghost symmetry” mechanism, in which physical and ghost sectors genuinely interact and a nonzero cross term is shown to vanish in the trace. The finitist framework’s ghost and physical sectors are separated from the outset by construction; the interesting content lies elsewhere — in a number-theoretic mechanism with no continuum precedent at all.

That mechanism is the Parity–Quadrume Character Theorem:

\[ \chi_p(\mathcal{V}(\gamma_k)) = -\varepsilon_k. \]

Physical states land in the quadratic non-residue class; ghosts land in the residue class. Combined with the classical quadratic Gauss sum \(g(\chi_p) = i\sqrt p\) (valid exactly because \(p\equiv3\pmod4\)), one can show that the ratio of ghost-sector to physical-sector partition sums acquires an asymptotic phase:

\[ \rho(p) \equiv \frac{Z^{(-)}}{Z^{(+)}} \longrightarrow -1 = e^{i\pi} \quad \text{as } p \to \infty. \]

A relative \(\pi\)-phase between two complex sums is a genuine, checkable arithmetic fact — a full destructive-interference argument for the full configuration variety is a separate and harder claim. The program establishes it rigorously on an explicit, geometrically distinguished sub-family: the Unit-Trirectangular variety \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{triad}}\), tetrahedra built on a fixed orthonormal spatial corner. There, the fiber cardinality has an exact closed form,

\[ N_{\text{triad}}(c) = p^2 - p\,\chi_p(c(c-1)), \]

and André Weil’s Riemann-Hypothesis-for-curves bound gives \(|\mathcal{W}(g)| \le 2\sqrt{p}\), which is exactly the estimate needed to show the relevant error term is \(\mathcal{O}(p^{-1/2}) = o(\sqrt p)\) — subdominant, as required.

In the interest of honesty about scope: \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{triad}}\), by construction, covers only the “all-quadratic-residue” sign sector of trirectangular tetrahedra — one-eighth of the natural family — and its point count (\(301\), for \(p=7\)) is a genuinely different quantity from the physical configuration-space dimension (\(109\), for \(p=7\)). This sub-variety is a rigorous proof of concept and reference benchmark for the destructive-interference mechanism, not (yet) a statement about the full physical path sum.

def legendre_symbol(a, p):
    return pow(a % p, (p - 1) // 2, p) if a % p != 0 else 0

def verify_triad_fiber_count(p):
    assert p % 4 == 3
    counts_by_k = {k: 0 for k in range(p)}
    for x in range(p):
        for y in range(p):
            for z in range(p):
                k = (x*x + y*y + z*z) % p
                counts_by_k[k] += 1

    for c in range(1, p):
        c_inv = pow(c, p - 2, p)
        k_c = (1 - c_inv) % p
        actual = counts_by_k[k_c]
        chi = legendre_symbol(c * (c - 1), p)
        chi_val = 1 if chi == 1 else (-1 if chi == p - 1 else 0)
        expected = p*p - p*chi_val
        assert actual == expected, f"mismatch at c={c}"
    print(f"[PASS] p={p}: N_triad(c) = p^2 - p*chi(c(c-1)) verified exactly for all c.")

6 Step 5: From arithmetic to a genuine path integral

Having a discrete state space with \(D\) basis vectors is one thing; recovering something resembling a continuum functional integral is another. Step 5 tackles this with what’s called the Triple-Scaling Scheme: send \(p\to\infty\), the number of simplices \(N\to\infty\), and the UV lattice cutoff \(a\to0\), all together, in a controlled way.

The key technical worry is modular wraparound: the coupling constant \(g\) lives in \(\mathbb{F}_p\), so if you’re not careful, matching it to a continuum coupling can silently wrap around modulo \(p\) and produce nonsense. The program proves a clean sub-Planckian bound,

\[ a^2 < 32\pi^2\kappa_0\ell_P^2 \implies 0 < g(p,a) < p \text{ for all } p, \]

which eliminates wraparound entirely and gives \(g/p \to 0\) monotonically. As a bonus, matching the background-volume phase term produces an emergent cosmological constant with exactly the right physical dimensions, \(\Lambda \sim a^{-2}\), with \(\hbar\) and \(G\) canceling cleanly out of the final expression.

The measure theory itself uses Prokhorov’s theorem: since the compactified metric moduli space \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_N\) is compact, any sequence of configuration-sampling probability measures on it is automatically tight, hence has weak-\(*\)-convergent subsequences. Identifying the actual limit as the normalized Lebesgue measure on moduli space requires one more ingredient — the Simplicial Uniform Metric Sampling Hypothesis — which is stated as an explicit, open conjecture, not proven. Conditional on it, the discrete sum converges to a genuine Feynman–Regge path integral, complete with a strictly unimodular phase \(|\exp(iS_{\text{Regge}}/\hbar)|=1\) that (unlike Euclidean quantum gravity’s infamous conformal-factor problem) is bounded by construction.

7 Step 6: Recovering Einstein’s equations — the conjectural frontier

This is the most ambitious step, and also the one where the honest labeling matters most.

The chain of reasoning goes: differentiate the discrete quadrume action with respect to edge quadrances (using the exact Gram-adjugate machinery from Step 3); interpret this via a Metric-Quadrance Jacobian relating edge lengths to a local metric tensor; and ask whether, in the \(N\to\infty, a\to0\) limit, the resulting object converges to the continuum variation of the Einstein–Hilbert action.

The monograph is careful — and this careful labeling survived a full adversarial peer-review cycle — to mark exactly where rigor gives way to conjecture. There’s a genuine, well-known obstruction here worth naming: the Bahr–Dittrich curvature obstruction, a real result from the discrete-gravity literature showing that diffeomorphism invariance in Regge calculus is only an exact symmetry on flat vertex stars, and is broken by curvature at order \(\mathcal{O}(a^3\cdot\text{Riemann})\), restored only asymptotically as the lattice refines. The program engages with this honestly rather than hand-waving past it.

Three explicit conjectures carry the remaining weight:

  1. The Simplicial Algebraic Schläfli Hypothesis — a discrete analogue of the classical Schläfli differential identity, needed to make edge-quadrance variations of the curvature sum vanish appropriately across closed hinge stars.
  2. The Tensorial Regge–Einstein Convergence Hypothesis — that the discrete hinge-curvature sum, contracted appropriately, converges to the actual Einstein tensor \(R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12 Rg_{\mu\nu}\) as the triangulation refines.
  3. The Simplicial Divergence Convergence Hypothesis — needed for the discrete Bianchi identity \(\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}\equiv0\) to emerge as an asymptotic Ward identity.

Conditional on all three (plus the Step-5 sampling hypothesis, plus a stated rest-frame identification for matter coupling), the stationary-phase limit of the discrete path sum reproduces the full non-vacuum Einstein equations:

\[ G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}. \]

That’s a real, precisely-stated target — not a vague gesture — but it remains a conjectural convergence statement, not a theorem, and the monograph says so explicitly.

8 What’s actually established, and what isn’t

Here’s the honest summary, matching the status table from the monograph itself:

Component Status
Clifford transport, unimodularity, curvature holonomy Proven theorem
Master Dihedral Relation, Gram adjugate identity Proven theorem
Ghost-parity superselection, Parity–Quadrume Character Theorem Proven theorem
Ghost destructive interference on \(\mathcal{M}_{\text{triad}}\) Proven, scoped to a specific \(1/8\)-sector sub-variety
Triple-scaling coupling identification, \(\Lambda\) dimensional consistency Proven theorem
Weak-\(*\) convergence to Feynman–Regge measure Proven conditional on the Sampling Hypothesis
Recovery of the Einstein Field Equations Formulated, conditional on three named continuum conjectures

The algebraic and number-theoretic core of this program — arguably its most interesting and durable contribution — stands on its own as rigorous, exactly-verified mathematics over finite fields, independent of whether the continuum conjectures in Step 6 ultimately pan out. Whether the Tensorial Regge–Einstein Convergence Hypothesis is actually true is a serious open question in discrete differential geometry, and answering it is where the program’s future work needs to go next.

9 Closing thought

There’s something appealing about a picture where spacetime, at bottom, doesn’t need \(\mathbb{R}\) at all — where every geometric quantity is an exact rational number modulo a prime, and the passage to continuum physics is a genuine limit to be proven, not an assumption baked in from line one. Whether this specific arithmetic substrate is the right one for quantum gravity is very much an open question. But the discipline of building the finite-field theory first, proving what can be proven exactly, and clearly flagging what remains conjectural, is — regardless of how the physics ultimately shakes out — the right way to do this kind of speculative foundational work.
