1. The question left open by the rotor construction

The preceding posts developed a projective \(2\times2\) rotor architecture, rational Cayley variables, lattice transport, and several exact identities for curvature and discrete evolution. They also identified an important gap: an algebraic representation of transport is not, by itself, an action principle for interacting matter and electromagnetic fields.

This post takes a deliberately limited next step. We specify a minimal Hamiltonian lattice model with charged scalar matter, gauge links, electric variables, magnetic plaquettes, and a Gauss constraint. We then identify the exact gauge and continuity identities that the model supports, distinguish the continuous-time Hamiltonian formulation from its numerical splitting, and state precisely where the rotor construction has not yet supplied a derivation.

The distinction is essential:

  • A model definition specifies the variables, Hamiltonian, and constraints.
  • An algebraic identity follows from those definitions and transformation laws.
  • A numerical test checks the implementation of a specified update.
  • A rotor derivation would explain why these variables, couplings, and equations follow from the rotor anchor rather than being independently chosen.

The first three can be investigated without having completed the fourth. The results below should be read in that order.

2. Lattice variables

Let \(\Lambda\) be a periodic square lattice in two spatial dimensions. Write \(x\) for a lattice vertex and \(\hat\mu\), \(\mu=1,2\), for the two positive lattice directions.

At each vertex introduce a complex scalar field and its canonical momentum, \[ \phi(x),\qquad \pi(x). \]

On each positively oriented link introduce a gauge phase \(U_\mu(x)\) and an electric variable \(E_\mu(x)\). The reverse link carries \(U_\mu(x)^{-1}\). In the usual unitary realization, \[ U_\mu(x)=e^{i\theta_\mu(x)}. \]

For the rational parametrization used in the rotor programme, write \[ U_\mu(x)=\frac{1-i a_\mu(x)}{1+i a_\mu(x)}. \] Here \(a_\mu\) is a Cayley coordinate wherever this chart is defined. Its group operation is \[ a\oplus b=\frac{a+b}{1-ab}, \] with the projective extension used when the displayed quotient is undefined.

This representation is useful because link composition can be expressed rationally. It does not mean that every familiar real-valued Hamiltonian or every time update automatically becomes a rational map in \(a_\mu\).

For an oriented plaquette \(P\), let \(U_P\) denote the ordered product of the four link phases around its boundary. In the unitary realization write \[ U_P=e^{i\theta_P}. \] The plaquette phase is the lattice curvature variable.

The model also has a real electric variable on each link. The electric field is canonically paired with the link angle \(\theta_\mu\), not automatically with the Cayley coordinate \(a_\mu\) under the ordinary canonical symplectic form.

3. A minimal Hamiltonian

Consider the Hamiltonian \[ \begin{aligned} H={}& \frac12\sum_{x,\mu} E_\mu(x)^2 +\sum_P\bigl(1-\cos\theta_P\bigr)\\ &+\sum_x \pi(x)\pi(x)^* +m^2\sum_x|\phi(x)|^2\\ &+\sum_{x,\mu} \left|U_\mu(x)\phi(x+\hat\mu)-\phi(x)\right|^2. \end{aligned} \]

The terms have the following roles:

  1. The quadratic electric term supplies the canonical electric energy.
  2. The plaquette term penalizes nontrivial magnetic curvature in the real \(U(1)\) model.
  3. The scalar momentum term supplies the matter kinetic energy.
  4. The mass term is a local gauge-invariant contribution.
  5. The hopping term couples matter at neighbouring vertices through the link variable.

This is a conventional minimal lattice gauge–matter Hamiltonian expressed using the variables of interest to the rotor programme. The rational Cayley parametrization can be used to encode its link phases, but the Hamiltonian has not been derived uniquely from the rotor anchor.

The energy expression is intended for the real or complex unitary realization. Over a finite field there is no compatible ordering in general, so the same expression cannot simply be interpreted as a real energy functional there. Exact finite-field identities require a separate algebraic formulation.

4. Gauge transformations

Let \(g(x)\) be a unit-modulus phase at each vertex. Adopt the transformation rules \[ \begin{aligned} \phi'(x)&=g(x)\phi(x),\\ \pi'(x)&=g(x)^{-1}\pi(x),\\ U_\mu'(x)&=g(x)U_\mu(x)g(x+\hat\mu)^{-1},\\ E_\mu'(x)&=E_\mu(x). \end{aligned} \]

The momentum transformation is chosen to make the local charge expression below invariant. In the unitary realization, \(g^{-1}=g^*\).

Define the covariant link difference \[ D_\mu\phi(x)=U_\mu(x)\phi(x+\hat\mu)-\phi(x). \] Under the transformation, \[ \begin{aligned} D_\mu'\phi'(x) &=U_\mu'(x)\phi'(x+\hat\mu)-\phi'(x)\\ &=g(x)\bigl(U_\mu(x)\phi(x+\hat\mu)-\phi(x)\bigr)\\ &=g(x)D_\mu\phi(x). \end{aligned} \] Consequently, \[ |D_\mu'\phi'(x)|^2=|D_\mu\phi(x)|^2. \]

The mass and scalar-momentum terms are also invariant. Since the gauge group is abelian, the vertex phases cancel around each plaquette, leaving \[ U_P'=U_P. \] The electric term is invariant because \(E_\mu\) is unchanged.

Thus the Hamiltonian is invariant under the stated local gauge transformations.

This is an identity of the specified lattice model. It does not by itself establish that the link phases have been derived from a rotor field, or that the model has exact Lorentz covariance.

5. Charge and Gauss law

Define the vertex charge by \[ \rho(x)=-2\,\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\pi(x)\phi(x)\bigr). \] It is gauge invariant because the phases in \(\pi\) and \(\phi\) cancel: \[ \pi'(x)\phi'(x)=\pi(x)\phi(x). \]

For a positively oriented link, define \[ J_\mu(x)= 2\,\operatorname{Im}\left( U_\mu(x)\phi(x+\hat\mu)\phi(x)^* \right). \] This current is also gauge invariant.

Choose the lattice divergence convention \[ (\operatorname{div}E)(x) =\sum_\mu\bigl(E_\mu(x)-E_\mu(x-\hat\mu)\bigr). \] The Gauss constraint is \[ G(x)=(\operatorname{div}E)(x)+\rho(x)=0. \]

The relative signs in these definitions matter. With \[ J_\mu(x)=\frac{\partial H_{\mathrm{hop}}}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)}, \qquad \dot E_\mu(x)=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)}, \] the hopping contribution to the electric equation is \(-J_\mu(x)\). The compatible matter identity is \[ \dot\rho(x)=(\operatorname{div}J)(x). \] If the conventional continuity equation is written as \[ \dot\rho+\operatorname{div}j_{\mathrm{phys}}=0, \] then \(j_{\mathrm{phys}}=-J\) under these conventions.

This sign convention is not cosmetic: using a current with the opposite orientation without changing the electric update would spoil the cancellation in the Gauss constraint.

6. Why the continuity identity holds

The continuity identity follows from the scalar Hamilton equations and the hopping term. It does not need to be inferred from a small numerical residual.

For one link from \(x\) to \(y=x+\hat\mu\), put \[ z=U_\mu(x)\phi(y)\phi(x)^*. \] The hopping energy on that link contains the phase-dependent terms \[ -z-z^*. \] Differentiation with respect to the link angle gives \[ \frac{\partial H_{\mathrm{hop}}}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)} =2\,\operatorname{Im}(z)=J_\mu(x). \]

The corresponding scalar momentum equations give opposite charge changes at the two endpoints: \(+J_\mu(x)\) at \(x\) and \(-J_\mu(x)\) at \(y\), with the definitions above. Summing over incident links yields \[ \dot\rho(x)= \sum_\mu\bigl(J_\mu(x)-J_\mu(x-\hat\mu)\bigr). \]

The electric equation has the opposite hopping contribution, \[ \dot E_\mu(x)=-J_\mu(x)+\text{magnetic contribution}. \] The divergence of the magnetic contribution vanishes by the lattice boundary-of-a-boundary identity: the oriented plaquette contributions cancel around each vertex. Therefore \[ \frac{d}{dt}G(x)=0 \] for the unforced Hamiltonian evolution, provided the initial data satisfy \(G(x)=0\).

This is the continuous-time identity for the specified Hamiltonian system. A numerical integrator must still be checked against the corresponding discrete identity for its actual update.

7. The action principle and the temporal multiplier

The Hamiltonian can be placed in a canonical action principle. Schematically, in the real unitary realization, \[ \begin{aligned} S=\int dt\,\Bigg[ &\sum_{x,\mu} E_\mu(x)\dot\theta_\mu(x)\\ &+\sum_x\left( \pi(x)\dot\phi(x) +\pi(x)^*\dot\phi(x)^* \right)\\ &-H+\sum_x\lambda(x,t)G(x) \Bigg]. \end{aligned} \] The canonical convention is chosen so that the scalar Hamilton equations include \(\dot\phi=\pi^*\). The multiplier \(\lambda\) imposes Gauss law. The sign assigned to the multiplier can equivalently be absorbed into its definition.

The important point is the order of operations: introduce the multiplier and vary the action before choosing temporal gauge. The multiplier enforces the constraint; setting it to zero is a gauge choice, not a replacement for the constraint equation.

A continuous-time multiplier \(\lambda(x,t)\), often associated with \(A_0\), should not be identified without qualification with a discrete temporal link. A temporal link represents parallel transport between time slices. To formulate a fully discrete spacetime gauge theory, its transformation law and its relation to the time update must be specified.

The displayed action is a Hamiltonian action for the chosen lattice model. It is not yet a rotor-derived action. In particular, the model’s matter hopping term, scalar mass, electric energy, and magnetic plaquette energy have been selected as the minimal gauge-invariant ingredients; the rotor construction has not uniquely forced these choices.

8. Splitting the dynamics: what is exact and what is not

A useful numerical strategy is a symmetric kick–drift–kick splitting. The scalar and gauge variables are advanced in stages so that the position-dependent forces are applied in kicks and the momentum-dependent terms are advanced in drifts.

For the canonical angle–electric pair, the electric Hamiltonian is \[ H_E=\frac12 E^2. \] Its exact Hamiltonian flow for a time interval \(h\) is \[ E'=E,\qquad \theta'=\theta+hE. \]

This exact angle drift is generally not a rational update in the Cayley coordinate \(a\). If instead one uses the Cayley group update \[ a'=a\oplus\left(-\frac{hE}{2}\right), \] the corresponding phase angle changes by a rationally parametrized Cayley increment. In the angle coordinate, this is a shear of the form \[ \theta'=\theta+f(E),\qquad E'=E, \] with \(f(E)\) determined by the Cayley increment.

Such a shear is symplectic in the canonical \((\theta,E)\) variables and is reversible when the increment is reversed. But it is not the exact time-\(h\) flow of \(H_E=E^2/2\): the exact flow uses \(hE\), not the nonlinear Cayley increment.

These are distinct integrators. A numerical result obtained with one cannot be used as a verification of the other without a separate argument.

For the scalar drift, the update \[ \phi'=\phi+h\pi^*,\qquad \pi'=\pi \] leaves the charge invariant, since \[ \pi\phi'=\pi\phi+h|\pi|^2 \] differs from \(\pi\phi\) by a real quantity. Hence \(\rho'=\rho\) during this drift. The link drift changes the link phase but leaves \(E\) unchanged, so it also leaves the electric part of Gauss law unchanged.

During a hopping kick, the scalar field and link phases are fixed while the momenta change. The charge update is then governed by the current evaluated at that kick configuration. The electric update uses the opposite current contribution, so the two changes cancel in \(G\).

For a full kick–drift–kick step, the current appearing in the combined change is the average of the two kick currents, \[ \overline J_\mu(x)= \frac12\left(J_\mu^{\mathrm{start}}(x) +J_\mu^{\mathrm{end}}(x)\right). \] The full-step continuity statement must use this time-staggered current. It should not be written using only the initial or final current unless a separate identity justifies that replacement.

9. The rotor anchor: what it motivates and what it does not derive

In the four-dimensional electromagnetic interpretation, the complex rotor anchor is written schematically as \[ \zeta=\mathbf E+i\mathbf B. \] Its quadratic product contains the familiar field invariants: \[ \operatorname{Re}(\zeta\cdot\zeta) =\mathbf E^2-\mathbf B^2,\qquad \operatorname{Im}(\zeta\cdot\zeta) =2\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B. \]

These expressions motivate the usual quadratic electromagnetic action. The term involving \(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B\) is associated with \(F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}\). Its integral is a boundary or topological contribution under appropriate assumptions, including a suitable globally defined potential and boundary conditions. On nontrivial topologies or in the presence of boundary contributions, it cannot simply be discarded without qualification.

This invariant calculation is a motivation for the gauge-field sector; it does not derive the full lattice Hamiltonian above. In particular, it does not establish:

  • a unique map from the rotor anchor to every link variable;
  • a unique rational regularization of the magnetic plaquette energy;
  • the charged scalar hopping term or scalar mass term;
  • a complete spacetime action with temporal links and matter coupling;
  • a general sourced Maxwell–matter evolution with a proven discrete Gauss constraint.

Those are separate mathematical and numerical tasks.

10. What has been checked, and what remains open

The status should be separated into three categories.

Algebraic structure

For the specified model and conventions:

  • the hopping term is gauge invariant;
  • the abelian plaquette phase is gauge invariant;
  • the charge expression is gauge invariant;
  • the current is the link-angle derivative of the hopping Hamiltonian;
  • the scalar continuity identity cancels the hopping contribution to the time derivative of Gauss law;
  • the exact electric drift and the Cayley-coordinate drift are distinct updates.

These statements are consequences of the definitions and equations above.

Numerical evidence

Separate experiments have examined pure-gauge evolution, linearized wave dispersion, reversibility-related behaviour, and a manufactured coupled solution with spatially varying magnetic curvature. The manufactured-solution study showed approximately second-order convergence in the scalar, momentum, gauge potential, electric field, and magnetic field, while the discrete Gauss residual remained near floating-point precision.

Those numerical results support the consistency of the particular tested discretization. They do not constitute a general convergence theorem, and the manufactured forcing does not establish the behaviour of the unforced coupled system. Detailed tables and reproducible numerical experiments belong in the follow-up validation post.

Theoretical gaps

The principal unresolved tasks are:

  1. Establish a fully specified unforced coupled evolution with nonzero charge and nontrivial magnetic curvature.
  2. Prove the discrete continuity and Gauss identities for the exact full numerical step, including the chosen time staggering.
  3. Separate spatial and temporal convergence orders.
  4. Establish the energy and reversibility behaviour of the coupled integrator under nontrivial initial data.
  5. Derive—or explicitly leave open—the relationship between the rotor anchor and the chosen action, links, and matter coupling.
  6. Specify the role of temporal links in a spacetime-gauge formulation.

The existence of a gauge-invariant Hamiltonian is not a substitute for these results.

11. Conclusion

A minimal action principle makes the variables and constraints explicit. It also reveals exactly where the algebraic and numerical claims must be kept separate.

The gauge-invariant lattice Hamiltonian provides a concrete setting for charged matter, electric variables, magnetic plaquettes, and Gauss law. The current identity explains the constraint cancellation, while the distinction between exact angle drift and Cayley drift prevents a numerical approximation from being misidentified as an exact Hamiltonian flow.

The rotor anchor supplies a motivating algebraic representation of electromagnetic invariants, but the full interacting lattice action and its matter coupling remain additional structure rather than a completed derivation from the rotor.

The next step is therefore not to claim that the entire rotor-based electromagnetic theory has been validated. It is to validate the specified coupled dynamics in increasingly demanding tests, while continuing to investigate which parts of the model can be derived intrinsically from the rotor architecture.
