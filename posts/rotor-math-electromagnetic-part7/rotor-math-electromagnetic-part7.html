Where Part 6 left off

Part 6 ended with a narrow task: answer five questions in a small model. What are the independent variables? What is the exact discrete action? What follows from varying it? Do the gauge and charge-conservation identities hold? Where does the rotor anchor enter, and what is derived from it? This post answers them in the smallest model I could build that has a gauge field, a charged matter field, a Gauss constraint and a matter current.

Scope
  • Calculation 22 defines a rational, reversible kick–Cayley-drift–kick update for a charged complex scalar coupled to compact U(1) lattice links in \(2{+}1\) dimensions. The Gauss function is preserved exactly by each sub-step, the kick satisfies an exact discrete continuity identity (and the full step satisfies one with the time-centred current), and gauge covariance and reversibility hold. These are proved in the text and checked in exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb F_7\) and \(\mathbb F_{11}\).
  • Calculation 23 identifies the two Lorentz-invariant real quadratic forms in the electromagnetic field. Under the usual assumption that the pseudoscalar term is a boundary contribution, the rotor’s Lorentz action selects the Maxwell quadratic field Lagrangian up to normalisation. It does not derive the lattice regularisation, the links, or the matter action.

Calculations 22 and 23 are separate results. Calculation 22 is a \(2{+}1\)-dimensional lattice model (two electric components per site and one plaquette variable per square). Calculation 23 is a \(3{+}1\)-dimensional calculation with three-component \(\mathbf E\) and \(\mathbf B\). Calculation 23 motivates the form of the continuum field action; it does not derive Calculation 22 or show that its dynamics reproduce \(3{+}1\)-dimensional Maxwell theory.

The continuous-time Hamiltonian action supplies the canonical equations and the Gauss constraint. The rational update is a separate discretisation: its Cayley drift is the exact flow of a modified electric kinetic energy \(T_h(E)\), not of \(\tfrac12E^2\), so the update is not claimed to be an exact Strang splitting of the displayed Hamiltonian, and it is not derived by varying a discrete action. Its convergence to Maxwell–matter dynamics is not established here.

Not claimed: that the scheme approximates Maxwell theory with matter (wave speed, dispersion and energy behaviour in the coupled system were not tested); a real-time fermion (a complex scalar is used, not the Wilson–Dirac operator of Part 3); Lorentz covariance on the lattice; a rotor-derived regularisation, link structure or matter action; any conformal or convergence result.

Proofs and checks. The identities are proved in the text. They are algebraic identities in \(K=F[i]\) and hold wherever the operations are defined: the Cayley denominators \(1+a^2\) and \(1+(hE/2)^2\) are non-zero (automatic when \(-1\) is not a square: over \(\mathbb Q\) and for \(p\equiv3\pmod4\)), the point \(a=\infty\) is handled projectively, and the update needs only that \(2\) is invertible (\(\mathrm{char}\,F\neq2\), for the factor \(h/2\) in the drift). The step \(h\) may be any element of \(F\), and in the coordinates \(K=F\oplus iF\) the real and imaginary parts are coordinates and need no division. The divisions by \(2\) and \(2i\) in the conjugation formulas for \(\mathrm{Re}\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\), and the division by \(h\) in the real-variable formula for \(T_h'\), belong to the interpretation, not to the update. The tests use \(h=1/3\) as a convenient value, which requires \(p\neq3\). The tables are exact-arithmetic verification (random states, including \(a=\infty\)) that checks the proofs and the script; they do not establish validity over fields not tested. One check is in floating point and is marked. Four kinds of statement are kept apart throughout: a model definition (variables, Hamiltonian, constraints), an algebraic identity (a consequence of the definitions), a numerical test (a check of an implementation), and a rotor derivation (why the model follows from the rotor anchor). This post contains the first three; the fourth is largely open. Floating-point consistency tests of the coupled scheme are reported in Part 8. Over \(\mathbb Q\) I ran single steps only, because exact rational numbers grow about fourteen-fold in size per step (Part 1); the multi-step checks are over \(\mathbb F_p\). The script is at the end.

The model

Variables (question 1). On an \(L\times L\) periodic spatial lattice (so \(2{+}1\) dimensions with time):

  • Cayley link parameters \(a_\mu(x)\in\mathbb P^1(F)\), with \(U_\mu(x)=(1-ia_\mu)/(1+ia_\mu)=e^{i\theta_\mu(x)}\);
  • electric fields \(E_\mu(x)\in F\), conjugate to \(\theta_\mu\);
  • a charged complex scalar \(\phi(x)\in K=F[i]\) with conjugate momentum \(\pi(x)\in K\);
  • the temporal gauge component \(A_0(x)\), which in the Hamiltonian form below is a Lagrange multiplier (its discrete-time counterpart is the temporal link \(U_0=e^{i\Delta t\,A_0}\)).

I chose a scalar and the Hamiltonian form for three reasons. A scalar is the smallest matter field with a Noether charge. The Hamiltonian form keeps \(E\) real and the drift rational (a \(\oplus\) with a rational element). And a second-order Lagrangian discretisation of the Wilson term in the temporal plaquettes is not rational-explicit: the next temporal link must be solved from \(\sin\theta=-2a/(1+a^2)=s\), which gives \(a=(-1\pm\sqrt{1-s^2})/s\), a square root in general. (This is an algebraic observation; I did not run it.)

Algebraic versus real-variable notation. The expressions \(e^{i\theta}\), \(\cos\theta_P\) and \(\arctan\) in this post have their ordinary meaning in the real model. They are not operations over \(\mathbb Q\) or \(\mathbb F_p\). In the algebraic implementation, \(a_\mu(x)\in\mathbb P^1(F)\), \(U_\mu\in K=F[i]\) has norm one, and \(\bar{\phantom{u}}\) is the conjugation of \(K/F\). The plaquette energy is \(H_B=\sum_P(1-\mathrm{Re}\,U_P)\) with \(\mathrm{Re}\,U=(U+\bar U)/2\) (which needs \(2\) invertible), the plaquette force \(\sin\theta_P\) is \(\mathrm{Im}\,U_P=(U_P-\bar U_P)/2i\), and squared norms are written \(z\bar z\). Over \(\mathbb F_p\) there is no ordering, so the Hamiltonian and the Poisson brackets are formal there: only the algebraic identities of Proposition 22 are meaningful, not an energy.

Canonical conventions. The variables \(\phi,\phi^*,\pi,\pi^*\) are treated as independent canonical variables with \(\{\phi,\pi\}=1\), \(\{\phi^*,\pi^*\}=1\) and \(\{\theta_\mu,E_\mu\}=1\); physical states have \(\phi^*=\overline\phi\) and \(\pi^*=\overline\pi\). Then \(\rho=i(\pi\phi-\pi^*\phi^*)=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(\pi\phi)\) generates \(\phi\mapsto e^{i\alpha}\phi\), \(\pi\mapsto e^{-i\alpha}\pi\), because \(\{\phi,\,i\alpha\pi\phi\}=i\alpha\phi\) and \(\{\pi,\,i\alpha\pi\phi\}=-i\alpha\pi\); and \(\sum_x\alpha_x(\operatorname{div}E)_x\) generates \(\theta_\mu(x)\mapsto\theta_\mu(x)+\alpha_x-\alpha_{x+\hat\mu}\), because \(\{\theta_\mu(x),\sum_y\alpha_y(E_\mu(y)-E_\mu(y-\hat\mu))\}=\alpha_x-\alpha_{x+\hat\mu}\). So \(G=\operatorname{div}E+\rho\) generates exactly the gauge transformation of the model. A kinetic term \(\pi\pi^*\) gives \(\dot\phi=\partial H/\partial\pi=\pi^*\).

Action and Hamiltonian (question 2). In continuous time the first-order action is \[ S=\int dt\Bigl[\sum_{x,\mu}E_\mu\dot\theta_\mu+\sum_x\bigl(\pi\dot\phi+\pi^*\dot\phi^*\bigr)-H-\sum_xA_{0}(x)\,G(x)\Bigr], \] with \[ H=\sum\tfrac12E_\mu^2+\sum_P(1-\cos\theta_P)+\sum_x\pi\pi^*+\sum_{x,\mu}\bigl|U_\mu(x)\phi(x{+}\hat\mu)-\phi(x)\bigr|^2+m^2\sum_x|\phi|^2 . \] The gauge transformation is \(\phi\mapsto g\phi\), \(\pi\mapsto g^{-1}\pi\), \(U_\mu(x)\mapsto g(x)U_\mu(x)g(x{+}\hat\mu)^{-1}\), with \(E\) unchanged. Its generator is the Gauss function \[ G(x)=(\operatorname{div}E)(x)+\rho(x),\qquad(\operatorname{div}E)(x)=\sum_\mu\bigl[E_\mu(x)-E_\mu(x-\hat\mu)\bigr],\qquad \rho=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(\pi\phi). \]

Equations from varying (question 3). Varying \(A_0\) first gives the constraint \(G=0\) (the standard Dirac structure; I did not test this statement computationally). Temporal gauge \(A_0=0\) is then chosen for the evolution: the constraint is imposed on the initial data and shown below to be preserved by the update. Varying the remaining variables gives Hamilton’s equations: \(\dot\theta=\partial H/\partial E\), \(\dot E=-\partial H/\partial\theta\), \(\dot\phi=\pi^*\), \(\dot\pi=-\partial H/\partial\phi\). In these, the matter current enters the electric equation through \[ J_\mu(x)\equiv\frac{\partial H_{\rm hop}}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)}=2\,\mathrm{Im}\bigl(U_\mu(x)\,\phi(x{+}\hat\mu)\,\phi(x)^*\bigr),\qquad \dot E_\mu=-(\text{plaquette force})-J_\mu . \] So the current is not an external source: it is computed by varying the same hopping term that couples the matter to the links.

The scheme and its exact identities (question 4)

The time step is a symmetric kick–drift–kick scheme, \(\mathrm{step}(h)=\mathrm{kick}(h/2)\,\mathrm{drift}(h)\,\mathrm{kick}(h/2)\), where

  • the kick advances the momenta by the position-dependent forces: \(E\leftarrow E-h\,\partial H_{\rm pot}/\partial\theta\) and \(\pi\leftarrow\pi-h\,\partial H_{\rm pot}/\partial\phi\), with \(H_{\rm pot}\) the plaquette, hopping and mass terms (these depend on positions only, so the three kicks commute);
  • the drift advances the positions: \(\phi\leftarrow\phi+h\pi^*\) (the exact flow of \(\pi\pi^*\)) and \(a_\mu\leftarrow a_\mu\oplus(-\tfrac h2E_\mu)\) (the Cayley drift of Part 1, Calculation 10).

The Cayley drift is not the exact flow of \(\tfrac12E^2\). The exact flow would be \(\theta\mapsto\theta+hE\), i.e. \(U\mapsto Ue^{ihE}\). The Cayley drift gives \(U\mapsto U\,(1+ihE/2)/(1-ihE/2)\), which is \(\theta\mapsto\theta+2\arctan(hE/2)\) in the real model (the code never evaluates \(\arctan\); this is a real-variable interpretation only, and the expansion below is not an identity over \(\mathbb Q\) or \(\mathbb F_p\)). This is the exact flow of the modified kinetic energy \(T_h(E)\) with \(T_h'(E)=(2/h)\arctan(hE/2)\), that is \(T_h(E)=\tfrac12E^2-\tfrac{h^2}{48}E^4+O(h^4)\). So every sub-step is the exact flow of a gauge-invariant piece of the modified Hamiltonian \(H_h\) (with \(\tfrac12E^2\) replaced by \(T_h(E)\)), and the scheme deviates from the displayed \(H\) at order \(h^2\). The composition is a splitting integrator for the modified Hamiltonian, not its exact full-time flow; the existence of a useful conserved modified Hamiltonian for the complete discrete map is not established here. The exact real drift \(U\mapsto Ue^{ihE}\) is not rational over the fields used here. I keep the rational drift, and its approximation to the intended dynamics must be tested separately. With \(\phi=\pi=0\) the scheme reduces to Part 1’s Calculation 10 integrator, whose dispersion, energy behaviour and convergence were measured there. The coupled system is tested in floating point in Part 8 (constraints, charge, energy, reversibility and temporal convergence); its spatial convergence and dispersion have not been tested.

Every operation is rational over \(K\): the plaquette force is \(\sin\theta_P=\mathrm{Im}\,U_P\), and \(\mathrm{Im}\,z=(z-\bar z)/2i\).

Proposition 22.

  1. Gauss law is preserved exactly. Each of the kick and the drift leaves \(G\) unchanged, for any \(h\); hence so does the full step.
  2. Discrete continuity. For the kick, \(\rho_x^{\rm after}-\rho_x^{\rm before}=h\,(\operatorname{div}J)_x\), where \((\operatorname{div}J)_x=\sum_\mu[J_\mu(x)-J_\mu(x-\hat\mu)]\). For the full step, \(\rho^{n+1}-\rho^n=\tfrac h2\operatorname{div}J^{(0)}+\tfrac h2\operatorname{div}J^{(1)}=h\operatorname{div}\overline J^{\,n+1/2}\), where \(J^{(0)}\) and \(J^{(1)}\) are the currents at the states where the first and second half-kicks are evaluated and \(\overline J^{\,n+1/2}=\tfrac12(J^{(0)}+J^{(1)})\).
  3. Gauge covariance. The step commutes with every gauge transformation.
  4. Reversibility. \(\mathrm{step}(-h)\) is the exact inverse of \(\mathrm{step}(h)\).

Proof. (1) The plaquette kick changes only \(E\) by a lattice curl, so \(\Delta(\operatorname{div}E)=0\) and \(\Delta\rho=0\). The mass kick changes \(\pi\) by \(-hm^2\phi^*\), so \(\Delta\rho=2hm^2\,\mathrm{Im}|\phi|^2=0\). For the hopping kick, \(\Delta E_\mu(x)=-hJ_\mu(x)\), so \(\Delta(\operatorname{div}E)_x=-h(\operatorname{div}J)_x\). The momentum change is \(\Delta\pi_x=-h\sum_\mu\bigl(2\phi_x^*-U_\mu(x{-}\hat\mu)\phi^*_{x-\hat\mu}-U_\mu(x)^*\phi^*_{x+\hat\mu}\bigr)\), and \(\Delta\rho_x=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(\Delta\pi_x\phi_x)\); the terms \(2\lvert\phi_x\rvert^2\) are real and drop out, and with \(z_\mu(x)=U_\mu(x)\phi(x{+}\hat\mu)\phi(x)^*\) one finds \(\mathrm{Im}\bigl(U_\mu(x{-}\hat\mu)\phi^*_{x-\hat\mu}\phi_x\bigr)=\mathrm{Im}\,z_\mu(x{-}\hat\mu)\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\bigl(U_\mu(x)^*\phi^*_{x+\hat\mu}\phi_x\bigr)=-\mathrm{Im}\,z_\mu(x)\). Hence \(\Delta\rho_x=h\,(\operatorname{div}J)_x=-\Delta(\operatorname{div}E)_x\), which is (2) and gives \(\Delta G=0\). In the drift, \(\phi\leftarrow\phi+h\pi^*\) changes \(\rho\) by \(-2h\,\mathrm{Im}(\pi\pi^*)=0\), and the Cayley drift changes only the \(a_\mu\), on which neither \(\operatorname{div}E\) nor \(\rho\) depends. (3) Each sub-step is built from gauge-covariant expressions (\(\phi\), \(U\) and \(\pi\) enter only through gauge-invariant combinations or covariantly). (4) Each sub-step is inverted by \(h\to-h\), and the scheme is symmetric. For the full-step continuity in (2), the drift does not change \(\rho\) (shown in (1)), so the change of \(\rho\) over the step is the sum of the two half-kick changes, each given by the kick identity at its own state. \(\square\)

Three logically distinct statements are involved: the action gives Gauss’s law (standard, stated above), the matter equations give a continuity identity (item 2), and the evolution preserves the constraint (item 1). In this model the third is a theorem about the scheme: Proposition 22(1) is proved by direct algebra for each sub-step. Equivalently, each sub-flow is the exact flow of a gauge-invariant piece of the modified Hamiltonian \(H_h\) above, so each conserves its Noether charge \(G\).

What this does and does not give

  • Gives: a complete small coupled system in which the Gauss constraint, charge continuity and gauge covariance hold exactly for the discrete update, with a current derived from the same Hamiltonian that couples the matter to the links. This is the compatibility statement the earlier posts lacked, in one explicit model.
  • Does not give: evidence that the scheme approximates Maxwell theory with matter (wave speed, dispersion, energy behaviour were not tested); a fermionic matter field; a real-time version of the Wilson–Dirac operator; Lorentz covariance.

Where the rotor enters (question 5)

The rotor packages the field as \(\zeta=\mathbf E+i\mathbf B\), on which Lorentz transformations act by complex rotations (Part 1, Calculation 6). Consider real quadratic forms in \((\mathbf E,\mathbf B)\) invariant under the rotation and boost generators (\(\delta\zeta=\mathbf r\times\zeta\) and \(\delta\zeta=i\,\mathbf b\times\zeta\)).

Calculation 23. Solving the invariance conditions exactly (SymPy) gives a space of dimension 2, with basis \[ B^2-E^2=-\mathrm{Re}(\zeta\cdot\zeta),\qquad \mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B=\tfrac12\mathrm{Im}(\zeta\cdot\zeta). \]

So among local, gauge-invariant actions quadratic in the field strength, Lorentz invariance leaves two terms. The second, \(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B\) (the pseudoscalar \(F\tilde F\)), is a total derivative when the field strength comes from a globally defined potential with suitable boundary conditions (a standard fact, not tested here). With boundaries or non-trivial topological sectors it need not be irrelevant to the action, although it does not change the local source-free field equations. So, for a local quadratic action in the field strength on a topologically trivial domain with appropriate boundary conditions, Lorentz invariance fixes the ordinary free Maxwell action up to its overall normalisation: \(S_{\rm field}\propto\int\tfrac12(E^2-B^2)\). In the \(2{+}1\)-dimensional lattice model, the kinetic term \(\tfrac12E^2\) and the plaquette term \((1-\cos\theta_P)\approx\tfrac12\theta_P^2\) (where the single plaquette variable plays the role of \(B_z\)) match the continuum density \(\tfrac12(E^2-B^2)\) at leading order. This is a leading-order comparison that motivates the lattice terms; it is not a derivation of the lattice Hamiltonian, and the cosine regularisation is not fixed by the quadratic form.

This is what the rotor contributes: the form of the field action, in one line, from its Lorentz action. It does not supply:

  • the links, or the Cayley regularisation \(\cos\theta_P\) (the quadratic form fixes only the leading behaviour);
  • the matter action, the coupling, or the form of the hopping term;
  • the time discretisation (the Strang splitting and the Cayley drift are my choices).

Conformal symmetry

I agree with the suggestion to treat conformal symmetry as a later structural test, not a prerequisite. Classical Maxwell theory in four dimensions is conformally invariant, and the invariants \(\mathrm{Re}(\zeta\cdot\zeta)\) and \(\mathrm{Im}(\zeta\cdot\zeta)\) have the right scaling. Three separate statements need separate treatment: conformal invariance of the continuum rotor equations (a reasonable target), exact invariance on a fixed lattice (not expected), and recovery in a scaling limit. I have not tested any of them, and the \(2\times2\) rotor structure is not automatically the full conformal group of \(3{+}1\) dimensions; that connection would need its own result. A first concrete step would be to check that the solution space of the free equation is mapped to itself by a special conformal transformation combined with a position-dependent complex rotation of \(\zeta\).

Next directions

  1. Small-step expansion and consistency. The Cayley drift is already expanded (\(T_h(E)=\tfrac12E^2-\tfrac{h^2}{48}E^4+\dots\)). Part 8 measures the Gauss function, charge, energy, reversibility and temporal convergence of the coupled scheme in floating point. Still open: spatial convergence and wave dispersion of the coupled system, a manufactured-solution test, and a weak-field comparison with Calculation 18 and Part 1’s compact dynamics, so that the scheme can be described as a verified discretisation and not only a constraint-preserving rational model.
  2. A fermion. Replace the scalar by a real-time lattice fermion, with the doubling issue of Part 3, and repeat the Gauss and continuity identities.
  3. A discrete-action derivation. Derive the update (or an alternative) as a variational integrator of a discrete action with temporal links, and state which part of the update corresponds to which part of the action.
  4. Where the rotor could constrain more. Identify any further place where Lorentz covariance of \(\zeta\) restricts the coupling or the matter action, beyond the field action.
  5. Rotor-to-link construction. An explicit map from projective \(2\times2\) rotor data to lattice links, with its covariance and curvature properties. Part 5 gives such a map only for polynomial free solutions, through a chosen regularisation.
  6. Conformal test, as above.

Reproducing Calculations 22 and 23

The exact-arithmetic claims refer to the script below, minimal_action_model.py, which is embedded in full. SHA-256 of the tested file: a365ed41a471495f60b4a480ef3a8ee27d411d6265e560fd9468567ce413ddca. The floating-point validation of Part 8 (validate_coupled_scheme.py, SHA-256 20da795c2f78832520ec45406af55fb15ec40e579e57f854478f6d6e93057d8a) imports it, so keep both files in one directory. Run with python3 minimal_action_model.py (or with the argument Q, F7, F11, float or inv to run one part). Tested with Python 3.12.3 and SymPy 1.14.0.

"""
Calculations 22 and 23 (Part 7): a minimal coupled gauge-matter lattice model in exact arithmetic.

MODEL (2+1 D periodic L x L lattice, Hamiltonian form, temporal gauge; the temporal link is the Lagrange multiplier of the Gauss constraint)
  variables:  Cayley link parameters a_mu(x) in P^1(F),  electric fields E_mu(x) in F,  complex scalar phi(x) in K = F[i],  momentum pi(x) in K
  H = sum_{x,mu} E^2/2  +  sum_P (1 - cos theta_P)  +  sum_x pi pi*  +  sum_{x,mu} |U_mu(x) phi(x+mu) - phi(x)|^2  +  m^2 sum_x |phi|^2
  gauge transformation:  phi -> g phi,  pi -> g^-1 pi,  U_mu(x) -> g(x) U_mu(x) g(x+mu)^-1,  E unchanged
  Gauss generator:  G(x) = (div E)(x) + rho(x),   rho = -2 Im(pi phi),   (div E)(x) = sum_mu [E_mu(x) - E_mu(x-mu)]
  link current:     J_mu(x) = dH_hop/d theta_mu(x) = 2 Im( U_mu(x) phi(x+mu) phi(x)* )
CONVENTIONS: phi, phi*, pi, pi* are independent canonical variables with {phi, pi} = 1, {phi*, pi*} = 1, {theta, E} = 1; physical states have
  phi* = conj(phi), pi* = conj(pi).  rho = i (pi phi - pi* phi*) = -2 Im(pi phi) generates phi -> e^{i alpha} phi, pi -> e^{-i alpha} pi;
  alpha_x (div E)_x summed over x generates theta_mu(x) -> theta_mu(x) + alpha_x - alpha_{x+mu}.
SCHEME (symmetric kick-drift-kick):  step(h) = kick(h/2) drift(h) kick(h/2).
  The kicks are exact flows of the potential terms of H.  The matter drift is the exact flow of pi pi*.  The Cayley drift of the links is the
  exact flow of the MODIFIED kinetic energy T_h(E) with T_h'(E) = (2/h) arctan(hE/2), i.e. T_h(E) = E^2/2 - h^2 E^4/48 + O(h^4);
  it is NOT the exact flow of E^2/2.
  kick:   E <- E - h dH_pot/d theta,  pi <- pi - h dH_pot/d phi          (H_pot = plaquette + hopping + mass; depends on positions only)
  drift:  phi <- phi + h pi*,   a <- a (+) ( -(h/2) E )                  (Cayley drift)
Checks over Q (one full step: exact rational numbers grow about 14-fold in size per step), F_7 and F_11 (five full steps); random states, including a = infinity:
  (a) G is conserved exactly by every sub-step, for any h        (b) exact discrete continuity  Delta rho = h div J  for the kick
  (c) the step commutes with gauge transformations               (d) step(-h) is the exact inverse
  (b') continuity over a FULL step:  rho(n+1) - rho(n) = (h/2) div J(first kick) + (h/2) div J(second kick)
  (e) the space of real quadratic Lorentz invariants of (E, B) is 2-dimensional, spanned by E^2 - B^2 and E.B   (sympy)
  (f) floating point: the implemented kick forces equal the gradients of the stated potential Hamiltonian (finite differences)
Needs sympy for (e).  Tested with Python 3.12.3, SymPy 1.14.0.

# ------------------------------------------------------------------ fields ---
def make_Fp(p):
    class FpP:
        P = p
        def __init__(s, v): s.v = int(v) % FpP.P
        def __add__(s, o): return FpP(s.v + _v(o))
        __radd__ = __add__
        def __sub__(s, o): return FpP(s.v - _v(o))
        def __rsub__(s, o): return FpP(_v(o) - s.v)
        def __neg__(s): return FpP(-s.v)
        def __mul__(s, o): return FpP(s.v * _v(o))
        __rmul__ = __mul__
        def __truediv__(s, o): return FpP(s.v * pow(_v(o), -1, FpP.P))
        def __eq__(s, o): return o is not None and s.v == _v(o) % FpP.P
        def __hash__(s): return hash(s.v)
        def __repr__(s): return str(s.v)
    return FpP
def _v(o): return o.v if hasattr(o, "v") else int(o)

def kadd(u, v): return (u[0] + v[0], u[1] + v[1])
def ksub(u, v): return (u[0] - v[0], u[1] - v[1])
def kmul(u, v): return (u[0] * v[0] - u[1] * v[1], u[0] * v[1] + u[1] * v[0])
def kconj(u): return (u[0], -u[1])
def ksc(s, u): return (s * u[0], s * u[1])

class Ring:
    """P^1(F) with the Cayley law; a = None is infinity.  Requires -1 not a square (p = 3 mod 4)."""
    def __init__(s, mk, p=None):
        if p is not None: assert p % 4 == 3
        s.mk, s.p, s.one, s.zero = mk, p, mk(1), mk(0)
    def phase(s, a):
        if a is None: return (-s.one, s.zero)
        d = s.one + a * a
        if d == s.zero: raise ZeroDivisionError("1 + a^2 = 0")
        return ((s.one - a * a) / d, s.mk(-2) * a / d)
    def a_of(s, u): return None if u[0] == -s.one else -u[1] / (s.one + u[0])
    def oplus(s, a, b): return s.a_of(kmul(s.phase(a), s.phase(b)))
    def neg(s, a): return None if a is None else -a
    def eq(s, a, b): return (a is None and b is None) or (a is not None and b is not None and a == b)
RQ = Ring(Fr)
def RF(p): return Ring(make_Fp(p), p)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------ lattice ---
L = 3
SITES = [(i, j) for i in range(L) for j in range(L)]
def sh(x, mu, s=1): return ((x[0] + s * (mu == 0)) % L, (x[1] + s * (mu == 1)) % L)

class State:
    def __init__(s, a, E, phi, pi): s.a, s.E, s.phi, s.pi = a, E, phi, pi
    def copy(s): return State(dict(s.a), dict(s.E), dict(s.phi), dict(s.pi))
    def same(s, R, o):
        return all(R.eq(s.a[k], o.a[k]) and s.E[k] == o.E[k] for k in s.a) and all(s.phi[x] == o.phi[x] and s.pi[x] == o.pi[x] for x in s.phi)

def random_state(R, rng, with_infinity=True):
    if R is RQ:
        num = lambda: Fr(rng.randint(-5, 5), rng.randint(1, 4))
        par = lambda: None if (with_infinity and rng.random() < 0.05) else num()
    else:
        num = lambda: R.mk(rng.randrange(R.p))
        par = lambda: None if (with_infinity and rng.randrange(R.p + 1) == R.p) else R.mk(rng.randrange(R.p))
    a = {(x, mu): par() for x in SITES for mu in (0, 1)}; E = {(x, mu): num() for x in SITES for mu in (0, 1)}
    phi = {x: (num(), num()) for x in SITES}; pi = {x: (num(), num()) for x in SITES}
    return State({(x, mu): a[(x, mu)] for x in SITES for mu in (0, 1)}, E, phi, pi)

def links(R, st): return {k: R.phase(v) for k, v in st.a.items()}

def forces(R, st, m2):
    """Returns (dE_kick, dpi_kick, J) such that a kick of size h does E -= h*dE, pi -= h*dpi.   J_mu(x) = dH_hop/d theta."""
    U = links(R, st); two = R.mk(2)
    # plaquette: xi = sin(theta_P) = Im U_P
    xi = {}
    for (i, j) in SITES:
        UP = kmul(kmul(U[((i, j), 0)], U[(sh((i, j), 0), 1)]), kmul(kconj(U[(sh((i, j), 1), 0)]), kconj(U[((i, j), 1)])))
        xi[(i, j)] = UP[1]
    dE = {}
    for (i, j) in SITES:
        dE[((i, j), 0)] = xi[(i, j)] - xi[(i, (j - 1) % L)]                      # E_x -= h (xi(i,j) - xi(i,j-1))
        dE[((i, j), 1)] = -(xi[(i, j)] - xi[((i - 1) % L, j)])                   # E_y += h (xi(i,j) - xi(i-1,j))
    # hopping: J = 2 Im( U phi(x+mu) phi(x)* ),  dH/dphi_x = sum_mu [ 2 phi*_x - U_mu(x-mu) phi*_{x-mu} - U_mu(x)* phi*_{x+mu} ]
    J = {}
    for x in SITES:
        for mu in (0, 1):
            z = kmul(kmul(U[(x, mu)], st.phi[sh(x, mu)]), kconj(st.phi[x]))
            J[(x, mu)] = two * z[1]
            dE[(x, mu)] = dE[(x, mu)] + J[(x, mu)]
    dpi = {}
    for x in SITES:
        d = (R.zero, R.zero)
        for mu in (0, 1):
            d = kadd(d, ksc(two, kconj(st.phi[x])))
            d = ksub(d, kmul(U[(sh(x, mu, -1), mu)], kconj(st.phi[sh(x, mu, -1)])))
            d = ksub(d, kmul(kconj(U[(x, mu)]), kconj(st.phi[sh(x, mu)])))
        dpi[x] = kadd(d, ksc(m2, kconj(st.phi[x])))                               # + m^2 phi*
    return dE, dpi, J

def kick(R, st, h, m2):
    dE, dpi, J = forces(R, st, m2); out = st.copy()
    for k in out.E: out.E[k] = st.E[k] - h * dE[k]
    for x in out.pi: out.pi[x] = ksub(st.pi[x], ksc(h, dpi[x]))
    return out

def drift(R, st, h):
    out = st.copy()
    for x in out.phi: out.phi[x] = kadd(st.phi[x], ksc(h, kconj(st.pi[x])))
    half = h / R.mk(2)
    for k in out.a: out.a[k] = R.oplus(st.a[k], -(half * st.E[k]))
    return out

def step(R, st, h, m2):
    h2 = h / R.mk(2)
    return kick(R, drift(R, kick(R, st, h2, m2), h), h2, m2)

def gauss(R, st):
    G = {}
    for x in SITES:
        div = R.zero
        for mu in (0, 1): div = div + st.E[(x, mu)] - st.E[(sh(x, mu, -1), mu)]
        rho = -R.mk(2) * kmul(st.pi[x], st.phi[x])[1]
        G[x] = div + rho
    return G

def gauge_transform(R, st, gam):
    g = {x: R.phase(gam[x]) for x in SITES}; out = st.copy()
    for (x, mu) in out.a: out.a[(x, mu)] = R.oplus(R.oplus(gam[x], st.a[(x, mu)]), R.neg(gam[sh(x, mu)]))
    for x in out.phi:
        out.phi[x] = kmul(g[x], st.phi[x]); out.pi[x] = kmul(kconj(g[x]), st.pi[x])
    return out


def invariants_test():
    import sympy as sp
    E = sp.symbols('E1:4'); B = sp.symbols('B1:4'); v = list(E) + list(B)
    S = sp.Matrix(6, 6, lambda i, j: sp.Symbol(f's{min(i, j)}{max(i, j)}'))
    unk = sorted(S.free_symbols, key=str)
    def gen(rot, boost):
        # delta E = rot x E - boost x B ,  delta B = rot x B + boost x E   (rotation by a complex angle: delta zeta = (rot + i boost) x zeta ... i.e. i (boost x zeta))
        Ev = sp.Matrix(E); Bv = sp.Matrix(B); r = sp.Matrix(rot); b = sp.Matrix(boost)
        dE = r.cross(Ev) - b.cross(Bv); dB = r.cross(Bv) + b.cross(Ev)
        return sp.Matrix(list(dE) + list(dB))
    eqs = []
    unit = [sp.Matrix([1, 0, 0]), sp.Matrix([0, 1, 0]), sp.Matrix([0, 0, 1])]
    for k in range(3):
        for (r, b) in ((unit[k], sp.zeros(3, 1)), (sp.zeros(3, 1), unit[k])):
            dv = gen(r, b); q = sp.Matrix(v).T * S * dv * 2
            eqs += list(sp.Poly(sp.expand(q[0]), *v).coeffs())
    M, _ = sp.linear_eq_to_matrix(eqs, unk); ns = M.nullspace()
    forms = []
    for n in ns:
        sub = {unk[i]: n[i] for i in range(len(unk))}; Sm = S.subs(sub)
        forms.append(sp.expand((sp.Matrix(v).T * Sm * sp.Matrix(v))[0]))
    return len(ns), forms


def forces_vs_hamiltonian(seed=3, m2=1.0, delta=1e-6):
    """Floating point: compare the implemented kick forces with finite-difference gradients of
    H_pot = sum_P (1 - cos theta_P) + sum |U phi(x+mu) - phi(x)|^2 + m2 sum |phi|^2   (theta = -2 arctan a)."""
    import math
    Rf = Ring(float); rng = random.Random(seed)
    th = {(x, mu): rng.uniform(-1.2, 1.2) for x in SITES for mu in (0, 1)}
    phi = {x: complex(rng.uniform(-1, 1), rng.uniform(-1, 1)) for x in SITES}
    def Hpot(th, phi):
        U = {k: complex(math.cos(v), math.sin(v)) for k, v in th.items()}; H = 0.0
        for (i, j) in SITES:
            UP = U[((i, j), 0)] * U[(sh((i, j), 0), 1)] * U[(sh((i, j), 1), 0)].conjugate() * U[((i, j), 1)].conjugate(); H += 1 - UP.real
        for x in SITES:
            for mu in (0, 1): H += abs(U[(x, mu)] * phi[sh(x, mu)] - phi[x]) ** 2
            H += m2 * abs(phi[x]) ** 2
        return H
    a = {k: -math.tan(v / 2) for k, v in th.items()}
    st = State(a, {k: 0.0 for k in a}, {x: (phi[x].real, phi[x].imag) for x in SITES}, {x: (0.0, 0.0) for x in SITES})
    dE, dpi, J = forces(Rf, st, m2)
    errE = errP = 0.0
    for k in th:
        up = dict(th); dn = dict(th); up[k] += delta; dn[k] -= delta
        errE = max(errE, abs((Hpot(up, phi) - Hpot(dn, phi)) / (2 * delta) - dE[k]))
    for x in SITES:
        pr = dict(phi); mr = dict(phi); pi_ = dict(phi); mi = dict(phi)
        pr[x] += delta; mr[x] -= delta; pi_[x] += 1j * delta; mi[x] -= 1j * delta
        dRe = (Hpot(th, pr) - Hpot(th, mr)) / (2 * delta); dIm = (Hpot(th, pi_) - Hpot(th, mi)) / (2 * delta)
        wirt = 0.5 * (dRe - 1j * dIm)                                              # dH/dphi (Wirtinger derivative)
        errP = max(errP, abs(wirt - complex(dpi[x][0], dpi[x][1])))
    return errE, errP

def run_ring(label, R, nsteps, ntrials):
    rng = random.Random(5); m2 = R.mk(1); h = R.mk(1) / R.mk(3)
    res = dict(subG=0, subN=0, stepG=0, cont=0, contN=0, full=0, fullN=0, gauge=0, rev=0)
    for trial in range(ntrials):
        st = random_state(R, rng); G0 = gauss(R, st)
        for op in (lambda s: kick(R, s, h, m2), lambda s: drift(R, s, h), lambda s: step(R, s, h, m2)):
            res['subN'] += 1; res['subG'] += gauss(R, op(st)) == G0
        s5 = st
        for _ in range(nsteps): s5 = step(R, s5, h, m2)
        res['stepG'] += gauss(R, s5) == G0
        dE, dpi, J = forces(R, st, m2); k1 = kick(R, st, h, m2)
        for x in SITES:
            rho0 = -R.mk(2) * kmul(st.pi[x], st.phi[x])[1]; rho1 = -R.mk(2) * kmul(k1.pi[x], k1.phi[x])[1]
            divJ = R.zero
            for mu in (0, 1): divJ = divJ + J[(x, mu)] - J[(sh(x, mu, -1), mu)]
            res['contN'] += 1; res['cont'] += (rho1 - rho0 == h * divJ)
        # (b') continuity over a full step with the time-centred current
        h2 = h / R.mk(2); sa = kick(R, st, h2, m2); J0 = forces(R, st, m2)[2]; sb = drift(R, sa, h); J1 = forces(R, sb, m2)[2]; sc = kick(R, sb, h2, m2)
        for x in SITES:
            rho0 = -R.mk(2) * kmul(st.pi[x], st.phi[x])[1]; rho1 = -R.mk(2) * kmul(sc.pi[x], sc.phi[x])[1]
            d0 = d1 = R.zero
            for mu in (0, 1):
                d0 = d0 + J0[(x, mu)] - J0[(sh(x, mu, -1), mu)]; d1 = d1 + J1[(x, mu)] - J1[(sh(x, mu, -1), mu)]
            res['fullN'] += 1; res['full'] += (rho1 - rho0 == h2 * d0 + h2 * d1)
        gam = {x: (None if (R is not RQ and rng.randrange(R.p + 1) == R.p) else (Fr(rng.randint(-4, 4), rng.randint(1, 3)) if R is RQ else R.mk(rng.randrange(R.p)))) for x in SITES}
        res['gauge'] += step(R, gauge_transform(R, st, gam), h, m2).same(R, gauge_transform(R, step(R, st, h, m2), gam))
        res['rev'] += step(R, step(R, st, h, m2), -h, m2).same(R, st)
    print(f"   {label:<5} G conserved by kick/drift/full step: {res['subG']}/{res['subN']};  by {nsteps} full step(s): {res['stepG']}/{ntrials};  "
          f"continuity, kick (per site): {res['cont']}/{res['contN']};  continuity, full step: {res['full']}/{res['fullN']};  gauge covariance of the step: {res['gauge']}/{ntrials};  step(-h) inverts step(h): {res['rev']}/{ntrials}", flush=True)

def main():
    import sys
    which = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else "all"
    if which in ("all", "Q"):
        print("22. minimal coupled model, 2+1 D torus L = 3 (9 sites, 18 links), exact arithmetic, h = 1/3, m^2 = 1", flush=True)
        run_ring("Q", RQ, 1, 2)
    if which in ("all", "F7"): run_ring("F_7", RF(7), 5, 6)
    if which in ("all", "F11"): run_ring("F_11", RF(11), 5, 6)
    if which in ("all", "float"):
        eE, eP = forces_vs_hamiltonian()
        print(f"   (f) floating point: max |dH_pot/d theta - implemented force| = {eE:.2e};  max |dH_pot/d phi (Wirtinger) - implemented force| = {eP:.2e}", flush=True)
    if which in ("all", "inv"):
        print("23. Lorentz-invariant quadratic forms in (E, B)")
        dim, forms = invariants_test()
        print(f"   dimension of the space of real quadratic forms invariant under rotations and boosts of zeta = E + iB: {dim}")
        for f in forms: print("   basis element:", f)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
