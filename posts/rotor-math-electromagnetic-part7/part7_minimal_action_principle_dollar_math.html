Where Part 6 left off

The preceding parts of this series developed parts of an algebraic rotor and lattice-gauge architecture: projective rotor coordinates, rational Cayley maps, exact curvature identities, and selected covariance and conservation checks. They did not establish a single, fully coupled, sourced Maxwell–matter evolution derived from the rotor anchor.

This part makes a narrower contribution. It specifies a conventional compact-\(U(1)\) lattice Hamiltonian coupled to a charged complex scalar and gives a reversible kick–Cayley-drift–kick update. The update has exact algebraic conservation properties that can be checked over \(\mathbb Q\) and finite fields, subject to the stated non-degeneracy assumptions.

A separate calculation at the end examines quadratic Lorentz invariants of a \(3+1\)-dimensional electromagnetic field represented by the rotor anchor. That calculation provides a limited bridge to the usual Maxwell quadratic action. It is not a derivation of the \(2+1\)-dimensional lattice model or its numerical update.

Scope and terminology

Calculation 22 is a \(2+1\)-dimensional model: a two-dimensional spatial lattice with compact \(U(1)\) links and one plaquette variable per square. Calculation 23 is instead a \(3+1\)-dimensional calculation with three-component electric and magnetic fields. They are separate results.

The claims are limited to: - Algebraic identities: Gauss preservation, discrete continuity, gauge covariance, and reversibility of the specified update. - Computational checks: exact-arithmetic tests on sampled configurations over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb F_7\), and \(\mathbb F_{11}\), and floating-point checks of selected derivatives. - Rotor result: classification of quadratic forms invariant under the stated infinitesimal Lorentz transformations, with a conditional argument for the free Maxwell quadratic action. - Not established: derivation of lattice links from the projective rotor anchor, derivation of the update from a discrete action, a complete sourced Maxwell–matter theory, spatial convergence, or a continuum limit.

Lattice variables and Gauss law

Let \(\Lambda\) be a periodic two-dimensional spatial lattice, with site \(x\) and positive coordinate direction \(\mu\in\{1,2\}\). Each oriented edge \(x\to x+\hat\mu\) carries a link \(U_\mu(x)\), and each edge carries an electric variable \(E_\mu(x)\).

For the algebraic implementation, represent a link by a rational Cayley coordinate \[ a_\mu(x)\in\mathbb P^1(K),\qquad U_\mu(x)=\frac{1-i a_\mu(x)}{1+i a_\mu(x)},\qquad i^2=-1. \] The projective value \(a=\infty\) is handled by the projective extension of the map. The coefficient field \(K\) must support the operations used; all denominators must be nonzero. In finite characteristic, the implementation must avoid values where a required denominator vanishes.

The plaquette product is \[ U_P(x)=U_1(x)U_2(x+\hat 1)U_1(x+\hat 2)^{-1}U_2(x)^{-1}. \] For exact arithmetic, write \[ \operatorname{Re}U_P=\frac{U_P+\bar U_P}{2}, \] where \(\bar U\) denotes the chosen involution (and equals \(U^{-1}\) on the unitary slice). Thus the algebraic plaquette energy uses \(1-\operatorname{Re}U_P\), not a primitive cosine operation. This requires \(2\) to be invertible. Similarly, scalar squared norms are written as \(z\bar z\).

At each site, the matter variables are a complex scalar \(\phi(x)\), momentum \(\pi(x)\), and corresponding starred variables \(\phi^*(x),\pi^*(x)\). On the real physical slice, starred variables have their usual complex-conjugate meaning. In an algebraic extension they may be represented as formal variables equipped with a specified involution.

Use canonical brackets \[ \{\theta_\mu(x),E_\nu(y)\}=\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{xy},\quad \{\phi(x),\pi(y)\}=\delta_{xy},\quad \{\phi^*(x),\pi^*(y)\}=\delta_{xy}. \] The angle notation is for the real interpretation \(U=e^{i\theta}\); the rational implementation need not recover angles.

Define \[ \rho(x)=i\bigl(\pi(x)\phi(x)-\pi^*(x)\phi^*(x)\bigr), \] which on the physical conjugate slice is \(\rho=-2\operatorname{Im}(\pi\phi)\). Define \[ (\operatorname{div}E)(x)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{2} \left[E_\mu(x)-E_\mu(x-\hat\mu)\right], \qquad G(x)=(\operatorname{div}E)(x)+\rho(x). \] Gauss’s law is the constraint \(G(x)=0\) at every site.

Continuous-time Hamiltonian and gauge action

The real-variable Hamiltonian is \[ H=H_E+H_B+H_{\rm matter}, \] where \[ H_E=\frac12\sum_{x,\mu}E_\mu(x)^2,\qquad H_B=\sum_x(1-\cos\theta_P(x)), \] and \[ \begin{aligned} H_{\rm matter}={}&\sum_x\pi(x)\pi^*(x)\\ &+\sum_{x,\mu}|U_\mu(x)\phi(x+\hat\mu)-\phi(x)|^2 +m^2\sum_x|\phi(x)|^2. \end{aligned} \] For exact algebraic arithmetic, use \(H_B=\sum_x(1-\operatorname{Re}U_P(x))\) and write each norm as \(z\bar z\). The cosine form is only the familiar real-angle interpretation, not a finite-field operation.

A continuous-time first-order canonical action is \[ S=\int dt\left[ \sum_{x,\mu}E_\mu(x)\dot\theta_\mu(x) +\sum_x\bigl(\pi\dot\phi+\pi^*\dot\phi^*\bigr) -H-\sum_x A_0(x)G(x)\right]. \] Here \(A_0(x)\) imposes Gauss’s law. This action specifies the continuous-time Hamiltonian system in the real canonical interpretation. The discrete update in Section 4 is not obtained by varying a discrete action.

With a site-valued gauge transformation \(g(x)\), use \[ \begin{aligned} \phi(x)&\mapsto g(x)\phi(x),& \pi(x)&\mapsto g(x)^{-1}\pi(x),\\ U_\mu(x)&\mapsto g(x)U_\mu(x)g(x+\hat\mu)^{-1},& E_\mu(x)&\mapsto E_\mu(x). \end{aligned} \] The starred variables transform in the conjugate representation. The hopping term and plaquette energy are gauge invariant.

In the real-angle interpretation, the oriented hopping current is \[ J_\mu(x)= \frac{\partial H_{\rm hop}}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)} =2\operatorname{Im}\!\left( U_\mu(x)\phi(x+\hat\mu)\phi(x)^* \right), \] with the link orientation and sign convention fixed by these definitions. The electric equation contains the negative of the plaquette force and this current: \[ \dot E_\mu(x)=-\frac{\partial H_B}{\partial\theta_\mu(x)}-J_\mu(x). \] In the algebraic implementation the force is evaluated from rational link expressions; angle extraction is unnecessary.

The kick–Cayley-drift–kick update

Define the symmetric composition \[ \Phi_h=K_{h/2}\,D_h\,K_{h/2}, \] where the rightmost map is applied first. \(K\) is the force kick and \(D\) is the drift. Although this resembles kick–drift–kick splitting, the link drift is a rational Cayley rotation rather than the exact flow of the displayed electric energy \(E^2/2\). Therefore \(\Phi_h\) is not an exact Strang splitting of the Hamiltonian in Section 3.

Kick

During a kick of size \(s\), the configuration variables are fixed and the momenta are updated by the force terms. In the reference implementation’s convention, \[ E\mapsto E-s\,dE,\qquad \pi\mapsto\pi-s\,d\pi, \] with the corresponding update for \(\pi^*\). Here \(dE\) contains the plaquette force and hopping current, and \(d\pi,d\pi^*\) are the matter gradients of the hopping and mass terms. These signs must be kept consistent with the current, divergence, and Gauss-law conventions used by the implementation.

Drift

The matter drift is \[ \phi\mapsto\phi+h\pi^*,\qquad \phi^*\mapsto\phi^*+h\pi, \] with momenta held fixed. The link drift is \[ U_\mu(x)\mapsto U_\mu(x) \frac{1+i hE_\mu(x)/2}{1-i hE_\mu(x)/2}. \] This is rational and reversible wherever the denominator is nonzero; changing \(h\) to \(-h\) inverts it.

For real \(E,h\), and \(U=e^{i\theta}\), the same map has the real-angle interpretation \[ \theta\mapsto\theta+2\arctan(hE/2). \] The implementation does not compute \(\arctan\). In the real-variable interpretation only, the link drift can be regarded as the exact flow of a modified kinetic function \(T_h\) satisfying \[ T_h'(E)=\frac{2}{h}\arctan(hE/2),\qquad T_h(0)=0, \] so that \[ T_h(E)=\frac{E^2}{2}-\frac{h^2E^4}{48}+O(h^4). \] This expansion is a real-variable asymptotic statement, not an algebraic identity over \(\mathbb Q\) or a finite field.

Calculation 22: properties of the coupled update

The reference implementation is named minimal_action_model.py. It defines rational link operations, matter forces, kick, drift, full step, Gauss function, gauge transformations, and the invariant calculation. For reproducibility, distribute the exact tested script alongside the article or in a versioned repository, and record its revision or checksum. Test counts alone are not a substitute for the source.

Proposition 22.1 — Gauss preservation

Claim. The specified kick–drift–kick update preserves \(G(x)\) at each site, provided all operations are defined and the assumptions used in the algebraic identities hold.

Proof sketch. During a kick, the electric field and matter momentum change together. Substitution of the kicked momenta into \[ \rho(x)=i\bigl(\pi(x)\phi(x)-\pi^*(x)\phi^*(x)\bigr) \] gives a change that is linear in the momentum increments, since the fields \(\phi,\phi^*\) are held fixed. Inserting the hopping-gradient expressions groups edge contributions into an oriented lattice divergence. The resulting continuity identity cancels the corresponding change in \(\operatorname{div}E\), so \(\Delta G(x)=0\). During the drift, \(E\) is fixed and the specified matter drift leaves the charge density used in \(G\) unchanged. Thus each substep and their composition preserve \(G\). The signs in the continuity equation must be read together with the chosen current and divergence conventions. \(\square\)

Proposition 22.2 — Kick continuity

Claim. For a kick of size \(s\), the change of charge is the discrete divergence of the oriented hopping current, with the sign convention fixed by the implementation: \[ \Delta\rho(x)=s\,(\operatorname{div}J)(x). \]

Proof sketch. Substitute the momentum increments into the definition of \(\rho\). The result is linear in those increments. Terms belonging to an edge appear with opposite signs at its two endpoints; grouping them by site gives the oriented divergence. The mass term cancels from the charge difference. This is an algebraic rearrangement, not a continuum approximation. \(\square\)

Proposition 22.3 — Full-step continuity

Claim. The full step satisfies a time-centred continuity relation formed from the currents evaluated at the two kick configurations.

Proof sketch. Apply the kick continuity identity to the first and second half-kicks. The drift moves the configuration between the current evaluations. Adding the two identities expresses the full-step charge change as the divergence of the appropriately weighted sum of those two currents. The implementation’s time levels and weights are part of the statement; a current evaluated at an unrelated time level cannot be substituted. \(\square\)

Proposition 22.4 — Gauge covariance

Claim. The update commutes with the sitewise gauge action wherever the operations are defined.

Proof sketch. Plaquette products are gauge invariant and hopping terms transform according to their site and edge representations. Their gradients therefore transform in the same representations as the variables updated by the forces. The Cayley link drift multiplies \(U_\mu(x)\) by a rational function of the gauge-invariant \(E_\mu(x)\), preserving the link transformation law. Each substep, hence the composition, is gauge covariant. \(\square\)

Proposition 22.5 — Reversibility

Claim. The map satisfies \[ \Phi_{-h}\circ\Phi_h=\operatorname{id} \] provided all intermediate denominators are nonzero and the maps are implemented consistently.

Proof sketch. A kick is inverted by the opposite kick because the configuration is fixed during it. The Cayley drift is inverted by replacing \(h\) with \(-h\). The symmetric composition reverses the order of the substeps, giving the inverse map. \(\square\)

These statements concern this particular update and its conventions; they are not universal claims about every rational lattice scheme.

Exact-arithmetic checks and numerical evidence

The accompanying script reports tests on a \(3\times3\) periodic lattice over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb F_7\), and \(\mathbb F_{11}\). The finite-field cases use an algebraic complex extension and require every denominator to be invertible.

The rational tests use fewer repeated steps because exact rational numerators and denominators can grow quickly. These counts are the cases reported by the script, not an exhaustive enumeration of states. The general claims rely on the algebraic arguments above, subject to their assumptions.

The script also compares analytic force expressions with numerical gradients. For the tested configuration, the reported maximum discrepancies are approximately \(4.2\times10^{-9}\) for the link-angle derivative and \(3.4\times10^{-9}\) for the Wirtinger matter derivative. These are floating-point checks, not exact proofs.

These tests support the implementation on the sampled states. They do not establish spatial convergence, a continuum limit, correct wave speed, or agreement with an independently specified Maxwell–matter solver. They also do not show that the links or update have been derived from the rotor anchor.

Calculation 23: quadratic Lorentz invariants of the rotor field

This calculation is separate from the \(2+1\)-dimensional lattice model. In \(3+1\) dimensions, write the rotor anchor as \[ \zeta=\mathbf E+i\mathbf B, \] where \(\mathbf E,\mathbf B\) are real three-vectors. With the conventions used here, the infinitesimal transformations are \[ \delta\mathbf E=\mathbf r\times\mathbf E-\mathbf b\times\mathbf B,\qquad \delta\mathbf B=\mathbf r\times\mathbf B+\mathbf b\times\mathbf E, \] for infinitesimal rotation and boost parameters \(\mathbf r,\mathbf b\).

Requiring a real quadratic form in \(\mathbf E,\mathbf B\) to be invariant under these transformations gives a two-dimensional space of quadratic invariants. A basis is \[ I_1=\mathbf B^2-\mathbf E^2 =-\operatorname{Re}(\zeta\cdot\zeta), \qquad I_2=\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B =\frac12\operatorname{Im}(\zeta\cdot\zeta). \] Signs and normalization depend on conventions; the displayed equations define the conventions here. The symbolic calculation in the source checks the invariant subspace for the specified infinitesimal action.

The first invariant is the familiar Maxwell quadratic density up to sign and normalization. The second is proportional to the pseudoscalar \(F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}\), with convention-dependent factors. If the field is represented by a potential and suitable boundary conditions are imposed, the spacetime integral of the pseudoscalar term is a boundary contribution. Under these assumptions, the usual free Maxwell quadratic action is selected up to normalization and addition of that boundary term.

This is a conditional field-theoretic argument. It does not derive the lattice Hamiltonian, rational link representation, scalar matter action, kick–Cayley-drift–kick update, a discrete variational principle, a sourced Maxwell equation, or a continuum limit. The rotor calculation motivates the Maxwell-type quadratic action at the continuum level; it does not establish a rotor-derived lattice regularization.

Four levels of claim

  1. Model definition. The Hamiltonian, gauge action, and update are specified choices. Their definition does not show that they follow from the rotor architecture.
  2. Algebraic identity. Gauss preservation, continuity, gauge covariance, and reversibility concern the specified maps under stated assumptions.
  3. Computational evidence. Exact-arithmetic tests cover selected configurations and fields. Floating-point derivative checks assess selected numerical calculations. Neither replaces a proof of a general identity.
  4. Rotor-derived result. Calculation 23 classifies quadratic invariants for a specified \(3+1\)-dimensional infinitesimal Lorentz action and gives a conditional connection to the free Maxwell action. It does not provide the missing lattice or matter derivation.

A conventional gauge-invariant lattice Hamiltonian can be a useful testbed for the rational rotor program without being a consequence of the rotor anchor.

Limitations and next tests

The following tasks remain open:

  • Discrete variational formulation: find, or prove an obstruction to, a discrete action whose equations yield the intended coupled update while retaining the rational link law.
  • Matched matter and gauge dynamics: specify temporal links, charge/current staggering, and sourced Gauss law in one consistent real-time formulation.
  • Spatial convergence and dispersion: compare with manufactured solutions and measure dispersion only after source terms, staggering, and scaling are consistently specified.
  • Continuum comparison: compare a controlled weak-field regime with an independently defined Maxwell–matter discretization. Agreement would be a consistency check, not proof of rotor derivation.
  • Rotor-to-link construction: give an explicit map from projective \(2\times2\) rotor data to lattice links and establish its covariance and curvature properties.
  • Matter extensions: couple fermionic matter and a Wilson–Dirac sector consistently to the same Gauss constraint and current.
  • Additional symmetry tests: conformal symmetry remains a proposed investigation, not a result of this part.

Part 8 examines numerical consistency and temporal convergence for a specified coupled scalar–gauge scheme. Those results must be interpreted within their own assumptions and do not establish the unresolved items above.

Reproducibility notes

The exact-arithmetic claims refer to the Python reference implementation minimal_action_model.py. For a reproducible release:

  1. distribute the tested script alongside this article or in a versioned public repository;
  2. record the revision or checksum of the exact file used to produce the reported output;
  3. specify the Python and dependency versions;
  4. preserve the printed test output, including any failures;
  5. rerun the full suite after changing force signs, link conventions, or projective exceptional cases.

The script is the executable specification of implementation-level conventions, not a substitute for the proofs or their assumptions.

Conclusion

This part specifies a lattice Hamiltonian model and a rational kick–Cayley-drift–kick update with algebraic checks for Gauss preservation, continuity, gauge covariance, and reversibility. The exact-arithmetic implementation does not evaluate trigonometric or inverse-trigonometric functions; real-angle expressions are used only to explain the compact-\(U(1)\) interpretation.

Separately, the \(3+1\)-dimensional rotor-anchor calculation identifies two quadratic Lorentz invariants and motivates the usual free Maxwell quadratic action under stated boundary assumptions. It does not derive the lattice model or update from the rotor architecture. The next task is to construct and test the missing bridge between rotor data, lattice connection, and a variationally consistent coupled evolution.
